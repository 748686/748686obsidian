## Event Overview
In June and July 2026, the Law Enforcement Inspection Group of the Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (NPC) conducted inspections on the implementation of the *Law of the People's Republic of China on the Construction of a Barrier-Free Environment* across six provinces and municipalities: Beijing, Inner Mongolia, Shanghai, Hunan, Chongqing, and Gansu.

The inspection evaluated progress made over the three years since the law’s enactment, focusing on physical facility construction, information accessibility, and social services. While acknowledging significant improvements in living quality and social participation for elderly and disabled persons, the inspection group identified persistent issues regarding non-standard facilities, "digital divides," and inadequate enforcement of mandatory standards.

## Core Facts
*   **Inspection Body:** Law Enforcement Inspection Group of the NPC Standing Committee.
*   **Timeframe:** June–July 2026.
*   **Locations Inspected:** Beijing, Inner Mongolia, Shanghai, Hunan, Chongqing, and Gansu.
*   **Subject Law:** *Law of the People's Republic of China on the Construction of a Barrier-Free Environment*.
*   **General Assessment:** The inspection group affirmed that local governments and departments have actively responded to public concerns and promoted barrier-free construction, providing legal保障 (guarantees) for improving people's lives and high-quality economic development.
*   **Key Findings (Physical Facilities):**
    *   New construction projects have largely solved the issue of whether barrier-free facilities exist ("有无"问题).
    *   Significant progress has been made in transforming old residential communities and improving transportation accessibility.
    *   **Deficiencies:** Existing facilities are often "present but difficult to use" (有而难用). Specific issues include wheelchair ramps with excessive slopes, unreasonably installed handrails, and blind tracks that lack color contrast, use inappropriate materials, or are paved incorrectly despite high coverage rates.
*   **Key Findings (Information Accessibility):**
    *   Progress has been made in extending information accessibility to multiple scenarios.
    *   **Deficiencies:** Some websites and applications feature "elderly modes" containing advertisements and misleading buttons. The "digital divide" remains particularly pronounced in rural areas.
*   **Key Findings (Social Services):**
    *   A complete travel support system combining "hard measures" and "soft services" has basically taken shape.
    *   Over 220 cities have launched the 95128 ride-hailing service phone.
    *   All urban rail transit lines have opened "love reservation" services.
    *   Major ride-hailing platforms have launched "one-click ride-hailing" services in over 320 cities.

## Event Conclusion
The NPC Standing Committee's law enforcement inspection conducted in mid-2026 confirmed that the *Law on the Construction of a Barrier-Free Environment* has effectively promoted physical and information accessibility across China, with notable advancements in elevator installations, digital services, and judicial protections. However, the inspection revealed critical gaps in the quality and usability of existing facilities, particularly regarding construction standards and the digital divide in rural areas. The findings underscore the need for stricter enforcement of mandatory standards and further standardization of information accessibility to transition from "having facilities" to "having usable facilities."
