## Event Overview

On October 14, 2026, *People's Daily* published a special report and accompanying editorial commentary regarding the deepening of East-West cooperation between Shandong Province ("Lu") and Chongqing Municipality ("Yu") in agricultural technology. The coverage highlights a shift from simple technology transfer to a bidirectional model of co-creation, where Shandong’s advanced cultivation techniques adapt to Chongqing’s specific conditions, and Chongqing’s ecological advantages provide new growth spaces for Shandong’s agricultural industry.

## Core Facts

1.  **Event Date:** October 14, 2026 (Publication date in *People's Daily*).
2.  **Geographic Scope:** Shandong Province and Chongqing Municipality, specifically focusing on Shougang/Kaizhou (Chongqing) and Fengdu (Chongqing).
3.  **Nature of Cooperation:** The collaboration has evolved from "teaching technology" to "forming teams" and establishing industrial parks and R&D platforms.
4.  **Technological Adaptation in Kaizhou:**
    *   Agricultural technology from Shouguang (Shandong) was introduced to Kaizhou, Chongqing.
    *   Local farmers in Kaizhou, who traditionally relied on experience, collaborated with Shandong technicians to develop new standards suited to local climates and soils.
    *   The dynamic shifted from farmers "following along" to "proactive execution."
5.  **Bidirectional Flow in Fengdu:**
    *   Fengdu leverages its high-altitude and cool climate conditions to cultivate strawberry seedlings.
    *   These seedlings are sold back to main production areas in Shandong, reversing the traditional flow of technology support.
6.  **Conceptual Framework:** The cooperation is characterized as "inputting technology and creating technology together," moving beyond simple adoption ("East technology to West") to recombination and mutual improvement based on complementary strengths.

## Event Conclusion

The Lu-Yu (Shandong-Chongqing) collaboration represents a maturation of China’s East-West cooperation model in agriculture. By moving beyond unidirectional aid to a reciprocal relationship based on comparative advantage—Shandong providing technical expertise and Chongqing providing specialized ecological conditions for seed propagation—the partnership has generated tangible operational changes. Farmers in Kaizhou have adopted new standards through co-creation, and producers in Fengdu have integrated into Shandong’s supply chain. This case illustrates a shift from simple technology transfer to a collaborative innovation ecosystem supported by institutional mechanisms like industrial parks and R&D platforms.
