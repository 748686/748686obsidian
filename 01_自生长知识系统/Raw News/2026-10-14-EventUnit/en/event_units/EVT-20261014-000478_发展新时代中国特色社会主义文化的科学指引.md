## Event Overview

On October 14, 2026, *People's Daily* (Renmin Ribao) published a special report and editorial note on Page 11 (Culture Edition) regarding the integration of Artificial Intelligence (AI) into education. The primary article, titled *"Facing AI, How to Become a Person Who Knows How to Ask Questions"* (part of a series "Four Questions on 'AI + Education': Ensuring High-Quality Balanced Development of Education"), explores the shift from answer-seeking to question-formulating as a critical competency in the AI era. An accompanying editorial note, *"Questioning is the Starting Point of Thinking,"* reinforces the pedagogical philosophy underpinning the main report.

The event centers on the consensus among educators, researchers, industry developers, and students that while AI can generate answers instantly, the human capacity to define problems, judge quality, and ask high-quality questions has become the defining metric of educational success and professional competitiveness.

## Core Facts

**1. The Shift in Educational Competency**
*   Education in the AI era is transitioning from assessing "ability to calculate/answer" to assessing "ability to judge and question" (Source: #565).
*   "Asking" is now considered more valuable than "answering" because AI can provide complete solutions rapidly (Source: #565, #566).
*   Asking questions is defined as a high-order ability based on understanding, not随意 (random/casual) inquiry (Source: #565).

**2. Pedagogical Innovations and Case Studies**
*   **Fudan University Case:** In a "Data Mining Technology" course exam, students were tasked with designing 10 questions each to "stump" three AI large models.
    *   *Result:* 50 students made at least one AI model answer incorrectly; 4 students achieved scores of 0 for some AI models (meaning the AIs failed all their questions).
    *   *Implication:* Traditional exams with standard answers are partially失效 (ineffective) because AI answers faster and more accurately than students (Source: #565).
*   **Professor Xiao Yanghua’s Approach:** Students must first master the solution and derivation before designing questions that test AI’s structural weaknesses. Good questions require clear constraints, complete reasoning chains, and targeted probing of AI flaws (Source: #565).

**3. Risks and Cognitive Implications**
*   **"Cognitive Debt":** Cao Peijie warns that outsourcing thinking to AI leads to "cognitive debt," where the brain’s startup speed slows down, similar to a computer that hasn’t been used for a long time (Source: #565).
*   **Amplifier Effect:** AI acts as an amplifier. For students with solid foundations and judgment, it enhances learning; for those with weak foundations who only copy-paste, it erodes thinking capabilities (Source: #565).
*   **Loss of Independent Thought:** Directly copying AI answers without understanding the derivation process weakens independent thinking over time (Source: #565).

**4. Professional Competitiveness**
*   "Proficient in using AI" is becoming a basic entry requirement (likened to "proficient in office software" 10 years ago) rather than a competitive advantage (Source: #565).
*   True competitiveness lies in defining problems, breaking them down, judging results, and solving complex practical problems using AI as a partner (Source: #565).
*   The ideal profile is a "composite talent" possessing both professional domain expertise and AI application skills (Source: #565).

**5. Disciplinary Integration**
*   AI lacks "human touch" and deep understanding of human emotion and social context. Therefore, humanities insights are necessary to train AI to be more aligned with human values (Source: #565).
*   The future demands "文理兼备" (integration of arts and sciences): combining technical/logical thinking with humanistic insight and emotional literacy (Source: #565).

## Event Conclusion

The synthesis of Articles #565 and #566 establishes that as of October 14, 2026, the dominant framework for AI integration in Chinese education prioritizes **higher-order cognitive skills**—specifically questioning, critical judgment, and problem definition—over rote knowledge retrieval. The evidence indicates a consensus among academic and industry leaders that AI serves as a tool that amplifies existing capability gaps: it rewards foundational knowledge and critical thinking while penalizing passive consumption. The educational response is shifting from teaching students how to answer to teaching them how to interrogate, with interdisciplinary literacy (combining STEM logic and Humanities empathy) identified as the new benchmark for future talent.
