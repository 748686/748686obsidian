## Core Facts

**1. The Centennial of Xiyin Site Excavation**
*   **Historical Milestone:** Exactly 100 years prior to the 2026 congress, Li Ji (Li Chi) conducted the first scientific archaeological excavation in China at Xiyin Village, Xia County, Yuncheng, Shanxi. This event marks the beginning of modern Chinese archaeology.
*   **Methodological Innovations:** The 1926 excavation established four "firsts" for Chinese archaeology:
    *   First use of three-point recording, stratigraphic layering, and trench/pit methods for 3D coordinate recording of artifacts.
    *   Publication of *Prehistoric Remains of Xiyin Village* (1927), the first archaeological excavation report in China, establishing the standard format for field maps, profiles, artifact line drawings, and statistical tables.
    *   First application of typology to domestic archaeological data (Liang Siyong’s 1930 master’s thesis on pottery).
    *   First archaeological excavation management system (*Management Measures for Excavating Historical Relics in Shanxi Province*), drafted by Li Ji and the Freer Gallery of Art.
*   **Key Findings:**
    *   **Silkworm Cocoon:** A half-carbonized silkworm cocoon with a straight cut was found, pushing back the history of sericulture in China to approximately 6,000 years ago. This challenged the "Chinese Culture Western Origin Theory" proposed by Swedish scholar Johan Gunnar Andersson.
    *   **Xiyin Pattern:** Painted pottery with dots, arcs, and triangles, named "Xiyin Pattern" by Li Ji, demonstrated an indigenous cultural system.
    *   **1994 Excavation:** Clarified cultural sequences including Miaodigou Phase II and Erligang cultures, formally naming the "Xiyin Culture."
    *   **2025-2026 Excavation (3rd):** Conducted by the Shanxi Institute of Cultural Relics and Archaeology. Findings include the distribution range, the dual moat outline of the Yangshao period settlement, plant remains (millet, broomcorn millet), animal bones (pig, dog, deer), confirmation that pottery pigments were ochre (via Raman spectroscopy), and evidence of fermentation technology (red yeast mold and yeast in small-mouthed pointed bottles), indicating knowledge of bothQu-Jiu (distilled liquor base) and Guya-Jiu (sprouted grain liquor) over 5,000 years ago.
*   **Geographic Context:** The site is located in Xia County (ancient Anyi), near the Yuncheng Salt Lake and Zhongtiao Mountain copper mines. These resources (salt for survival/population aggregation, copper for state formation) are identified as key factors in the prosperity of Yangshao-era large sites in southern Shanxi.
*   **Museum:** The Xiyin Archaeology Museum, shaped like a silkworm cocoon, has completed exhibition setup and is preparing for public opening.

**2. Musical Fusion in the Sixteen Kingdoms and Northern Dynasties**
*   **Context:** In the Guanzhong region (centered on Chang'an/Xi'an), a unique musical style emerged blending Central Plains ritual music traditions with Western Regions (Hu) instruments, described as "entering elegance with Hu music, Han rhymes with Hu style."
*   **Archaeological Data:** Over 400 music figurines (Jile Yong) have been discovered in dozens of tombs from this period, concentrated in the Xianyang Plain, Fengqi Plain, Shaoling Plain, and Gaoyang Plain. Types include playing musicians, military drum-and-blow ensembles (Guchui), and pose figurines.
*   **Early Period (Former Qin):** Tomb M63 at Bili Village, Xianyang, yielded 31 figurines. The ensemble featured wind/woodwind instruments (panpipes, sheng, flute), string instruments (pipa, se, zheng, zhu), and percussion (drums). Notably, ritual bells and chimes (Zhong/Qing) from the Shang/Zhou/Han periods had disappeared, replaced by silk and bamboo strings, maintaining the Han-Wei Qingyue (Clear Music) tradition.
*   **Later Period (Post-Qin to Northern Wei):** A shift occurred where Guchui (military drum and horn) ensembles replaced chamber music (Jile). Tomb M1 at Caochangpo, Liang Meng’s tomb at Hongqingyuan, and Tomb M3 at Dengjiazhongcun (Xi'an) feature Guchui figurines predominantly.
*   **Causal Analysis:** The decline of Central Plains court music is attributed to Liu Yu’s invasion at the end of the Later Qin, during which musicians were taken south, weakening Guanzhong’s court music. Subsequently, under Northern Wei rule, Xianbei rulers preferred their native military music ("playing local tunes"), leading to the rise of Guchui.
*   **Instrument Transmission:**
    *   **Horn (Jue):** Likely introduced via Gaochang (highways to Western Regions) during wars/migrations. Recorded in Song dynasty *Yushu* as a Gaochang instrument.
    *   **Banggu:** Found in Tomb M3 (Xi'an), similar to those in Gansu Gaotai Di Gengpo Wei-Jin tomb murals, indicating interaction between Guanzhong and Hexi corridors.
    *   **Vertical Konghou:** Discovered in Tomb M2 at Po Liu Village, Xianyang. This is the only known example from the Sixteen Kingdoms period in Guanzhong. Its introduction is linked to Lu Guang’s western expedition against Kucha, as recorded in *Taiping Yulan*.
