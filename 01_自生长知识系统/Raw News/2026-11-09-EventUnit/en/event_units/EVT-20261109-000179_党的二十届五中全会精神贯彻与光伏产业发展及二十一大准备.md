## Core Facts

### 1. The 21st National Congress Date and Electoral Procedures
The 5th Plenum of the 20th CPC Central Committee decided that the 21st National Congress of the Communist Party of China will be held in the **second half of 2027** in Beijing. The Central Committee subsequently issued the "Notice on the Election of Delegates to the 21st National Congress of the Communist Party of China." A Central Organization Department official, interviewed by Xinhua News Agency, detailed the electoral framework.

The election is divided into **38 electoral districts**, consistent with the 20th National Congress. The total number of delegates is set at **2,360**, an increase of 60 from the 20th National Congress. The allocation is primarily based on the number of party organizations and members, with considerations for previous congresses. A portion of delegates already retired from leadership positions will be invited as special delegates.

The process follows five main steps:
1.  **Recommendation and Nomination:** Grassroots level, based on democratic centralism.
2.  **Organizational Investigation:** Comprehensive scrutiny of political integrity, cleanliness, and conduct.
3.  **Determination of Initial Candidates:** Public notification and consultation within electoral districts.
4.  **Determination of Reserve Candidates:** Voted on by party committee plenums.
5.  **Election:** Conducted via party congress or representative meeting, with a differential election ratio of more than 15%.

Key structural requirements include ensuring that production and work frontline party members constitute no less than one-third of delegates from provincial-level, financial, and state-owned enterprise systems, and no less than 15% from central and state organs. Female and ethnic minority representation must also reflect their proportion within the party membership. Political standards are paramount, with strict vetting against "sick" candidates (those with disciplinary or legal issues).

### 2. Local Policy Implementation: Yunnan's "Dual Handover" System
In the context of implementing the spirit of the 5th Plenum, which called for rectifying issues such as "new officials not attending to old accounts" and "blind decision-making," **Pu'er City, Yunnan Province**, implemented a "dual handover" system for city-managed cadres. This requires both outgoing and incoming officials to complete a written handover checklist under organizational supervision, ensuring seamless continuity.

Between May and October 2026, this system facilitated the resolution of the "Nanping Road" project in Simao District—a three-year stalled "dead-end road" affecting over 20,000 residents. As of the report date, Pu'er City had completed job handovers for **70 principal officials and 439 deputy officials**, involving over **5,000 items**. Yunnan Province formally issued its own "Measures for the Handover of Changes in Principal Leaders of Cities and Counties" in July 2026, detailing a "one book, one chart, and seven lists" framework covering important work, economic development, livelihood security, reform exploration, risk hazards, key rectifications, and party building.

### 3. Grain Procurement Statistics
According to the National Food and Strategic Reserves Administration, as of November 8, 2026, China had purchased over **100 million tons** of autumn grain, with progress largely in line with the previous year. Six provinces, including Hunan and Hubei, activated the minimum purchase price execution plan for late rice, acquiring nearly 1 million tons. Officials projected that during the "15th Five-Year Plan" period, annual grain purchases would remain above 400 million tons, maintaining a market-oriented approach combined with farmer interest protection.
