## Core Facts

### 1. Jiajia Site Archaeology (Page 08, "Approaching the Earliest Prehistoric City Site in the Lower Reaches of the Yellow River")
*   **Location:** Jiajia Site, Zhangqiu District, Jinan City, Shandong Province.
*   **Date:** Major cultural deposits date to the Mid-to-Late Dawenkou Culture period, approximately 5,200 to 4,400 years ago.
*   **Significance:** It is the earliest known prehistoric city site in the lower reaches of the Yellow River. It is a contemporary representative of the "Liangzhu Ancient City" in the Yangtze River basin. In 2024, the "Jiajia Ancient City" was incorporated into the People's Education Press national unified seventh-grade history textbook (Volume 1).
*   **Discovery Timeline:**
    *   Discovered during archaeological surveys in 1987.
    *   Reviewed and test-dug in the 1990s.
    *   Systematic archaeological work began in 2016, led by the School of Archaeology and Museology at Shandong University in collaboration with other institutions.
*   **Key Findings (10-Year Survey):**
    *   **City Structure:** The site features a "three-ring wall and moat" system.
        *   *Small City:* ~122,500 square meters, dating to the Mid-Dawenkou period. Built with bundled-stick rammed earth walls.
        *   *Middle City:* Second moat discovered in 2023–2024, covering ~495,000 square meters, dating to the Late Dawenkou period.
        *   *Outer City:* Clues for a third ring of walls and moats discovered in 2025.
    *   **Social Stratification:** Burials show clear hierarchy. Over 70% of tombs used coffins. Large tombs contained multiple coffin layers (triple coffins confirmed in Late Dawenkou), jade axes, and refined pottery.
    *   **Diet and Labor:** Staples were millet and broomcorn millet; meat consisted mainly of domesticated pigs, deer, and freshwater fish. Isotope analysis shows dietary differences between elites and commoners. Physical anthropology indicates robust builds (males avg. >1.7m, females avg. >1.6m) and clear labor division, with even high-status individuals showing skeletal signs of manual labor.
    *   **Resource Networks:** Jade analysis reveals specialized production. Serpentine sourced from the Taihang Mountains; nephrite possibly from Northeast China; turquoise possibly from Northwest Hubei. This indicates a long-distance resource circulation network existing ~5,000 years ago.
    *   **Cultural Exchange:** Artifacts show influences from the Central Plains (Yangshao/Miaodigou II), Lower Yangtze (Liangzhu), and Middle Yangtze (Qujialing) cultures, indicating a "super-regional cultural community" among elites.
*   **Academic Status:** Selected as a core site for Phase V of the "Probing the Origins of Chinese Civilization Project" and a major project of "Archaeology China." Rated as one of the "Top Ten Archaeological Discoveries in China for 2017."
*   **Public Engagement:** Designated as the first "Shandong Province Public Archaeology Base," hosting tens of thousands of school students. Exhibitions have been held at the National Museum of China ("Rites from the East") and the Liangzhu Art Museum ("Eastern Five Thousand Years").

### 2. Min River Olive Planting System (Page 08, "Min River Olives, Fuzhou's 'Blessed Fruit'")
*   **Designation:** In October 2026, the Fuzhou olive planting system was designated as the **eighth batch of China Important Agricultural Cultural Heritage**.
*   **Historical Context:** Historical records in the *New Book of Tang* (*Xin Tang Shu*) list olives as a tribute item from Fuzhou (Changle Commandery) to the imperial court, indicating cultivation history dating back to the Tang Dynasty.
*   **Geographical Characteristics:** Olives are grown on sandy islets along the Min River and on adjacent gentle slopes. Farmers adjust planting locations based on water levels and sedimentation changes in the river islets.
*   **Ecological Practices:**
    *   Planting on islets prevents soil erosion and utilizes fertile soil/water sources.
    *   Intercropping: Crops are grown under trees, and poultry are raised, with manure returned to the orchard, creating an ecological and economic win-win.
*   **Techniques:**
    *   *High-position grafting:* Used to rejuvenate old trees without replanting, following the local maxim "three parts grafting, seven parts nurturing."
    *   *Varietal Preservation:* Native varieties such as Hu Yuan, Chang Ying, and Tan Xiang are preserved for fresh consumption or processing.
*   **Processing and Culture:**
    *   Traditional processing includes salting, soaking, sugar boiling, and pickling (e.g., "Kao Bian Lan" / flattened olives).
    *   Minhou olive preserve making and Minqing olive dew手工制作 are listed in the Fuzhou Intangible Cultural Heritage representative projects.
    *   Cultural symbolism: Olives are known as "Jian Guo" (Memorial Fruit) in classical literature (南宋 *Qi Dong Ye Yu*) due to their bitter-then-sweet taste, symbolizing honest advice. In Fuzhou, they are called "Fu Guo" (Blessed Fruit), used in gifting during festivals.

### 3. Red Culture and Revolutionary History (Page 08)
*   **The Eternal "Huaxi Company" (Jiangxi):**
    *   **Location:** Huaxi Village, Xiangxing Township, Yongxin County, Jiangxi Province. Site: Liu Family Ancestral Hall (Headquarters of the 17th Division, Red Sixth Army).
    *   **History:** In early 1934, Xiao Ke's Red Sixth Army 17th Division rested in Huaxi. During the "expanding red" (recruitment) movement, over 120 young men from the village enlisted. The "Huaxi Company" was formally established in April 1934 under the 49th Regiment, 17th Division.
    *   **Casualties:** The company participated in the Long March (crossing snow mountains and grasslands). By the end of the Long March and subsequent Anti-Japanese War service (integrated into the 8th Route Army 359 Brigade), over 200 soldiers from the original 280+ members had died. Only over 10 survivors remained by the founding of the PRC.
    *   **Legacy:** General Xiao Ke revisited the site in 1954 and expressed regret for not bringing the soldiers back. He inscribed: "The Huaxi Company is the pride of the Huaxi people... inherit and carry forward the glorious traditions." The site is now a county-level cultural relic protection unit and a base for patriotic education.
*   **Female Red Army Legacy (Guizhou):**
    *   **Site:** Tucheng Female Red Army Memorial Hall, Xishui County, Guizhou Province.
    *   **Key Figure:** Wang Dingguo (1913–2020), a female Red Army soldier who participated in the Long March with the Fourth Front Army. She lost a toe to frostbite while crossing snow mountains but continued marching with nine toes.
    *   **Artifact:** In 2017, at age 105, Wang Dingguo wrote the inscription "Tucheng Female Red Army Memorial Hall." In September 2019, she pressed a red handprint, which was donated by her son Xie Yaxu. This serves as a spiritual relic.
    *   **Memorial Data:** The hall has recorded the names of 4,113 female Red Army soldiers and focuses on the stories of 731 of them. It runs annual "Female Red Army Story Telling Sessions" and youth研学 (study tour) programs.

### 4. Craftsmanship and Accountability (Page 08, "Names on Artifacts Still 'Speaking'")
*   **Theme:** Analysis of how division of labor and responsibility were recorded in Chinese gold, silver, and bronze ware from Han to Qing dynasties.
*   **Han Dynasty:**
    *   *Institutional Collaboration:* Western Han gold-gilt silver-plated copper bamboo joint incense burner shows two official bureaus ("Neiguan" and "Sigong") collaborating, with separate inscriptions on lid and base.
    *   *Craft分工:* Eastern Han silver-gilt wine vessel lists four specific craft workers by name (copper worker, silver-gilt worker, pattern worker, maker) on the base.
*   **Tang Dynasty:**
    *   *Hierarchical Recording:* Metalwork from Famen Temple (e.g., gilded flying fairy crane silver tea sieve) lists responsibilities by rank: craftsman (Shao Yuan), reviewing official (Li Shicun), judge/supervisor (Wu Hongque), and envoy (Nengshun).
    *   *Flexibility:* Other items show collaboration between temples and individual craftsmen, moving beyond strict state作坊 hierarchy.
*   **Qing Dynasty:**
    *   *Quantification:* Shift from naming individuals to recording工时定额 (labor quotas) in palace archives (Zaobanchu). Records specify the number of craftsmen and work units (e.g., area of chasing) rather than individual names on the artifact itself.
