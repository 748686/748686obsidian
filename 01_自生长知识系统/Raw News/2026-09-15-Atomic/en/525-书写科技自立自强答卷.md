### [Cultural essay outlines the evolving role of Chinese folk songs](http://paper.people.com.cn/rmrb/pc/content/202609/15/content_30181056.html) ⭐️ ?/10

A cultural essay published in People&\#x27;s Daily argues that Chinese folk songs, or &\#x27;minge&\#x27;, remain vital and are not inherently obsolete despite rapid social changes. The author highlights recent viral successes on social media, such as the Northwest &\#x27;Hua&\#x27;er&\#x27; song &\#x27;Galian Shou&\#x27; and the Jingpo ethnic &\#x27;Munao Song&\#x27;, which have garnered hundreds of millions of plays and demonstrated the renewed appeal of traditional music. The essay uses the twenty-year resurgence of Mongolian long songs on the Xilin Gol grassland as a case study, noting that younger generations are increasingly adopting and adapting the art form in both traditional settings and modern urban contexts. It cautions that successful innovation requires a deep understanding of a song&\#x27;s historical, geographic, and social roots, warning against superficially appropriating traditional musical symbols without grasping their underlying cultural context.

rss · 人民日报\(People&\#x27;s Daily\) · Sep 15, 00:26

**「Background」** Chinese folk songs are diverse traditional musical forms that are primarily transmitted through oral tradition and vary significantly by region and ethnic group. As China undergoes rapid urbanization and modernization, many of the everyday and ceremonial environments that historically supported these songs have diminished, leading to a loss of their traditional living context. However, contemporary media, educational institutions, and modern performance stages have created new avenues for their preservation and widespread dissemination.

**「Impact」** For contemporary musicians, cultural practitioners, and the public, the essay advocates that authentic creative transformation of folk music requires deep historical and cultural research to preserve the original traditions and prevent the loss of foundational knowledge. It highlights the importance of maintaining the practice of listening to traditional music within its specific geographic and lifestyle context to avoid reducing complex cultural heritage to superficial pop adaptations.
