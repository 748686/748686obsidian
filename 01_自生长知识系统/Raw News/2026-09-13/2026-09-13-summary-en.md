### [Epistemic provenance in scientific epistemology](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/2BByBCpzaChsGuPhg/scientific-epistemology-needs-history-part-1-of-2) ⭐️ ?/10

rss · LessWrong AI风险社区 · Sep 12, 11:26

**「Background」** Contemporary rationalist epistemology largely relies on Bayesian updating over a fixed set of hypotheses, but scientific progress often involves generating entirely new theories that were not in the original hypothesis space.

**「Solution」** The author builds on Richard Ngo&\#x27;s extension of Garrabrant Induction, which handles multiple computational algorithms and shifting actions over time, and argues that even this approach misses a critical dimension: epistemic provenance. This is the full history of how a model was constructed—including which observations were used, what was discarded, and what selection pressures shaped it. The author illustrates this with a thought experiment: two problem solvers produce the same equation M, but one derived it from first principles and then tested it against observations, while the other brute-forced a fit to those same observations. Although the final model is identical, their epistemic provenance differs, and only a record of the process can reveal whether the model&\#x27;s agreement with data was genuinely exposed to falsification or merely a selection artifact.

**「Takeaway」** A formal scientific epistemology must track not just model accuracy but epistemic provenance—because a record of success cannot by itself establish how reliable that success is.
