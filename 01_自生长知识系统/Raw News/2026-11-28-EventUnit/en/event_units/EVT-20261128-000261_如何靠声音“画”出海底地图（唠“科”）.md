On November 28, 2026, the *People's Daily* (People's Daily / *Renmin Ribao*) published a popular science article in its Technology and New Knowledge section (Page 06). The article, titled "How to 'Draw' a Seafloor Map Using Sound," explains the technological process of underwater acoustic surveying. The piece describes how sound waves are utilized to map the deep sea, detailing the integration of acoustic detection, signal processing, geodesy, satellite navigation, and computer modeling. It highlights the transition from single-beam to multi-beam echosounders and explains the complex data correction processes required to generate accurate seafloor topographic maps.

## Core Facts
- **Publication Details**: The article was published in the *People's Daily* on November 28, 2026, on Page 06 (Technology · New Knowledge).
- **Authorship**: The content was authored by Liu Xiaodong, a researcher at the Institute of Acoustics, Chinese Academy of Sciences. It was compiled by reporter Wu Yuehui.
- **Technical Principle**: Sound waves are the primary information carrier for exploring the ocean because light and electromagnetic waves (such as radar signals) attenuate rapidly or cannot propagate over long distances in seawater. Sound waves can travel tens of kilometers or more in water.
- **Equipment Evolution**: Underwater surveying equipment has iterated from single-beam to multi-beam echosounders. Modern multi-beam systems emit and receive hundreds to thousands of sound beams in different directions, forming an "acoustic fan" that scans the seabed perpendicular to the ship's track.
- **Data Processing Steps**:
  - Raw data from acoustic equipment must be converted into true geographic coordinates in the Earth's reference frame.
  - Vessel-mounted Global Satellite Positioning Sensors are used to lock the ship's absolute location and provide a unified time reference for spatial-temporal synchronization.
  - Sound speed profile data is required to correct for changes in sound propagation velocity caused by variations in seawater temperature, salinity, and static pressure at different depths.
  - Vertical reference differences caused by tides are corrected to unify the elevation baseline.
  - False points caused by fish schools or bubbles are filtered out.
  - Adjacent survey line overlap areas are aligned to generate a regular grid digital seafloor model.
  - Bathy lines are traced, and shading/layering colors are applied to create a 3D visual representation.
- **Applications**: High-precision seafloor maps are essential for safe shipping routes, port construction, laying submarine cables/pipelines, and marine engineering. They support "terrain-aided navigation" for underwater platforms and aid scientific research in locating hydrothermal vents, cold seeps, coral reefs, and studying underwater landslides and plate tectonics.
- **Extended Capabilities**: Hydroacoustics is also used to detect whale populations, seismic waves, and underwater volcanic eruptions.
