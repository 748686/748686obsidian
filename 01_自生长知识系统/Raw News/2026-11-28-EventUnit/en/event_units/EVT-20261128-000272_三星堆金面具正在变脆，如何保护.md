# Event Name
**Research and Conservation Strategy for Sanxingdui Gold Mask Embrittlement**

## Event Overview
This event documents the publication of a comprehensive research report by the Beijing University of Science and Technology Institute of Science, Technology History and Cultural Heritage, detailing the micro-corrosion mechanisms causing Sanxingdui gold masks to become brittle. Published in the *People's Daily* on November 28, 2026, the article explains how researchers identified lead-containing intermetallic phases within the gold-silver alloy as the primary cause of fragility. The study utilized Scanning Electrochemical Cell Microscopy (SECCM) to map corrosion activity at the micro-scale and identified sodium decanoate (sodium caprate) as an effective corrosion inhibitor to protect the artifacts. This research represents a significant advancement in cultural heritage conservation by linking atomic-scale material analysis with practical protective treatments for major archaeological finds.

## Core Facts
*   **Phenomenon**: Sanxingdui gold masks exhibit signs of brittleness and "crumbling" (loss of material) at damaged edges, despite retaining metallic luster.
*   **Material Composition**: The masks are not pure gold but gold-silver alloys with residual lead-containing intermetallic phases.
*   **Cause of Brittlness**: The lead-containing phases are more corrosion-active than the main gold-silver matrix. As these phases corrode and are lost, the binding between grains weakens, leading to structural fragility.
*   **Methodology**: Researchers used a glass probe with a ~3-micrometer tip equipped with Scanning Electrochemical Cell Microscopy (SECCM) to create "corrosion maps" of micro-regions.
*   **Conservation Solution**: Sodium decanoate (a saturated fatty acid sodium salt) was identified as the optimal corrosion inhibitor. Its structure allows one end to bind with surface lead while the carbon chain acts as a hydrophobic barrier against water.
*   **Institutional Collaboration**: The project is coordinated by the Sichuan Institute of Archaeology, led by Chief Scientist Ma Yanru (National Museum of China), with research conducted at Beijing University of Science and Technology and Peking University.
*   **Publication Date**: The findings were reported in the *People's Daily* on November 28, 2026.

## Event Conclusion
The 2026 research initiative successfully diagnosed the root cause of brittleness in Sanxingdui gold masks as the corrosion of lead-containing intermetallic phases within the gold-silver alloy. By employing high-resolution SECCM technology, researchers mapped the vulnerable micro-zones and validated sodium decanoate as an effective, mechanism-based corrosion inhibitor. This work bridges the gap between archaeological conservation and materials science, offering a precise, chemically-tailored solution to preserve one of China's most significant cultural heritage sites. The event marks a shift from reactive repair to proactive, data-driven prevention in museum conservation.
