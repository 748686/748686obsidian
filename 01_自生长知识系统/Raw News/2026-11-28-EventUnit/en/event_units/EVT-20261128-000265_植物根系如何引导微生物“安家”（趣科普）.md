2.  **Biological Mechanism**:
    *   **Role of Roots**: Plant roots host large numbers of microorganisms that help absorb nutrients.
    *   **Casparian Strip Function**: The Casparian strip (located in the endodermis of the root) acts as an "intelligent gate." When gaps appear in this structure, internal nutrients leak outward.
    *   **Leaking Substance**: The primary leaked nutrient is amino acids, specifically **glutamine** (谷氨酰胺), which is released in the highest quantities.
    *   **Microbial Response**: Microorganisms sense the glutamine leakage and migrate toward it, settling on the root surface in an orderly manner.

3.  **Trigger for "Settlement"**:
    *   **Side Root Growth**: The Casparian strip "opens" (forms gaps) when side roots grow from the main root.
    *   **Signal**: This leakage acts like a "signal flare," indicating the plant's high need for nutrients during side root development.
    *   **Map Creation**: Researchers used the location of these microbial "settling zones" to map the distribution of root-associated microbes.

4.  **Impact on Plant Health**:
    *   **Beneficial vs. Pathogenic**: The outcome depends on the type of microbe. Beneficial microbes promote root growth and nutrient absorption; pathogenic microbes harm the plant.
    *   **Regulation**: The Casparian strip maintains the health balance of the rhizosphere microbial community by preventing random nutrient leakage.

5.  **Agricultural Application**:
    *   The understanding of this "settling map" provides a new direction for developing **microbial fertilizers**.
    *   It offers a theoretical basis and technical pathway for **green agriculture**.
