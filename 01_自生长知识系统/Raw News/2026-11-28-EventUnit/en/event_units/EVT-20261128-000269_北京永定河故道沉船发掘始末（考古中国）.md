# Event Name
The 2025–2026 Archaeological Excavation of Three Sunk Wooden Boats in the Yongding River Ancient Channel (Beijing)

## Event Overview
Between September 2025 and late 2026, archaeologists from the Beijing Municipal Institute of Archaeology excavated three well-preserved ancient wooden boats at a depth of approximately 7 meters in the Yongding River ancient channel in Beijing. This excavation was conducted in the Fengtai District Wanquansi Village area to support urban development projects. The discovery is noted as the first formally excavated sunken ship site in the Beijing region and the largest, most intact group of Han-Jin dynasty inland river vessels found in northern China. The event involved multi-disciplinary scientific analysis, including carbon-14 dating, wooden preservation, and human remains analysis.

## Core Facts
*   **Location:** Wanquansi Village, Fengtai District, Beijing. Specifically located in the southwest corner of the Jin Zhongdu (Jin Capital), near the north-east side of the Duanli Gate site (less than 100 meters linear distance).
*   **Timeline:**
    *   **Sept 2025:** Initial archaeological fieldwork began to investigate early strata and river channel remains.
    *   **Winter 2025 / Early 2026:** The boats were exposed and excavated.
    *   **Jan 16, 2026:** A burnt "Guan" (official) character was discovered on the stern sideboard of the second boat.
    *   **Late 2026:** Report publication and initial preservation completed.
*   **Objects Excavated:**
    *   Three wooden boats, provisionally named "Yongding River No. 1," "No. 2," and "No. 3."
    *   Ten "Guan" (official) characters branded onto the boats (1 on No. 2 stern, 9 on other parts).
    *   Wooden oar models and suspected mat-weaving materials.
    *   Human remains: One female skeleton (approx. 25 years old, skull missing, posture suggesting struggle/drowning) and two separate human skulls found near the hulls.
    *   Faunal remains: Horses, yellow cattle, sheep, pigs, dogs.
    *   Pottery and other artifacts: Ceramic pots, tiles, and well rings scattered near the ships.
*   **Dating:**
    *   Radiocarbon dating indicates the boats date to the **Late Eastern Han to Wei-Jin period** (approx. 2000 years old).
*   **Preservation Method:**
    *   The boats were found in thick sand and stone layers at 7 meters depth, protected by silt.
    *   Extraction method: Steel beam framework internal support, overall packing, "rigid support + flexible buffering."
    *   Transport: "Ground tank" and forklifts to move to the laboratory.
    *   Current Status: Initial temperature and humidity control completed.

## Known Current Impact
*   **Cultural Heritage:** Designated as the first formally excavated sunken ship site in Beijing.
*   **Scientific Research:** Ongoing multi-disciplinary studies including carbon-14, environmental archaeology, wood archaeology, plant archaeology, human bone archaeology, animal archaeology, soil micromorphology, residue analysis, and pest analysis.
*   **Preservation:** The ships have been successfully moved to a laboratory environment for long-term preservation, using methods specifically designed for waterlogged fragile wooden artifacts.
*   **Urban Development:** The excavation was a prerequisite for the Wanquansi Village shantytown renovation project in Fengtai District.

## What Cannot Currently Be Determined

*   **Identity of Human Remains:** It is currently undetermined whether the two skulls and the female skeleton belong to the original crew, later fishermen, or victims of the same disaster that sank the ships. Ancient DNA analysis is ongoing to resolve this.
*   **Specific Historical Context:** While dated to Late Eastern Han-Wei-Jin, the specific political or logistical function of these "official" (Guan) ships in the Yongding River trade or transport network during that specific era is still under analysis.
*   **Fate of the Dock:** The nearby dock mentioned in the reconstruction is believed to have been destroyed by floodwaters and later river channel changes; its precise location or structure is unknown.

## Event Conclusion
The excavation of three wooden boats in the Yongding River ancient channel in Beijing represents a significant archaeological discovery for the region. Discovered during urban construction preparation in 2025, the vessels date to the Late Eastern Han-Wei-Jin period. The presence of "Guan" (official) brandings suggests state-controlled or official use of these inland waterways in ancient times. The exceptional preservation of the wood, resulting from waterlogged silt conditions, allows for detailed multi-disciplinary analysis. While the primary cause of the ships' submersion is inferred to be a sudden flood event that buried them after they had been set aside for repair, the full historical narrative, including the fate of the individuals associated with the remains found with the ships, remains under active investigation.
