## Event Overview

On November 28, 2026, *People's Daily* reported on a scientific advancement by the Extraterrestrial Environment Physics and Intelligent Detection Team at the Harbin Institute of Technology (HIT). The team has developed a novel solar concentrator designed to address the challenge of manufacturing construction materials on the Moon by sintering lunar soil. By harnessing solar energy through a specific mirror configuration, this technology aims to provide a sustainable method for lunar base construction, reducing the reliance on transporting materials from Earth. The research outcomes were published in the international academic journal *Engineering*.

## Core Facts

*   **Institution:** Harbin Institute of Technology (HIT), specifically the Extraterrestrial Environment Physics and Intelligent Detection Team and the Space Environment and Material Science Institute.
*   **Publication:** Relevant results were published in the international academic journal *Engineering*.
*   **Technology:** A novel solar concentrator utilizing a "zero-shadow three-mirror + conjugate mirror aberration compensation" design.
    *   **Components:** Three specially designed mirrors work collaboratively: an off-axis concentrator collects sunlight, a conjugate mirror corrects optical aberrations, and a plane mirror adjusts the light path.
    *   **Goal:** To concentrate sunlight into a high-temperature focal point to melt or sinter lunar regolith (soil) into building blocks ("in-situ resource utilization").
*   **Performance Metrics (Earth-based testing):**
    *   **Aperture:** 300 mm.
    *   **Concentration Ratio:** Effective ratio of 548.
    *   **Focal Temperature (Outdoor/Earth):** Stably exceeded 800°C, with a maximum recorded temperature of 892°C.
*   **Projected Lunar Performance:**
    *   **Estimated Focal Temperature:** Dr. Zhang Yanlong (HIT PhD student) estimated that under lunar irradiation and pure radiation heat exchange conditions, the focal temperature could reach 1,326°C.
    *   **Feasibility:** At 1,326°C, the mineral components of the majority of lunar regolith can be melted.
*   **Key Personnel:**
    *   **Li Lifang:** Researcher at HIT Space Environment and Material Science Institute; Leader of the Extraterrestrial Team.
    *   **Zhang Yanlong:** PhD student at HIT School of Mechatronic Engineering (performed lunar thermal estimation).
    *   **Fang Yuan:** *People's Daily* reporter.
    *   **Li Shuangyu:** Communications contributor to the article.
