# River Chief System Long-Term River Governance (10-Year Practice)

## Event Overview
In November 2026, the *People's Daily* published an article titled "From the River Chief System to Long-Term River Governance" ("从河长制看河长治"), reviewing the 10-year practice of the River Chief System. The system, which mandates that every river have a responsible leader ("River Chief"), was formally nationalized in 2016 and fully implemented across all 31 provinces by June 2018. This 10-year milestone review highlights the system's evolution from local pilots in Zhejiang (starting in 2003) to a national governance framework centered on Party and government leadership responsibility. The article argues that the system has successfully shifted China's river management from fragmented departmental control to coordinated "Party-government responsibility, basin coordination, regional synergy, and departmental linkage," resulting in improved water quality and ecological restoration.

## Core Facts
*   **System Origin & Nationalization:** The River Chief System was pioneered at the grassroots level in Changxing County, Zhejiang, starting in 2003. It was elevated to a national institutional innovation when the General Office of the CPC Central Committee and the General Office of the State Council issued the "Opinions on Comprehensively Promoting the River Chief System" on **November 28, 2016**.
*   **Full Implementation:** By **June 2018**, all **31 provinces** in China had fully established the River Chief System.
*   **Organizational Structure:** The system operates on a four-tier hierarchy: Provincial dual-chief river chiefs (led by provincial party and government heads) and river chiefs at the provincial, municipal, county, and township levels.
*   **Statistical Data (2016–2025/2026):**
    *   China has **45,203 rivers** with a basin area of 50 square kilometers or more.
    *   China has **2,865 lakes** with a permanent water surface area of 1 square kilometer or more.
    *   The proportion of national surface water sections rated Class I–III increased from **67.8% in 2016** to **91.4% in 2025**.
    *   Since 2022, the ecological flow attainment rate for important national rivers and lakes has remained stable above **90%**.
    *   Cumulatively, **315,000 "Four Illnesses"** (illegal occupation, illegal mining, illegal dumping, illegal construction) issues were rectified.
    *   **1,361** illegal docks were removed along the Yangtze River.
*   **Legal & Policy Framework:**
    *   The "Opinions on Comprehensively Promoting River and Lake Protection and Management" were issued.
    *   The "Ecological Environment Code" came into effect.
    *   The "Water Law of the People's Republic of China" is scheduled to be implemented on **March 22, 2027**.
    *   River Chief performance is included in Central Ecological and Environmental Protection Supervision and the assessment of the Water Resource Rigid Constraint System.

*   **Institutional Consolidation:** Establishment of a rigorous accountability system where river chiefs face assessment and ecological supervision.
*   **Economic Shifts:**

## Event Conclusion
The 10-year practice of the River Chief System (2016–2026) has successfully institutionalized a top-down, party-led accountability framework for water management in China. By the end of this decade, the system has transitioned from a pilot policy to a comprehensive national standard, achieving measurable improvements in water quality (91.4% Class I-III waters) and ecological flow stability. The governance model has evolved from fragmented "multiple heads" management to a coordinated "Party-Government-Basin-Region-Department" synergy. Looking forward, the system is being solidified through legislative frameworks (2027 Water Law) and expanded into economic value realization (eco-tourism, green industry), marking a shift from pure protection to integrated "ecological-high quality development."
