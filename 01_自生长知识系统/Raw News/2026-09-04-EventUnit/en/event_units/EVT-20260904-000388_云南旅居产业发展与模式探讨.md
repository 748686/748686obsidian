## Event Overview
In September 2026, *People's Daily* published a special report and expert commentary on the development of the "sojourn" (living/traveling) industry in Yunnan Province, China. The coverage analyzes the transition from traditional tourism to long-term sojourn tourism ("travel to stay"), highlighting how Yunnan is leveraging its climate, ecology, and culture to become a national hub for this emerging economic sector. The event encompasses policy implementations, statistical data from 2025, expert analysis on service upgrades, and case studies of elderly care and entrepreneurial support.

## Core Facts
*   **Market Scale (2025):** Yunnan received 5.5124 million sojourn visitors, generating total expenditures of 69.705 billion yuan. Both figures represented year-on-year growth of 41.4% and 54.2%, respectively, with an average stay duration of 83 days.
*   **Policy Framework:** The "15th Five-Year Plan" for building a strong tourism nation (issued July 2026) proposes guiding suitable regions to develop sojourn industries. Yunnan’s provincial "15th Five-Year Plan" emphasizes upgrading products and extending services toward entrepreneurship, learning, and health/wellness.
*   **Demographic Shift:** In 2025, nearly 980,000 elderly sojourners visited Yunnan, a 91.5% increase. Approximately 80% of sojourners are aged 18–60.
*   **Key Destinations:** 
    *   **Tengchong:** Popular for geothermal springs and mid-life/retiree wellness.
    *   **Yuanmou County:** Attracted over 110,000 sojourners in 2025 (a ~5x increase since 2019), noted for low living costs and railway connectivity.
    *   **Qujing (Qilin District):** A summer destination attracting over 300,000 visitors, focusing on elderly care infrastructure.
    *   **Dali:** A hub for creative entrepreneurs and cultural immersion.
    *   **Xishuangbanna (Jinghong):** A winter destination facing seasonal infrastructure pressure.

## Different Country / Regional Perspectives
*   **Yunnan Provincial Perspective:** Focuses on leveraging natural endowments (climate, biodiversity, ethnic culture) and converting "passengers" into "residents" and "creators." The strategy involves upgrading from sightseeing to immersive living, supported by public service standardization and negative-list regulation.
*   **National Comparison (via Expert Commentary):**
    *   **Hainan Sanya:** Positioned as a mature benchmark for "migratory bird" elderly care, evolving toward health-kongfu and quality long-stay models.
    *   **Guangxi Beibu Gulf:** Targeted at younger demographics seeking active lifestyle elements (water sports) driven by warm winter climates.
*   **Grassroots/Local Implementation:**
    *   **Qujing Qilin District:** Adopted a "low-cost lease + public construction/private operation" model to repurpose idle hotels into elderly care centers, integrating local medical resources.
    *   **Dali:** Implemented a "Human Resources and Social Security Government Bottom-line + Professional Agency Empowerment" dual service model, including a dedicated sojourn entrepreneurship service window.
    *   **Jinghong:** Utilized flexible resource allocation, renting buses from neighboring counties during peak seasons rather than permanent capital expansion.

## Event Conclusion
The Yunnan sojourn industry has evolved from a niche tourism trend into a significant economic engine, characterized by a shift from short-term sightseeing to long-term residential experiences. In 2025, the sector saw substantial growth with over 5.5 million visitors contributing nearly 70 billion yuan. Success is driven by Yunnan’s unique climatic advantages, diverse ethnic culture, and proactive policy measures, including the "Positive and Negative List" and the extension of services toward elderly care (sojourn-health) and entrepreneurship (sojourn-creation). Experts emphasize that sustainable growth requires moving beyond simple tourism development, focusing instead on elastic public service supply, strict adherence to regulatory red lines, and balancing the interests of sojourners with local residents. The model highlights a broader national trend where regions leverage specialized resources to capture the growing market for quality-of-life-based travel.
