# Event Name

Medical "Subtraction" and "Addition": Commentary on Healthcare Reform and Patient-Centric Service Optimization

## Event Overview

On September 18, 2026, *People's Daily* published a commentator observation titled "Perceiving the Steady and Warm 'Subtraction' and 'Addition'" (感悟踏实而温热的“减”与“加”). The article analyzes recent shifts in China’s medical care experience through the lens of two specific patient-physician interactions: one involving pre-visit online consultation to reduce unnecessary steps, and the other involving the mutual recognition of medical test results across different institutions.

The central thesis argues that China’s healthcare reform is transitioning from simple service provision to a dual strategy of "subtraction" (reducing redundant procedures, wait times, and information barriers) and "addition" (enhancing institutional standards, equipment uniformity, and professional quality control). The text frames these changes as part of the broader "Healthy China" initiative, emphasizing that efficiency gains are driven by systemic institutional upgrades rather than individual effort alone.

## Core Facts

*   **Publication Date:** September 18, 2026.
*   **Publication Source:** *People's Daily* (Paper Edition), Page 05 (Commentary Section).
*   **Article Type:** Commentator Observation (评论员观察).
*   **Author:** Li Hongxing.
*   **Key Anecdote 1 (Subtraction):** A patient’s family shared an experience where using an internet hospital for a preliminary consultation and basic test orders prior to an in-person visit saved time and reduced physical burden ("less hassle").
*   **Key Anecdote 2 (Addition):** A patient presented X-ray/imaging results from a different location and asked if they needed to be redone; the physician confirmed that if the images were qualified and clear, they did not need to be retaken, citing the mutual recognition of inspection and testing results.
*   **Institutional Mechanisms Cited:** Remote consultation, electronic prescriptions, mutual recognition of examination and testing results, medical consortia (医联体), and cross-regional medical insurance registration.
*   **Core Argument:** The "subtraction" of friction is enabled by the "addition" of standardized quality controls, unified equipment standards, and improved professional capabilities across medical institutions.
