In a September 29, 2026 People&\#x27;s Daily review, critic Gao Xiaoli praises director Hu Mei&\#x27;s film "Ning Zhi Shou" \(also referenced in English contexts as related to the tofu artisan story\) for its committed return to realism amid trends favoring big budgets and strong plot twists. The 117-minute film uses a跨年代跨年代 narrative centered on Pan Douni, a woman whose life is inspired by real tofu makers in Shipai Town, Zhongxiang, Hubei, weaving individual destiny with craftsmanship and social change. Hu Mei&\#x27;s team conducted dozens of research trips and interviewed nearly 100 tofu artisans, grounding details such as costumes, Sets, dried chicken, cloth artifacts, and rice tea in authentic local life. The review argues that the film&\#x27;s restrained yet layered emotional arcs—especially a culminating scene between adoptive and birth mothers—demonstrate how sincere, character-driven storytelling can resonate more effectively than spectacle.

**「Background」** Chinese cinema has seen increasing commercial pressure toward high-concept productions, making realist films that prioritize everyday labor and regional culture notable counterpoints. The "tofu west beauty" motif is a longstanding cultural archetype, but Hu Mei&\#x27;s version reframes it around female self-reliance rather than sacrifice for men or tragic exploitation.

**「Impact」** For viewers and critics concerned about the dominance of spectacle-driven narratives, the review signals official recognition that modest, research-grounded films can still carry significant cultural weight.
