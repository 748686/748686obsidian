Guinness to close Maryland brewery, leaving only one remaining US location

Guinness is preparing to close its Open Gate Brewery near Baltimore that opened in 2018, leaving 174 employees out of work and just one remaining U.S. location.

The Guinness Open Gate Brewery in Halethorpe, Maryland, just outside Baltimore, will soon close, leaving the iconic

Irish beer

brand's Chicago brewery as its only U.S. location.

Nov. 1 will mark the brewery's final day open to the public, leaving 174 Aramark employees out of work, according to a

notice filed

with the Maryland Department of Labor Oct. 1.

The move comes amid a $1 billion restructuring plan at Diageo, Guinness' parent company.

Some teams at Diageo are losing 20% to 30% of their staff,

Reuters reported

in July.

The Guinness Open Gate Brewery near Baltimore is closing to the public Nov. 1.

(Sébastien Duval/AFP via Getty Images)

"This decision follows a careful review of our operations and long-term business priorities," a Diageo spokesperson told Fox News Digital. "It is not a reflection of the passion, talent or dedication of our team, nor of the support we have received from consumers and the Baltimore community since we opened our doors in 2018."

Since opening in 2018, the Guinness Open Gate Brewery has welcomed more than 2 million visitors, according to a post on its Facebook page.

The brewery

was the first to open in the U.S. since the Irish beer maker's previous brewery in New York closed in 1954, FOX Business reported at the time.

"It's obviously a shame to see the Guinness brewery close in Maryland," said Derek Brown of Drink Company, a Washington, D.C.-based hospitality consulting and events company.

"However, I'm not surprised in the sense that their core product remains Guinness, and now Guinness Zero," Brown told Fox News Digital. "The brewery focused on experimental products, which are interesting in themselves, but not necessarily primary drivers of Guinness' business."

A drink expert told Fox News Digital that while it's sad to see the Guinness brewery closing, he's not surprised given the focus on "experimental products."

(Sebastien Duval/AFP via Getty Images)

The experimental portion of the brewery allowed guests to concoct their own brews, and more than 700 beers were created over the years, according to the brewery's Facebook post.

But changing trends in alcohol consumption likely played a role in the decision, industry experts say.

Changing drinking habits are likely another factor contributing to the closure, experts claim.

More Americans are also alternating between alcoholic and non-alcoholic beverages — a practice known as

"zebra striping"

— partly contributing to the decline, according to experts.
