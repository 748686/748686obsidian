## Core Facts

**1. Additive Manufacturing (3D Printing) Expansion**
*   **Global Market:** In 2025, the global 3D printing market size was approximately $24.2 billion (Article #550).
*   **Aerospace:**
    *   **China:** AVIC Maite (Zhonghang MAITE) utilized self-developed titanium alloy powder and bio-inspired topology optimization (mimicking bird bones/insect wings) to create one-piece high-performance wing support components. This reduced weight by 40% and increased endurance by 26% (Article #550).
    *   **USA:** SpaceX reduced the number of components in its Raptor engine series from hundreds to a few using 3D printing, significantly lowering manufacturing costs (Article #550).
*   **Marine Engineering:**
    *   **China:** Beihang University’s Chongqing Innovation Center used arc 3D printing to manufacture ship propellers, reducing delivery time from approximately 3 months (traditional casting) to 10 days (Article #550).
    *   **UAE:** In 2025, the UAE launched the world’s first fully 3D-printed catamaran yacht using recycled ocean plastic waste. Construction took just over 6 days, with costs at one-third of traditional yachts (Article #550).
*   **Nuclear Engineering:** US startup Ampera manufactured the world’s first full-size nuclear reactor module using 3D printing, including a silicon carbide reactor core and pressure vessel. The module is designed to operate for 30 years without intermediate refueling or frequent maintenance (Article #550).
*   **Space Manufacturing:**
    *   China achieved the first successful preparation of complete metal components in suborbital microgravity environments and completed space metal additive manufacturing demonstration verification in a 600 km orbit within the year (Article #550).
    *   Xi’an Jiaotong University is advancing metal wire direct writing processes and simulated lunar soil brick printing for lunar-base manufacturing (Article #550).
*   **Biomedical:** Xi’an Jiaotong University developed an individualized 3D-printed biodegradable breast stent that mimics breast tissue mechanics to guide autogenous tissue growth (Article #550).

**2. Brain "Self-Cleaning" Mechanism Discovery**
*   **Research Source:** A team from the Gladstone Institutes published findings in the journal *Cell* (Article #551).
*   **Methodology:** Researchers used transgenic mice expressing green fluorescent proteins in neurons (via neurotropic virus injection) to track endogenous protein clearance under physiological conditions, moving beyond traditional extrinsic fluorescent tracer methods (Article #551).
*   **Findings:**
    *   Brain-derived proteins do not rely solely on a single meningeal lymphatic system draining into deep cervical lymph nodes.
    *   Clearance follows a "nearest exit" principle: anterior brain regions drain via the nasal cavity and anterior meninges, while deep brain regions drain via the skull base and posterior meninges (Article #551).
    *   The system resembles a zoned drainage network rather than a single pipe (Article #551).
    *   Immune cells at meningeal boundaries monitor this process and promote immune tolerance to prevent autoimmune damage (Article #551).
*   **Pathological Implications:**
    *   In acute neuroinflammation, increased blood-brain barrier permeability causes proteins to "leak" into circulation (Article #551).
    *   In Alzheimer’s disease models, overall clearance capacity decreases, leading to protein accumulation (Article #551).
    *   The study suggests future therapies should focus on restoring/enhancing clearance pathways, not just reducing protein production (Article #551).

**3. Millimeter-Scale Soft Robot with Autonomous Continuous Jumping**
*   **Research Source:** North Carolina State University team published in the *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences (PNAS)* (Article #552).
*   **Design:** A泪-drop shaped soft ring robot made of liquid crystal elastomer (LCE) with a V-shaped rigid tail.
*   **Mechanism:** Uses "physical intelligence" rather than electronic control. Infrared light creates a temperature differential across the ring, causing twisting and elastic energy storage. At a critical point, the structure失稳 (loses stability), releasing energy to snap the tail against the ground, propelling the robot upward. It auto-unwinds in the air and lands ready for the next cycle (Article #552).
*   **Performance:**
    *   Over 1,000 consecutive jumps without manual resetting or complex control systems (Article #552).
    *   Vertical jump height up to 80 times its own height (Article #552).
    *   Horizontal jump distance over 3 times its body length (Article #552).
*   **Amphibious Capability:** Modified versions can float, swim, and jump back onto land ("jump-swim-jump") (Article #552).
*   **Center of Mass Regulation:** Adding weight to the head suppresses tumbling and improves directional stability (Article #552).
