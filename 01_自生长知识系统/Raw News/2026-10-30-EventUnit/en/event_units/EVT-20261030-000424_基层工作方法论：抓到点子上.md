## Core Facts

**1. Subject Identity and Background**
*   **Name:** Ding Yihui (丁一汇).
*   **Birth:** July 1938.
*   **Place of Birth:** Caoxian, Shandong Province.
*   **Profession:** Meteorologist and climatologist; Academician of the Chinese Academy of Engineering.
*   **Role:** First Director of the National Climate Center (established 1994).
*   **Education:** Undergraduate studies at Peking University.

**2. Career Origins**
*   Ding originally wished to study physics.
*   In 1957, prior to the National College Entrance Examination (Gaokao), he was diagnosed with red-green color weakness.
*   Due to this medical restriction, he could not enroll in physics and转向 (turned to) meteorology instead.
*   He describes this as the beginning of a "long quest" in a "mysterious and vast world."

**3. Research Evolution and Key Contributions**
*   **1975 Incident:** The collapse of reservoirs in Zhumadian, Henan, caused by localized cumulative precipitation, profoundly affected Ding. He joined an investigation team from the State Meteorological Administration (predecessor to the China Meteorological Administration).
*   **1975–1979 (Rainstorm Research):** Dedicated four years to studying rainstorms.
    *   Summarized the synoptic model, formation mechanism, and dynamic/thermodynamic conditions of heavy rain in China.
    *   Proposed parameterization schemes for describing the effects of mesoscale cumulus clusters.
    *   Conducted seven research trips to the Yellow River Conservancy Commission.
    *   Under the guidance of mentor Tao Shiyan, completed China's first monograph on rainstorm research.
    *   Awarded: First Prize of the Chinese Academy of Sciences Natural Science Award.
*   **1980s (Climate Prediction System):**
    *   At mid-decade, China's climate prediction relied primarily on empirical statistics.
    *   With the promotion of Zou Jingmeng (then Chairman of the World Meteorological Organization), Ding presided over the development of an objective, quantitative short-term climate prediction system.
    *   1994: National Climate Center established; Ding became its first director.
    *   1994–1999: Presided over the "Ninth Five-Year Plan" national key project: "Research on China's Short-term Climate Prediction System."
    *   1998: Numerical forecast model-based climate predictions officially entered application.
    *   2003: The project won the First Prize of the National Science and Technology Progress Award.
    *   The effort involved approximately 700 scientists.

**4. International Cooperation and Diplomacy**
*   **1979:** Dispatched by the WMO to India to participate in the Monsoon Experiment; analyzed weather charts and observation data late into the night.
*   **1995 (Rome Plenary Session):** Served as Vice Chairman of IPCC Working Group I and member of the IPCC Executive Committee. Acted as a lead spokesperson, listening to diverse viewpoints and organizing responses.
*   **1998 (South China Sea Monsoon Experiment):** Led the first large-scale international monsoon experiment hosted by Chinese scientists.
    *   Participants: Scientists from over a dozen countries.
    *   Resources: Six research vessels at sea.
    *   Ding maintained 24-hour communication with domestic and international researchers.
*   **2005:** Awarded the "Outstanding Achievement Award" by the World Meteorological Organization.

**5. Education and Mentorship**
*   Has supervised more than 70 graduate students (Masters and PhDs) over 50+ years.
*   **Standards:** Requires students to read more than 50 English-language literature references; strictly controls the "three gates" of admission, proposal defense, and thesis writing.
*   **Teaching:** Has taught the course "Advanced Synoptic Meteorology" for over 40 years.
*   **Philosophy:** Believes graduate education relates to a student's entire life and that educators must not "mislead the younger generation." He prepares lectures meticulously, visualizing the logic like a "movie" in his mind.

**6. Scientific Outlook**
*   **Current Status:** Short-term climate prediction requires persistent深耕 (deep cultivation); no shortcuts exist.
*   **Future Projection:** Ding believes breakthrough progress in short-term climate prediction requires at least another 10–20 years, but he is confident the problem will eventually be solved.
*   **Core Belief:** Research must be grounded in national needs and people's livelihoods ("贴着国家需求、连着民生安危"). Individual capabilities are maximized only through collective cooperation.
