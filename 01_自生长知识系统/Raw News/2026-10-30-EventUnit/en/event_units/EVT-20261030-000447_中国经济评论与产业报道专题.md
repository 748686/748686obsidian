## Event Overview

On October 30, 2026, *People's Daily* published three distinct articles covering economic collaboration, literary history, and cultural commentary. The first article (pp. 19) details the industrial cooperation between Shandong Province and Chongqing Municipality, specifically focusing on the Fengjie navel orange industry. The second article (p. 20) is a literary essay by Guo Shuai analyzing the works of Wang Yuanjian and his contributions to Long March literature. The third article (p. 20) is a personal essay by Lü Cha reflecting on his decade-long dedication to illustrating bookstores.

## Core Facts

**1. Shandong-Chongqing Cooperation on Fengjie Navel Oranges (Source: Article #567)**
*   **Investment:** Shandong Province has invested 40.9 million RMB in Luyu (Shandong-Chongqing) cooperation funds.
*   **Projects:** 18 navel orange industry projects have been landed.
*   **Sales:** Cumulative sales of Fengjie navel oranges amount to approximately 680 million RMB, with an annual average sales volume of over 13,000 tons.
*   **Target Beneficiaries:** The cooperation aims to increase income for 300,000 reservoir area fruit farmers.
*   **Infrastructure:** 5.34 million RMB was allocated to upgrade facilities in core production areas such as Baibai Town and Anping Town, including irrigation, smart picking, and water storage projects.
*   **Key Enterprise:** The Huiyuan Group (headquartered in Shandong) is developing a total investment of 3 billion RMB for a complete navel orange production and processing chain in Fengjie.
*   **Outstanding Recognition:** The "10,000 Tons of Fengjie Navel Oranges into Shandong" brand action was selected as a typical case of national consumption assistance aiding rural revitalization by the National Development and Reform Commission.
*   **Market Reach:** Sales channels include consumer cooperation stores in Binzhou and Qingdao ("Fengshang Haopin" exhibition centers), five large wholesale markets (including Qingdongfang Dingxin and Hebei Shouheng Gaobeidian), and overseas promotions in Southeast Asia and Central Asia.

**2. Literary Analysis of Wang Yuanjian (Source: Article #568)**
*   **Author:** Guo Shuai, affiliated with the School of Chinese Language and Literature, Southwest University.
*   **Subject:** Wang Yuanjian (1929–2000), a writer who did not personally participate in the Long March but became a pioneering non-participant narrator of the event.
*   **Key Works:** *Three People* (San Ren Xing), *Seven Matches* (Qi Gen Huochai), *Party Dues* (Dang Fei).
*   **Methodology:** Wang spent 10 years (starting 1956) editing *Star Fire Originals* (Xinghuo Liaoyuan), during which he interviewed eight of the Ten Marshals, six of the Ten Generals, and hundreds of other veterans. He also physically retraced the Long March route twice over a decade.
*   **Aesthetic Characteristic:** "Truthful and Sublime" (Zhenshi Er Chonggao). He sought "poetry within history" rather than just historical facts.
*   **External Pressure:** Wang faced质疑 (questioning) regarding his right to write about the Long March due to his lack of personal participation. His wife, Weng Yani, noted his internal anxiety about being advised to "write about subjects he was familiar with."
*   **Motivation:** Despite external doubts and advice to stick to familiar topics, Wang felt compelled by the stories he encountered, stating they "knocked on the doors and windows of his soul."

**3. Personal Essay on Illustrating Bookstores (Source: Article #569)**
*   **Author:** Lü Cha (pseudonym).
*   **Background:** Started working as a shop assistant at Beijing Fengrusong Bookstore in 1996. Has been illustrating bookstores for 10 years.
*   **Output:** Has illustrated over 500 bookstores.
*   **Publications/Exhibitions:** Hosted "Stopping for a Moment in Books" bookstore illustration exhibitions in Jinan Qianmo Bookstore, Zhangqiu Guilai Academy, and Wuhan Yingzhao Book Bureau. Edited the *Bookstore Calendar* for three consecutive years.
*   **Philosophy:** Lü Cha contrasts his "clumsy" hand-drawn style with AI generation, arguing that while AI can generate correct proportions and perspective, it lacks the "care and love" found in prolonged, attentive observation.
*   **Influence:** Cites the encouragement of painter Huang Yongyu, who told him, "It's good, keep drawing like this, don't learn [formal techniques], absolutely don't learn."
