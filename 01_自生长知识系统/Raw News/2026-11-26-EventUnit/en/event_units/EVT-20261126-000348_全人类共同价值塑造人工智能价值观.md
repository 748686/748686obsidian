# Event Name
**"Chinese Purchasing" Phenomenon: University Distribution of AI Tokens and Practical Labor Education Courses**

## Event Overview
On November 26, 2026, *People's Daily* reported on a dual trend in Chinese higher education: several major universities are distributing digital AI resources (tokens and computing power vouchers) to students to facilitate the use of generative AI, while simultaneously introducing practical, hands-on courses (such as baking dough and pipe repair) taught by non-academic staff. This phenomenon is framed as a response to the question of how universities should cultivate student capabilities in the AI era—balancing the "speed" of technological tools with the "resilience" of embodied labor skills. The article argues that while AI reduces the effort required to find answers, it increases the value of asking precise questions, whereas practical labor education preserves essential life skills and character formation that cannot be "one-click" acquired by technology.

## Core Facts
*   **Institutions Involved:** Peking University, Tsinghua University, Tongji University, Beijing University of Posts and Telecommunications, and Nankai University.
*   **AI Resource Distribution:** Peking University, Tsinghua University, Tongji University, and Beijing University of Posts and Telecommunications issued "digital red envelopes" to student accounts, specifically including AI tokens and computing-power vouchers.
*   **Labor Education Initiatives:** Nankai University launched general-education electives where cafeteria workers teach dough kneading and steam-bun making, and maintenance staff teach pipe installation. Other skills mentioned include CPR, manual weaving, beverage mixing, and gardening pruning.
*   **Governance Measures:**
    *   Tsinghua University published AI education-application guidelines covering key teaching and research scenarios.
    *   Beijing University of Posts and Telecommunications requires students to disclose the extent and context of AI usage in assignments, lab reports, and theses.
*   **Public Reaction:** Students reported high demand for the practical courses (described as "hard to register"), and internet users commented on the "strong aftertaste" (impact) of the contrast between high-tech AI resources and basic life skills.

## Event Conclusion
The event highlights a strategic pivot in Chinese higher education regarding AI integration. Rather than viewing AI adoption and practical labor education as competing priorities, institutions are treating them as complementary mechanisms for holistic development. By distributing AI tokens, universities facilitate technological agility, while mandating practical skills ensures embodied resilience. This synthesis reflects a broader governance philosophy that seeks to prevent "cognitive outsourcing" while leveraging AI for efficiency, maintaining human agency in both technical and manual domains.
