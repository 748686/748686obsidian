## Event Overview

This EventUnit synthesizes two distinct but thematically linked developments from mid-November 2026 regarding the Asia-Pacific region. The primary event is the **APEC 2026 Leaders' Week** held in Shenzhen, China (November 18–19), which produced the "Shenzhen Outlook." A secondary, concurrent development involves a **U.S. House of Representatives report** issued shortly before the APEC summit, addressing concerns over U.S.-China aerospace partnerships and potential technology transfers to the Chinese military. While both articles share the date of November 20, 2026, and concern U.S.-China relations in the Asia-Pacific, they represent separate political tracks: multilateral economic cooperation versus unilateral national security policy review.

## Core Facts

### 1. APEC 2026 Leaders' Meeting (Shenzhen, China)
*   **Dates:** November 18–19, 2026.
*   **Location:** Shenzhen, Guangdong Province, China.
*   **Theme:** "Building an Asia-Pacific Community, Promoting Shared Prosperity" (建设亚太共同体，促进共同繁荣).
*   **Key Outcomes:**
    *   Adoption of the **2026 APEC Leaders' Shenzhen Outlook**.
    *   Endorsement of the **"Open, Innovation, Cooperation"** priorities set by the Chinese host.
    *   Approval of the **APEC Connectivity Blueprint**.
    *   Welcome of the **APEC Framework for Trade Digitalization Cooperation (2027–2030)**.
    *   Acceptance of the final review of the **Supply Chain Interconnectivity Framework Action Plan (2022–2026)**.
*   **Future Hosts Acknowledged:** Vietnam (2027), Mexico (2028), Singapore (2030), Japan (2031), Chile (2032), Papua New Guinea (2033), and Peru (2034).
*   **Key Declarations:**
    *   Reaffirmation of APEC as the premier platform for regional economic cooperation.
    *   Commitment to supply chain resilience, particularly for essential goods.
    *   Emphasis on digital economy development, including AI and data cross-border flows.
    *   Recognition of the role of Small and Medium-sized Enterprises (SMEs) and intellectual property protection.

### 2. U.S. House Committee Report on Aerospace Ties
*   **Source:** House Select Committee on the Chinese Communist Party (Chair: John Moolenaar, R-Mich.; Ranking Member: Ro Khanna, D-Calif.).
*   **Report Title:** *"China's Wingmen: How Western Government Policy Enabled China's Rise in the Skies."*
*   **Length:** 65 pages.
*   **Publication Context:** Released November 19, 2026 (one day prior to APEC summit conclusion; reported by Fox News Nov 19, covered in人民日报 contextually on Nov 20 via source #432 metadata).
*   **Key Findings:**
    *   Allegation that legal commercial partnerships transferred advanced technology and manufacturing expertise to Chinese military-linked companies under Beijing's "military-civil fusion" strategy.
    *   Identification of six U.S.-China joint ventures involving **GE Aerospace**, **Honeywell Aerospace**, and **RTX subsidiary Collins Aerospace**.
    *   Specific allegation that GE transferred foundational avionics technology to a joint venture with a subsidiary of **AVIC** (Aviation Industry Corporation of China), which develops military aircraft including the **J-20 stealth fighter**.
    *   Note that Airbus, while European, maintains commercial partnerships with Chinese aerospace conglomerates despite selling its stake in an AVIC affiliate in 2025.
*   **Recommendations:**
    *   Urged the **Federal Aviation Administration (FAA)** not to certify Chinese-manufactured aircraft.
    *   Implicit call for restricting investments in China’s aerospace sector and withholding taxpayer funding from companies building capabilities for U.S. adversaries (as per headline/introductory summary).
