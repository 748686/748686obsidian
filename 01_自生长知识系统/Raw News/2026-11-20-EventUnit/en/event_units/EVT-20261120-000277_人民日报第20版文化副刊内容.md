## Unique Information by Source

### ARTICLE #424: 鲁迅的餐桌上有什么（书里书外）
- **Author**: Chen Wu (陈武).
- **Historical detail**: Lu Xun and Xu Guangping arrived in Shanghai in early October 1927. For over a month, they dined at various restaurants before deciding to contract a "baofan zuo" (包饭作, meal service) with Zhou Jianren's family, who lived in the same building (Jingyun Li).
- **Culinary record**: Two recipe notebooks survived Lu Xun's estate, donated by Xu Guangping in 1950 and now held by the Shanghai Lu Xun Memorial Museum. The first notebook covers November 14, 1927, to April 5, 1928; the second covers April 5 to June 1, 1928 ("今日止").
- **Dietary preferences**: Cantonese, Shaoxing, and local Shanghai cuisine; emphasis on steamed dishes (lotus root cakes, steamed chicken balls, egg-meatloaf, preserved vegetable steamed pork slices).
- **Book referenced**: *Lu Xun's Table* (北京十月文艺出版社), which examines his food preferences regarding seafood, fermented vegetables, freshwater products, mushrooms, and poultry.

### ARTICLE #425: 在江河之间发现中国
- **Author**: Liu Shigu (刘诗古), Associate Professor, Department of History, Peking University.
- **Book reviewed**: *川流不息：近代长江中游的沙洲、码头与滨水社会* (Continuous Flow: Sandbars, Wharves, and Waterfront Society in Modern Middle Reaches of the Yangtze River), by Liu Shigu, Shanghai People's Publishing House.
- **Geographic focus**: The confluence of the Yangtze River, Poyang Lake, and Gan River.
- **Themes**: Sandbar land reclamation disputes, wharf social organization (boat gangs, porters' associations), governance of floating populations (船民), and the interplay of state policy, customary law, and commercial interest in riverine society.

### ARTICLE #426: 逆龄不是逆转，科学护脑有方
- **Author**: Tang Yi (唐毅), Executive Vice President, Xuanwu Hospital, Capital Medical University; Executive Deputy Director, National Clinical Research Center for Neurological Diseases.
- **Book introduced**: 《逆龄脑计划：守护认知的力量》(Reverse Aging Brain Plan: The Power to Guard Cognition), People's Health Publishing House.
- **Key message**: Cognitive decline is not an inevitable result of aging; early screening, pharmacological intervention, lifestyle adjustment, and cognitive training can preserve brain function. The book combines clinical cases with scientific explanations and guidelines.
- **Target audience**: Families and caregivers of cognitive disorder patients.

### ARTICLE #427: 新书架
- **Books recommended**:
  1. 《中央红军长征初期电文研究》(Research on Telegrams During the Early Stage of the Central Red Army's Long March), by Sun Wei, People's Publishing House — focuses on Kuomintang-Communist telegram correspondence during the Long March.
  2. 《隶变》(Clerical Transformation), by Yao Ganming, Shandong People's Publishing House — analyzes the evolution from seal script to clerical script in Qin-Han period and its impact on Chinese calligraphy.

### ARTICLE #428: 先秦典籍中的规划智慧
- **Author**: Guo Lu (郭璐).
- **Book discussed**: 《经之营之：先秦传世文献所见中国早期规划》(Managing Through Creation: Early Chinese Planning as Seen in Pre-Qin Traditional Texts), Tsinghua University Press.
- **Classical sources analyzed**:
  - *Classic of Poetry* (《诗经·公刘》): Duke Liu's settlement planning — terrain survey, elevation-based public building placement, river-centered residential and agricultural zoning.
  - *Guanzi* (《管子》): Quantitative land assessment, population and tax allocation based on land productivity.
  - *Book of Documents* (《尚书》) and *逸周书*: Establishment of Luoyi (洛邑) under King Wu and Duke of Zhou — a comprehensive urban planning system from regional land division to temple and palace construction.
  - *Rites of Zhou* (《周礼》): A bureaucratic-spatial model of governance, with hierarchical networks extending from the king to household-level settlements.
- **Central argument**: Planning as a tool of state governance contributed to the continuity of Chinese civilization; modern urban planning must engage with indigenous textual traditions.
