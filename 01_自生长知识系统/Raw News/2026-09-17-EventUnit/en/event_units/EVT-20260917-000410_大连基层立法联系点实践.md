# Event Name

Dalian Grassroots Legislative Liaison Practice Integrating New Employment Forms and Labor Unions

## Event Overview

On September 17, 2026, *People's Daily* reported on an innovative grassroots legislative feedback model implemented in Dalian, Liaoning Province, China. The People's Square Sub-district in Xigang District, which operates as a grassroots legislative liaison office under the Legislative Affairs Commission of the Standing Committee of the National People's Congress, has integrated its legislative functions with local labor unions. This "Legislative Point + Union" model recruits frontline workers from new employment forms, such as food delivery riders and ride-hailing drivers, to serve as legislative information officers. These workers collect real-time public feedback on proposed regulations during their daily routines and submit it through digital channels. As of the report date, the sub-district had collected 111 pieces of regulatory feedback and provided 789 instances of legal consultation to local workers.

## Core Facts

*   **Location and Administration:** The initiative is based in the People's Square Sub-district, Xigang District, Dalian City, Liaoning Province.
*   **Institutional Basis:** The sub-district is a grassroots legislative liaison office established by the Legislative Affairs Commission of the Standing Committee of the National People's Congress.
*   **Partnerships:** The sub-district cooperates with 22 labor union organizations to facilitate the "Legislative Point + Union" fusion construction.
*   **Mechanism:** "Mobile legislative liaison points" were established, and online contact groups were created to enable 24-hour public opinion collection. Frontline workers (e.g., riders, drivers) are appointed as legislative information officers.
*   **Specific Policy Impact:** During the April 2026 public consultation period for the *Dalian Implementation Rules for the Management of Online Booking Taxi Business Services*, the liaison office organized a seminar with ride-hailing drivers, platform enterprises, and lawyers. This resulted in practical suggestions, including raising the age limit for drivers, unifying licensing management, and strengthening penalties for illegal operations.
*   **Quantitative Data (Since Inception of Model):**
    *   111 pieces of legal/regulatory opinions and suggestions collected.
    *   789 instances of legal consultation provided.
*   **Infrastructure Expansion:** In the current year (2026), the first batch of "Union Legislative Contact Stations" was launched at 3 community joint unions and 3 enterprise unions, with subsequent expansion to include multiple other enterprises and industry unions.
*   **Source Publication:** Reported in *People's Daily* (Issue 13, Political) on September 17, 2026.

## Event Conclusion

The Dalian grassroots legislative practice reported on September 17, 2026, represents a structural adaptation of China's national legislative feedback system to accommodate the "new employment forms" (gig economy) workforce. By integrating the authority of the NPC's grassroots legislative liaison points with the organizational reach of labor unions, the People's Square Sub-district has created a dynamic, 24-hour channel for legislative input. This model not only enhances the responsiveness of local regulations—demonstrated by the consultation on ride-hailing driver age limits—but also extends legal aid services to frontline workers, thereby institutionalizing their access to legal rights. The event reflects a broader trend in Chinese governance to deepen public participation in lawmaking by leveraging the mobile and distributed nature of the modern service workforce.
