'Healey offers cold comfort' and 'A £100 trick or treat?'

Published

4 hours ago

Image caption,

Thursday's papers focus entirely on the Budget delivered in the Commons by Chancellor John Healey. Millions of households are set to receive £100 "to cope with winter energy bills", the Metro reports. The papers asks whether the pre-Halloween announcement is a "trick or treat" before suggesting the discount "will be swallowed up by rising prices".

Image caption,

The iconic red despatch Budget box held by the chancellor features on the front of the Daily Mirror along with the headline "for the many not the few". For his autumn budget, Healey announced a tax increase on banks to fund "breathing space" measures for struggling families, the paper reports.

Image caption,

The chancellor "has started a tax raid on landlords, savers and banks to fund handouts for 20 million households", the Times reports. The Budget was "deliberately modest", the paper explains, as Healey kicked forward big decisions on welfare and defence spending until next autumn.

Image caption,

Meanwhile, the Daily Express focuses on Tory leader Kemi Badenoch criticising the Budget, and asks Healey "Really, is that it?" in its headline. The Conservative leader said his plans were "not bold enough" and the government was "overspent, overborrowed, overtaxed and drowning in an out-of-control welfare system", according to the paper.

Image caption,

The Financial Times describes Healey's Budget as "modest" after he announced "raised taxes on banks, savers and property owners to help fund a cost-of-living package". Healey said his measures were rooted in tight fiscal discipline, but critics warns it lacks tough decisions, the paper says.

Image caption,

The Daily Star focuses on the chancellor botching his words when he called Aldershot, "er, Aldersh*t", in what the paper describes as a "dull Budget".

Image caption,

Healey has put a "squeeze on savers" by mounting a "£4.3bn tax raid on savers and landlords" in his Budget, the Daily Telegraph reports. Elsewhere, the paper reveals the high street giant Poundland is on the "brink of declaring insolvency", even as Healey attempts to help struggling businesses and households, the paper writes.

Image caption,

The Sun playfully lists what Healey has achieved in his Budget including "shirkin'" the "ballooning welfare bill" and helping with the cost of "gherkins". The chancellor has been "accused of ducking tough decisions", the paper reports.

Image caption,

John Healey was set for the "greatest moment of his life", according to the Daily Mail, but the Budget he handed down on Thursday "had all the dramatic bite of cotton wool", writes Quentin Letts in his analysis piece.

Image caption,

UK households in the council tax bands A to D will receive the £100 energy payment, the i Paper reports. According to the paper, the prime minister has ruled out a snap general election and hit back at claims that the Budget is not bold enough, saying the "economy will improve".

Image caption,

The Independent leads with an image of Spain's deadliest train disaster in 13 years after two trains collided just south of the capital Madrid on Wednesday. "Spain is grieving", the paper says. Elsewhere, Healey's Budget "offers scant relief from 'grim picture'", the paper says.

Image caption,

And Healey warned that "global economic storms", including the war in Iran, were behind the cost-of-living crisis continuing as he handed down his first Budget, the Guardian writes.
