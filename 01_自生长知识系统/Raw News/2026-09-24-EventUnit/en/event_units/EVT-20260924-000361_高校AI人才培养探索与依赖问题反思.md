# EVT-20260924-000361: Higher Education AI Talent Cultivation Exploration and Reflection on Dependence

## Event Overview

On September 23, 2026, the *People's Daily* Education Section published two interrelated articles regarding the integration of Artificial Intelligence (AI) into higher education in China. The first article presents an in-depth report on the Beijing Zhongguancun Institute as a model for innovative, industry-integrated AI talent cultivation. The second article offers an editorial commentary warning against the risks of over-reliance on AI tools by university students, advocating for the preservation of independent critical thinking. Together, these sources illustrate the dual narrative of current educational policy: the active promotion of AI integration in talent development alongside concerns about the cognitive and academic integrity implications for students.

## Core Facts

**1. Institutional Innovation at Beijing Zhongguancun Institute**
*   **Establishment:** The institute was established two years prior to September 2026 (circa 2024) by the Ministry of Education and the Beijing Municipal Government, in conjunction with the Zhongguancun Artificial Intelligence Institute.
*   **Location & Structure:** Located in the Zhongguancun Xiaobeiwang Science Park, it features an open campus without walls, connected via a corridor to the Zhongguancun AI Beiwu Community.
*   **Student Body:** As of the September 2026 enrollment, there are over 300 joint-training doctoral students from 31 cooperating universities and more than 30 different majors.
*   **Pedagogical Model:** The institute rejects the traditional "classroom-first, research-later" model. Instead, students select projects first and learn relevant coursework on demand. Admission is based on "AI practical combat" competitions and hackathons rather than standard exams.
*   **Outcomes:** In its first two years, the institute has launched over 120 organized scientific research projects, with more than 10 student-initiated projects. Approximately 40 incubated startup projects have been created, roughly half of which are student-led.

**2. AI Usage and Dependence Among University Students**
*   **Survey Data:** A survey conducted by Fudan University among the entire 2026 freshman cohort revealed that nearly all respondents have used AI tools, with nearly 80% having formed a usage habit.
*   **Usage Patterns:** Students use AI for polishing text, creating presentation slides, translating foreign materials, generating literature reviews, and even as search engines or chat companions.
*   **Concept:** The article identifies this cohort as the "AI Native Generation," analogous to the "Digital Native" generation defined by internet growth.

**3. Leadership Perspectives on AI in Education**
*   **Liu Tieyan (President, Beijing Zhongguancun Institute):** Argues that future talent must be capable of solving complex problems and posing new questions, not just answering them. He emphasizes that entrepreneurship is part of the learning process, not a post-graduation activity.
*   **Gao Song (President, Peking University):** Stated that while AI can integrate existing information, it cannot replace genuine curiosity for unknown fields or independent judgment formed through trial and error.
*   **Shi Yigong (President, Westlake University):** Cited the Confucian concept "Junzi bu qi" (a gentleman is not a vessel/tool), arguing that humans must not become tools and that learning is about building logical systems and value judgments, not just storing knowledge.
*   **Jin Li (President, Fudan University):** Shared a student’s view that AI era education should focus on three skills: asking good questions, exercising judgment (finding AI errors), and taking responsibility for decisions in uncertainty.

## Unique Information by Source

**Article #526 (Beijing Zhongguancun Institute Report):**
*   **Specific Project Examples:**
    *   Zhang Boya (Nankai University joint PhD): Developing a breast tumor intelligent minimally invasive surgery system using AI for 3D imaging and robotic arm control.
    *   Lü Dingyang: Participating in "MatOS," an autonomous agent operating system for materials R&D, which reportedly accelerated R&D processes by 4x in enterprise feedback.
    *   Bai Fengshuo: A PhD student who declined a job at a major tech company to start his own company focused on self-evolving infrastructure for professional intelligence, serving chip design fields.
*   **K-12 Integration:** The establishment of the Beijing Youth Artificial Intelligence Academy (Haidian) in 2025 to create a "basic education – higher education – industry practice" full-chain cultivation system.
*   **Notable Student Achievements:** A 14-year-old middle school student won the "Lobster King" title at a "Lobster Competition" using an open-source AI agent framework.
*   **International Engagement:** Nobel Laureate in Chemistry Michael Levitt visited the institute and interacted with a 9-year-old visitor.
*   **Structural Details:** The institute has an "Education-Science-Talent Management Center" for unified planning. About 30% of mentors have industry backgrounds.

**Article #527 (Editorial on AI Dependence):**
*   **Statistical Data:** Specific metrics from the Fudan University 2026 freshman AI usage survey.
*   **Philosophical Argument:** The concept of the "AI Native Generation" and the risk of "information cocoons" where narrow AI prompts reinforce existing cognitive biases.
*   **Academic Warning:** Reference to a joint statement by 25 Fields Medalists (including Tao Zhexuan and Deng Yu) warning that rapid AI use in mathematics may cause AI development goals to seriously misalign with mathematical research goals, compressing time for validation and学术交流.
*   **Educational Philosophy:** Emphasis on the value of "low efficiency" moments—struggling with problems, debate, and failure—as essential for cognitive growth and character building, which AI cannot replicate.

## Different Country / Regional Perspectives

*   **Beijing, China (Institutional Level):** The Beijing Zhongguancun Institute represents a top-down, government-supported model (Ministry of Education + Beijing Municipal Government) aiming to integrate education, science, and talent strategies. It focuses on breaking down barriers between universities, disciplines, and industry to produce "AI + X" interdisciplinary innovators.
*   **China (University/Student Level):** The commentary from Fudan, Peking, and Westlake Universities reflects a concern for individual cognitive autonomy. The perspective is that while the *system* should embrace AI, the *individual student* must maintain agency to avoid becoming a passive consumer of AI-generated outputs.
*   **Global Scientific Community:** The citation of the Fields Medalists' statement introduces a global expert perspective, indicating that concerns about AI disrupting fundamental research methodologies (specifically in mathematics) are not limited to pedagogical debates but extend to the integrity of high-level scientific discovery.

## Information Differences and Conflicts

**No direct conflicts were identified between the sources.**

However, a **thematic tension** exists that is worth noting:
*   **Source 1** celebrates AI as a powerful tool for *acceleration* (e.g., "R&D process sped up 4 times") and *creation* (e.g., students building surgical robots, starting companies).
*   **Source 2** cautions that this same acceleration and ease of access may lead to a loss of deep cognitive engagement, independent judgment, and the "struggle" necessary for genuine intellectual growth.

This is not a contradiction in facts but a dialectic in educational strategy: *how to harness AI's efficiency without sacrificing the human cognitive processes that lead to true innovation.*

## Known Current Impact

*   **Policy Implementation:** Beijing has integrated education, science, and talent leadership under a single working group (established in 2025), with the Zhongguancun Institute serving as a pilot.
*   **Curriculum Changes:** Institutions are shifting from standard course selection to project-based learning, where students identify knowledge gaps and learn accordingly.
*   **Student Behavior:** A significant majority of top-tier university freshmen (as seen in the Fudan survey) have already normalized AI tool usage for academic tasks.
*   **Entrepreneurial Activity:** Students are moving from seeking employment at large tech firms to launching their own startups during their doctoral studies, supported by institutional venture capital and incubation services.
*   **Early Education Reach:** AI talent cultivation is now extending to middle school students (age 14), indicating a long-term strategic pipeline.

## What Cannot Currently Be Determined

*   **Long-term Academic Outcomes:** While the Zhongguancun Institute reports short-term project outputs (120+ projects, 40 startups), the long-term impact on the quality of AI research or the career success of its graduates over decades is not yet available.
*   **Effectiveness of Counter-Measures:** It is unclear whether the warnings in Article #527 (about dependency and information cocoons) have led to specific, measurable policy changes or curriculum adjustments in universities beyond the rhetorical statements by presidents.
*   **Generalizability:** The Fudan University survey data applies only to its 2026 freshman cohort. It is not stated whether these usage habits are representative of all Chinese universities or specifically elite institutions.
*   **Fields Medalists' Influence:** While the statement by the 25 Fields Medalists is reported, the concrete response from the mathematical community or educational bodies to this specific warning is not detailed in the provided sources.

## Event Conclusion

The events reported on September 23, 2026, highlight a pivotal moment in Chinese higher education where AI adoption is moving from experimental to institutionalized. The Beijing Zhongguancun Institute serves as a concrete example of how universities are restructuring to leverage AI for interdisciplinary innovation and industry collaboration. Simultaneously, the editorial commentary underscores a critical反思 (reflection) on the psychological and cognitive costs of this integration. The consensus among educational leaders (Presidents of Peking, Fudan, Westlake Universities) and global experts (Fields Medalists) is that while AI is an indispensable tool for efficiency and data processing, the core mission of university education must remain the cultivation of independent judgment, curiosity, and the ability to ask unstructured questions—skills that AI currently cannot replicate. The challenge for the "AI Native Generation" is to master the tool without surrendering their intellectual agency.
