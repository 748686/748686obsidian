## Core Facts

### 1. Pseudoscience Governance and Science Popularization
*   **Context:** The reporting coincides with the "National Science Popularization Month," featuring activities such as science master drama performances in campuses and nationwide exhibitions in science venues.
*   **Nature of Pseudoscience:** The *People's Daily* commentary notes that pseudoscience mimics scientific外衣 (appearance). Specific cited examples include claims that fast charging in summer halves EV battery life, beer increases MSG production yield, UV light glowing on vegetables indicates pesticide residue, and quantum insoles can heal illnesses.
*   **Drivers:** The spread is driven by profit motives, specifically a gray industry chain involving content creation, traffic pushing, and e-commerce monetization. Health-related supplements and services are identified as high-profit zones.
*   **Technological Factor:** The article highlights that bad actors utilize AI to batch-generate pseudoscience content and use algorithms for precise targeting, complicating identification and governance.
*   **Legal/Regulatory Framework:**
    *   The 2024 revised *Science and Technology Popularization Law* mandates that popular science products and information must be legal and scientific, prohibiting false content.
    *   In 2025, multiple departments issued a notice regulating "self-media" medical science popularization, requiring qualifications and banning unqualified accounts from selling products.
    *   National "Clear and Bright" (Qinglang) campaigns are actively curbing the spread.
*   **Prescribed Actions:**
    *   **Platform Responsibility:** Platforms must move beyond pure traffic metrics, enforcing stricter creator qualification audits and optimizing algorithms to boost authoritative content.
    *   **Content Innovation:** Advocates for a shift from "preaching" to connecting with the public emotionally. Cites ophthalmologist Tao Yong's view of科普 (science popularization) as "dialogue between people" and vegetable expert Li Baoju's interactive short-video approach.
    *   **Public Vigilance:** Citizens are urged to identify red flags like "cure-all" or "emergency warning" terminology.
