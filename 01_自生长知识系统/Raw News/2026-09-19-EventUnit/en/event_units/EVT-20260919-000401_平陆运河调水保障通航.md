# Event Name

**Explanation of How 4 m³/s Additional Water Transfer Supports 5,000-Ton Vessel Navigation in the Pinglu Canal**

## Event Overview

On September 19, 2026, People's Daily published a detailed explanation addressing a reader inquiry regarding the water supply requirements for the newly navigable Pinglu Canal. The article clarifies that to support 5,000-ton vessels in accordance with Class I inland waterway standards, the canal requires a water flow of 24 m³/s. This demand is met by increasing the existing water transfer from the Yu River to the Qin River (Yin Yu Ru Qin) from its previous maximum of 20 m³/s to 24 m³/s, representing a net increase of 4 m³/s. The article emphasizes that this relatively small increment is sufficient due to the integration of "water-saving pool" innovations in the canal’s three large sluice gates, which recirculate over 60% of the water used, and a comprehensive basin-level water resource scheduling system.

## Core Facts

*   **Navigation Requirement:** The Pinglu Canal requires a water flow of approximately 24 m³/s to meet the water demand for 5,000-ton vessel navigation based on Class I inland waterway standards for the near term.
*   **Water Source Mechanism:** The water is supplied via the "Yin Yu Ru Qin" (Yu River to Qin River) water transfer project.
*   **Incremental Transfer:** The transfer volume is increased from the previous design maximum of 20 m³/s to 24 m³/s, resulting in a new baseline supplementary flow of 4 m³/s.
*   **Annual Transfer Volume:** The 24 m³/s flow rate equates to an annual total transfer of approximately 700 million cubic meters.
*   **Hydrological Context:** The Qin River basin has an annual runoff of approximately 2.03 billion cubic meters, but distribution is uneven, with 83% occurring in the flood season and only 17% in the dry season. The river system has short sources and weak storage capacity, making external water transfer necessary for maintaining minimum navigation depth.
*   **Water-Saving Technology:** The canal’s Majia and Qishi hubs utilize the world’s largest three-level stacked water-saving sluice gates. This technology stores water from upstream passages in pools via water conduits and recycles it for downstream passages, achieving over 60% water recycling. The three major hubs together save more than 1 billion cubic meters of water annually.
*   **Management Strategy:** The 24 m³/s allocation is supported by a unified scheduling system for reservoirs on the Yu River mainstream, which have a total regulation capacity of several billion cubic meters. The project adheres to ecological flow thresholds for the Xijiang mainstream to protect downstream regions like the Pearl River Delta.

## Event Conclusion

The 4 m³/s increase in water transfer is a targeted engineering solution that bridges the gap between the existing "Yin Yu Ru Qin" infrastructure (20 m³/s) and the navigational requirements (24 m³/s) of the Pinglu Canal. This increment is viable not through massive new infrastructure, but through a combination of:
1.  **Technological Innovation:** The use of stacked water-saving pools in the major sluice hubs, which recycle >60% of water.
2.  **Systemic Integration:** Leveraging the existing regulation capacity of Yu River reservoirs and integrating the canal into the broader regional water scheduling framework.

This approach exemplifies a "minimum resource consumption" philosophy, allowing the canal to function as a critical component of the Western Land-Sea Corridor while safeguarding ecological and downstream water rights. The event confirms the operational transition of the Pinglu Canal to support heavy freight (5,000 tons), supported by a scientifically managed water supply chain.
