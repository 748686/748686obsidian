# Event Name
**The "Green Horse" (Ma Ta Fei Yan / Bronze Galloping Horse) Cultural Creative IP Trend at the Gansu Provincial Museum**

## Event Overview
On September 16, 2026, *People's Daily* (19th Edition, "Consumption") published an article under the series "Trendy Life Among Cultural Relics," reporting on the rising popularity of the "Green Horse" ("Lü Ma") cultural and creative (cultural creativity) IP derived from the Gansu Provincial Museum's treasure, the Bronze Galloping Horse (commonly known as "Ma Ta Fei Yan" or "Horse Trampling a Flying Swallow"). The article details how the museum transformed the historic artifact into a series of plush, stylized toys and immersive spaces to appeal to younger consumers, turning the museum into a hub for cultural consumption and tourism. The event highlights a broader trend where traditional "old brands" and cultural relics are being revitalized through modern, relatable creative expressions to drive economic activity.

## Core Facts
*   **Artifact Origin & Characteristics:** The Bronze Galloping Horse (Tong Benma) was unearthed in 1969 from the Lei Tai Han Dynasty tomb in Wuwei, Gansu Province. It measures 34.5 cm in height, 45 cm in length, and 13.1 cm in width, weighing 7.3 kg. Its side profile was designated as the China Tourism Graphic Mark in 1983.
*   **The "Green Horse" IP:** The IP features a green, round-headed horse with two rows of white teeth, designed based on the front view of the artifact, which appears "dazed" or "cute" (歪头撇嘴), contrasting with its elegant side profile.
*   **Product Variants:** The IP includes standing poses named "Lü Ma Tiao Tiao" (Green Horse Jumping) and running poses named "Lü Ma Ben Ben" (Green Horse Galloping).
*   **Design Process:** The design team, composed primarily of post-90s and post-00s (born after 2000) young people, spent over six months refining the design. They chose plush materials to convey "relaxation" and "healing" emotions, adding interactive details like the horse kicking its hind leg and the bird looking offended.
*   **Commercial Performance:** In the year of the zodiac Horse (2026, Lunar Year), sales of "Green Horse" related products continued to rise. These products account for 40% of the total sales volume of the Gansu Provincial Museum's cultural and creative products, establishing it as the museum's "top-tier IP."
*   **Immersive Experience:** The museum launched an immersive theme space called "Green Home" (Lü Ma Zhi Jia), featuring bedroom, study, kitchen, and sports scenarios where consumers can interact with the IP and take photos, directly driving the sale of pillows, keychains, and fridge magnets.
*   **Key Personnel:**
    *   **Cui Youxin:** Head of the Gansu Provincial Museum's Cultural and Creative Center.
    *   **Wang Wei:** Cultural and Creative Designer at the Gansu Provincial Museum.
    *   **Zeng Yichen:** Reporter for *People's Daily*.
    *   **Huang Minjia:** A Beijing tourist who visited the museum after receiving a "Green Horse" doll.

## Event Conclusion
The "Green Horse" phenomenon represents a successful case study in cultural IP commercialization. By leveraging the inherent "meme-ability" of the Bronze Galloping Horse's front profile, the Gansu Provincial Museum has bridged the gap between 2,000-year-old history and modern digital-native consumerism. The use of plush materials, interactive design elements, and immersive spatial experiences has transformed the artifact from a static display object into a dynamic emotional companion, driving significant retail sales (40% of museum creative goods) and increasing museum foot traffic. This trend aligns with the broader "New National Trend" movement in China, where heritage is repackaged for contemporary lifestyle integration. The event is well-documented by a major national media source, though specific financial totals remain unverified beyond the percentage share.
