## Event Overview

On September 16, 2026, international news reports focused on two distinct developments: China's ecological achievements in combating desertification, presented during the 17th United Nations Convention to Combat Desertification (UNCCD COP17) in Ulaanbaatar, Mongolia, and Kazakhstan's strategic moves to regulate and expand its e-commerce sector. China highlighted its success in reversing land degradation and integrating ecological restoration with economic development, positioning itself as a global leader in this domain. Simultaneously, Kazakhstan announced new regulatory frameworks and infrastructure investments aimed at growing its e-commerce market share to 18.5% of retail by 2029, while deepening logistical partnerships with Chinese firms.

## Core Facts

*   **China's Desertification Control (COP17):**
    *   China showcased its achievements at the UN COP17 conference held in Ulaanbaatar, Mongolia.
    *   A dedicated "China Corner" exhibition was highlighted, drawing attention from African and Central Asian officials and scientists.
    *   China is reported to have achieved zero net land degradation globally and reduced its desertified and sandy land.
    *   Forest coverage and timber volume have maintained a continuous 40-year dual increase.
    *   Key strategies included the Third-North Shelter Forest system, grain-for-green initiatives, and land enclosure and restoration.
    *   Technological innovations cited include water-retaining agents (absorbing >1000x their weight in water), drone sowing, satellite remote sensing, and drought-tolerant species breeding.
    *   The Kubuqi Desert's "solar power above, crop planting below, and livestock rearing in between" model was cited as an example of economic-ecological synergy.
*   **Global Desertification Context:**
    *   Approximately 40% of the world's land suffers from degradation.
    *   This affects nearly half of the global population.
    *   Annual global economic losses from land degradation, desertification, and drought exceed 870 billion USD.
*   **Kazakhstan E-Commerce:**
    *   Kazakhstan's Ministry of Trade and Integration released a catalog of prohibited and restricted goods for e-commerce platforms.
    *   E-commerce transaction volume increased from 4.76 trillion tenge in 2020 to 3.9 trillion tenge in 2025.
        *   *Note: There is a significant numerical discrepancy in the source text regarding this growth trajectory (see "Information Differences and Conflicts").*
    *   E-commerce currently represents 15% of total retail consumer spending in Kazakhstan.
    *   The government aims to expand this share to 18.5% by 2029.
    *   Infrastructure includes 10 logistics centers and a planned $200 million, 291,000-square-meter order processing hub in Astana and Almaty.
    *   Partnerships with Chinese firms (YTO Express, JD.com) are mentioned.
    *   Internet penetration in Kazakhstan is cited as 92.9%.
