*   **Key Literary Figures Discussed:**
    *   **Wang Zengqi:** Known for writing about his hometown. Left his home at age 19 in 1939; returned only twice in 42 years. Initially viewed returning to the rural area as restricting and "sour" (霉下来). His deep nostalgia emerged around age 60 (post-1987), particularly after interactions with Black American scholars in Iowa who expressed a lack of ancestral roots. He later wrote *I am a Chinese Person* and *The Influence of Traditional Culture on Contemporary Chinese Literary Creation*.
    *   **Shen Congwen:** Wang Zengqi's teacher. Identified as a "countryman" (乡下人). Wrote *Border Town* expressing longing, and *The Long River* reflecting anxiety and wistfulness upon returning to Xiangxi.
    *   **Lu Xun:** Wrote critical works like *Diary of a Madman* and *Blessing*, but also nostalgic pieces like *From Baicaoyuan to Sanwei Shuwu* and *The Fair*.
    *   **Xiao Hong:** Depicted the harsh reality of the Black Earth land ("busy living, busy dying") while also mourning her grandfather, the big elm tree, and the back garden.
    *   **William Faulkner:** Quote provided regarding his complex love-hate relationship with his Southern hometown.
    *   **Wang Meng:** Ancestral home in Hebei; raised in Beijing; identified Xinjiang Yili (Bayandao) as his spiritual hometown.
    *   **Shi Tiesheng:** Raised in Beijing; remained attached to Qingping Bay in Northern Shaanxi.
*   **Key Conceptual Argument:** The "spiritual hometown" often feels more "real" than the physical one. The bond of blood and land ("bloodland") is described as an inborn, fated connection that provides the baseline of "love," even if mixed with criticism.
*   **Specific Incident Mentioned:** In 1987, Wang Zengqi visited Iowa, USA, and conversed with two Black American scholars. Their inability to trace their ancestry beyond their great-grandfathers, and the lack of acknowledgment from African nations, led Wang to realize the importance of "roots," national culture, and tradition.
