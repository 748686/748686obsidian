# EVT-20261009-000446: France Commemorates World War II Shanghai Refugees' Protector

## Event Overview
In early September and October 2026, a series of commemorative events were held in France to honor Jiashu Rao (Robert Jacquinot de Besange), a French humanitarian who played a pivotal role in protecting Chinese civilians during the Second World War. The activities, supported by the Sino-Friendship Groups of the French National Assembly and Senate, included an exhibition at the Paris Chinese Cultural Center, the unveiling of a memorial statue in Sangatte, the screening of a documentary, and the launch of a new historical monograph. These events aim to raise public awareness of Rao’s legacy and the broader history of the Second World War in Asia, while drawing parallels to contemporary global challenges regarding peace and humanitarian protection.

## Core Facts

**1. Commemorative Activities in France**
*   **Exhibition:** The "Rao Jiashu—Great Warrior for Peace" biography exhibition was held at the Paris Chinese Cultural Center. It was supported by the Sino-Friendship Groups of the French National Assembly and Senate. The exhibition introduced Rao’s humanitarian practices to the French public.
*   **Statue Unveiling:** In September 2026, a two-meter-tall bronze statue of Rao Jiashu was unveiled in a public garden in Sangatte, a city in southwest France. This marks the 80th anniversary of his death. The statue was created by Chinese sculptor Li Xiaochao and donated by the Shanghai Research Society for the War of Resistance Against Japan and the World Anti-Fascist War, on behalf of descendants of Chinese civilians who were sheltered by Rao.
*   **Documentary Screening:** The Sangatte Cultural Center screened the documentary *"The Good Man of Shanghai: Rao Jiashu"* (French: *Shanghai Haoren Rao Jiashu*), produced by local director Sebastien Casen.
*   **Book Launch:** A reader meetup was held for the new book *Rao Jiashu: The Forgotten Pioneer of International Humanitarian Aid* by French historian Jean-Luc Pinol.

**2. Historical Background of Rao Jiashu**
*   **Early Life:** Rao Jiashu (Robert Jacquinot de Besange) was born in 1878 in Sangatte into a military family.
*   **Career in Shanghai:** He arrived in Shanghai in 1913, where he taught at Xuhui Middle School and Aurora University. He lived in China for over 20 years, actively participating in social welfare and engaging deeply with Chinese society.
*   **Nanshi Safety Zone:** Following the outbreak of the Battle of Songhu (淞沪会战) in 1937, which caused mass displacement of civilians, Rao, then Vice Chairman of the International Red Cross in Shanghai, advocated for and helped establish the "Nanshi Safety Zone" (南市安全区). This zone sheltered hundreds of thousands of civilians.
*   **Legacy:** His wartime practices for civilian protection are cited as significant precursors to the development of international humanitarian law and wartime civilian protection systems.

**3. Key Figures and Statements**
*   **Bo Xu (徐波):** Chairman of the Wu Jianmin Friends Association (France). Stated that Rao’s story allows people to "draw strength from history and guard the future of peace" in a world increasingly dominated by geopolitics.
*   **Delapogne (Mayor of Sangatte):** Noted that Rao left an indelible mark on Shanghai and expressed hope that the statue and future cultural/educational activities would help more people learn about him.
*   **Laurent Fabius (Former Prime Minister of France):** Emphasized that "peace does not fall from the sky; peace must be built." He highlighted the special responsibility of France and China as permanent members of the UN Security Council and called for strengthened dialogue and multilateralism to promote a balanced global governance system that respects different civilizations and the Global South.
*   **Sebastien Casen (Director):** Spent approximately seven years (starting in 2013) researching and producing the documentary across China and France. He noted that the Second World War in Asia has long been overlooked by European audiences and stressed the importance of understanding the plight of civilians in war to appreciate the意义 of safety zones.
*   **Jean-Luc Pinol (Historian):** Has researched Rao for nearly 10 years, beginning during a teaching exchange at East China Normal University in Shanghai. He described Rao’s refugee rescue efforts as an active choice for justice and kindness even in desperate circumstances.
*   **Gabriel (Student):** A Chinese-class student at Palissy High School in Sangatte, who stated that Rao’s story sparked his interest in Chinese history, including the Battle of Songhu, and expressed a desire to study in China.
