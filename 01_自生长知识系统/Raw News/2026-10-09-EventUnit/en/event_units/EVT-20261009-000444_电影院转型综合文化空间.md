# Event: Movie Theaters Transform into Comprehensive Cultural Spaces

## Event Overview

In 2025-2026, the Chinese movie theater industry underwent a significant strategic transformation from single-purpose exhibition venues into diversified cultural and social complexes. This shift was driven by declining traditional box office revenue, the rise of online video platforms and micro-dramas, and the inherently "tidal" nature of film exhibition (concentrated in spring and summer holidays with low weekday daytime occupancy). In June 2026, the National Film Administration and the State Administration for Market Regulation jointly issued the *Notice on Promoting Diverse Operations and Prospering Theater Culture*, explicitly encouraging theaters to become integrated spaces for viewing, socializing, entertainment, and consumption.

## Core Facts

**Industry Context and Policy Support**
*   **Regulatory Framework:** In June 2026, the National Film Administration and the State Administration for Market Regulation issued the *Notice on Promoting Diverse Operations and Prospering Theater Culture*. The notice calls for innovation in formats, models, and services to create comprehensive cultural experience spaces. It also clarified that market regulation departments should support cinemas in obtaining necessary business licenses, such as food service permits, to facilitate diverse operations.
*   **Economic Performance:** According to calculations by the National Film Administration, China's film industry chain reached a total output value of **817.259 billion yuan** in 2025. The box office stimulation coefficient is approximately **1:15.77**, meaning every 1 yuan of box office revenue drives 15.77 yuan in related downstream industries.
*   **Extraterritorial Value:** The extraterritorial value driven by and related to the film industry reached **339.095 billion yuan** in 2025, covering catering, transportation, retail, tourism in filming locations, and IP derivatives.

**The Decline of Traditional Models**
*   **Structural Problems:** The industry faces a structural decline in traditional box office revenue. High-grossing films are concentrated in specific seasons, and the prime viewing window is limited to 2-3 hours in the evening. Weekday daytime occupancy remains low.
*   **Competition:** The rise of online film/TV platforms and micro-dramas has diverted traditional viewing demand.
*   **Failed Strategies:** Some diversification attempts failed. For instance, Beijing Capital Cinema (Sub-center Store) built "flight halls" and "light and shadow halls" with immersive digital products in 2024, but these failed to attract local audiences due to high production costs and slow content updates. Similarly, Wuhan Yinxing International Cinema (Optics Valley Fashion City Store) previously introduced gaming consoles and massage chairs, which lacked independent attraction capabilities.

**Successful Transformation Models**
*   **Hangzhou Zheyin Times Cinema (West Lake Cultural Plaza Store):** Transformed in 2022 into a cultural complex based on the principles of "layered positioning, customer group matching, functional complementarity, and traffic interoperability." By 2025, it became the top-grossing single theater in Zhejiang with over **31.6 million yuan** in box office revenue. It hosts immersive performances, concerts, and comedy shows; hosted nearly 50 star meet-and-greets and roadshows in the past year, and over 100 IP-themed exhibitions. Its highest monthly foot traffic reached **100,000 people**.
*   **Starlight Jiaying Cinema (Raffles City Store, Hangzhou):** Located in a central business district catering to young office workers, this theater introduced high-quality dining (steak) and coffee, allowing patrons to dine while watching films. This addressed the lack of independent traffic in non-prime hours. In one year, catering revenue reached approximately **3 million yuan** against a box office of **9 million yuan**. Additionally, they widened seat row spacing from 1.2 meters to over 1.4 meters to improve comfort.
*   **Wuhan Wushang Dream Times Moore Cinema:** Opened in April 2023, featuring large IMAX screens capable of presenting 3D and floating image effects. In August 2025, these premium hall revenues accounted for **37%** of the total cinema revenue.
*   **Beijing Capital Cinema (Zhonghua Store):** Focused on traditional Peking Opera films, leveraging its location in the Tianqiao Performing Arts District and its history as the 1933-established Deshengxuan Theater. It hosts weekly opera film screenings (e.g., *The Legend of the Dragon and Phoenix*, *Generals Gathering Forces*).
*   **Ningbo Mingguang Cinema:** Preserved its retro style (originally the 1931 Minyuan Grand Theater) and focuses on art-house and emotional films to evoke city memories.
*   **Beijing Yaozhu Dacheng Cinema (Wukesong Store):** Held a special exhibition of "Family Letters in Qiaopi" (historical correspondence) in collaboration with the Jiangmen Museum during the screening of the film *Letter to My Grandmother*, creating an immersive pre-show experience.

**Integration with Urban Consumption ("Ticket Stub Economy")**
*   **Zhejiang "Chao Guang Ying" Mini Program:** Launched by Zhijiang Film Group in July 2026, linking cinema tickets with consumption discounts across 11 cities. It partnered with over 1,000 brands and 10,000 merchants. By late September, it had collected over **103,000 valid ticket stubs**, completed **24,000** redemption transactions (discounts/free entry), and drove nearly **20 million yuan** in related consumption.
*   **Beijing:** Leveraged the 38th Golden Rooster and Hundred Flowers Film Festival to offer discounts at over **1,100 merchants** in more than **40 commercial districts**.
*   **Hubei:** Launched a digital platform called "One Ticket Benefits Hubei" offering coupon packages for dining, shopping, accommodation, and tourism.

## Different Country / Regional Perspectives

**Beijing**
*   Approaches focus on niche cultural themes (Peking Opera at Zhonghua Store) and immersive/tech-forward installations (though the latter faced challenges at the Sub-center store). The city also integrates cinema with major events (Golden Rooster/Hundred Flowers) and broad urban commerce.

**Zhejiang (Hangzhou, Ningbo)**
*   Hangzhou is at the forefront of the "comprehensive cultural space" model, integrating VR, bookstores, and high-end dining. The "Chao Guang Ying" digital platform creates a provincial-level integration of cinema and tourism.
*   Ningbo focuses on historical preservation and emotional connection, maintaining a retro 1930s aesthetic.

**Hubei (Wuhan)**
*   Wuhan demonstrates the vulnerability of university-area cinemas to seasonal fluctuations (low summer occupancy). It also serves as a testbed for high-tech hardware upgrades, such as the massive 14-screen, 2,700-seat capacity complex with 3D floating image technology.

**Guizhou (Guiyang)**
*   Represents the strategy of competing purely on premium audiovisual hardware (IMAX GT), attracting cross-provincial tourism for blockbuster releases.

## Known Current Impact

*   **Shift in Revenue Models:** The industry is moving from relying solely on box office sales to a multi-stream revenue model including dining, merchandise, events, and venue rental.
*   **Extended Consumer Time:** Successful theaters have increased average customer dwell time significantly (e.g., from 2 hours to longer stays due to VR, exhibitions, and dining).
*   **Urban Integration:** Cinemas are becoming "living rooms" for cities, embedded in broader tourism and commercial ecosystems (the "ticket stub economy").
*   **Operational Requirements:** There is an increasing demand for "versatile" staff who can manage ticketing, event security, market operations, and放映 (projection) simultaneously.

## What Cannot Currently Be Determined

*   **Long-term Sustainability:** While 2025 data is positive for specific transformed venues, it is unclear if this trend is sustainable across the entire industry or limited to high-traffic urban centers.
*   **Standardization of "Ticket Stub Economy":** It is unknown if the Zhejiang "Chao Guang Ying" model will be successfully replicated nationally or if it remains a regional pilot.
*   **Profitability of Hardware Upgrades:** While the Guiyang IMAX GT example is successful, the high cost of such renovations makes it inaccessible for smaller theaters.

## Event Conclusion

The Chinese movie theater industry is executing a strategic pivot from "selling tickets" to "selling scenes" and "operating foot traffic." Driven by regulatory support and market pressures, successful theaters are differentiating themselves through localized diversification—ranging from high-end dining and VR experiences to cultural thematic preservation. The future of the sector lies not in scale expansion, but in integrating with urban life to become comprehensive cultural nodes that extend the economic value of a single movie ticket across the wider consumption chain.
