## Core Facts

**The Bangka Island Massacre (1942)**
*   **Context:** Following the fall of Singapore in February 1942, 65 Australian military nurses evacuated aboard the ship *Vyner Brooke*.
*   **Incident:** The ship was bombed and sunk by Japanese aircraft in the Bangka Strait.
*   **Surrender:** Survivors drifted at sea for several days before landing at Rendang Beach on Bangka Island (Indonesia), where they surrendered to Japanese forces.
*   **Casualties:** Japanese troops executed 22 unarmed Australian nurses by machine gun fire. Only one nurse, Vivian Bullwinkel, survived the shooting.
*   **Post-Capture:** Vivian Bullwinkel was imprisoned in a POW camp for three and a half years until the end of the war. She subsequently served as a key witness at the International Military Tribunal for the Far East (IMTFE).

**The Stage Play *The Twenty-One Hearts***
*   **Premiere:** 2024.
*   **2026 Tour:** Completed a nationwide tour across Australia in 2026.
*   **Performance Data:** As of October 2026, the play has been performed over 70 times since its premiere. Multiple performances sold out in advance, resulting in multiple encore encores.
*   **Significance:** The title derives from the 21 nurses who died in the massacre (alongside the single survivor).

**Historical and Legal Context (Reported)**
*   **Tokyo Trials (1946):** Conducted under the *Charter of the International Military Tribunal for the Far East*. An 11-nation bench tried 28 Class-A war criminals.
*   **Australian War Crimes Trials (1945–1951):**
    *   Australia conducted approximately 300 trials against Japanese Class-B and Class-C war criminals.
    *   Over 900 Japanese war criminal suspects were charged.
    *   William Webb, an Australian judge who presided over the Tokyo Trials, previously led systematic investigations into Japanese war crimes.
*   **Archival Evidence:** The Australian National Archives holds a 400+ page retrieval guide titled *Japanese War Crimes in the Pacific: Australian Investigations and Prosecutions*. *   **Political Statement:** In early 1944, Australian Minister for External Affairs and Attorney-General Herbert Evatt cited investigation conclusions describing the records as full of "crimes and barbarity," characterizing them as an indictment of the entire Japanese military management system.
