## Event Overview

This event synthesizes information regarding Ernest Hemingway’s residence in Key West (also translated as "West Key West" or "Cape Florida Island"), Florida, and its current status as a cultural heritage site. The source material is a travel essay published in the *People's Daily* on October 9, 2026. It details the history of the house, Hemingway’s creative output during his residency, the preservation efforts, and the associated cultural traditions such as the "Sloppy Joe Bar" and the annual "Best Dressed Hemingway Lookalike Contest."

## Core Facts

**Location and Property**
*   **Location:** Key West, Florida, the southernmost point of the Florida Keys, a tropical island with an area of approximately 11 square kilometers [Source 598].
*   **Address:** 907 Whitehead Street [Source 598].
*   **Building History:** The house was built in 1851 and is one of the largest single-family residences in Key West. It features white, yellow, and green exterior walls, surrounded by a palm courtyard and garden [Source 598].
*   **Museum Status:** Opened as a museum in 1964 and designated an American National Historical Landmark in 1968 [Source 598].

**Hemingway’s Residency**
*   **Period:** Hemingway lived in Key West from 1928 to 1939 [Source 598].
*   **Move-in Date:** He moved into the Whitehead Street house in 1931 from a rental property [Source 598].
*   **Departure:** He moved to Cuba in 1939 [Source 598].
*   **Ownership:** After his death, his heirs sold the house [Source 598].

**Creative Output**
*   Hemingway maintained a disciplined routine, writing in the morning in a second-story writing room [Source 598].
*   **Major Works Created/Developed:**
    *   Novels: *Death in the Afternoon*, *Green Hills of Africa*, *Winner Takes Nothing*, *For Whom the Bell Tolls* [Source 598].
    *   Short Stories: *The Snows of Kilimanjaro*, *The Short Happy Life of Francis Macomber* [Source 598].
    *   Essay: *Blue Mule: Dispatches from the Gulf Stream* (1936), which contained the preliminary concept for *The Old Man and the Sea* (1952) [Source 598].
*   **Thematic Focus:** His works from this period focused on life-limiting scenarios such as bullfighting, hunting, and deep-sea fishing, exploring themes of death, courage, and disillusionment. These works are described as bridging his earlier and later styles [Source 598].

**Interior and Artifacts**
*   The house contains antique furniture transported from Paris, 17th-century Spanish-style cabinets, and Hemingway’s personal weight scale [Source 598].
*   The second-floor writing room retains its original appearance, featuring a vintage Royal typewriter and Cuban cigars [Source 598].

**Cultural Legacy and Tourism**
*   **Cats:** Hemingway kept up to 30+ cats. Descendants of these cats still roam the grounds; the oldest reported is 16 years old [Source 598].
*   **Sloppy Joe Bar:** Founded in 1933 on Duval Street, this bar was a frequent social spot for Hemingway. It displays his portraits and personal items. It is known for hosting literary figures such as John Dos Passos, Waldo Pierce, and Captain Eddie Sonne [Source 598].
*   **Annual Contest:** Since 1981, Key West holds a "Best Dressed Hemingway Lookalike Contest" every July. As of 2026, the event is in its 45th year [Source 598]. Winners’ photos are displayed at Sloppy Joe Bar [Source 598].
