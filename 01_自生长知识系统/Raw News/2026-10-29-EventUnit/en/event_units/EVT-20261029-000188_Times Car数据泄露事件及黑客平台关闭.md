# Event Name

## Event Overview

This EventUnit synthesizes two distinct data breaches reported on 2026-10-29. The first involves the leak of over 1.4 million user records from Times Car, a Korean car rental service, resulting from an SQL injection vulnerability. The second concerns the shutdown of a leaked information trading platform owned by GMO, which was used by hackers to sell data from various compromised entities. These events are analyzed as separate incidents under a single synthesis header due to their shared context of data leakage and hacker activity in South Korea, though they involve different actors, platforms, and specific facts.

## Core Facts

### 1. Times Car Data Breach
*   **Incident:** A data breach affecting Times Car, a Korean car rental service.
*   **Cause:** An SQL injection vulnerability in Times Car's systems.
*   **Data Compromised:** Approximately 1.476 million customer records (out of a total user base of 1.563 million).
*   **Information Leaked:** Names, phone numbers, and credit card expiration dates. Credit card numbers and security codes were **not** compromised.
*   **Attack Vector:** The data was obtained via SQL injection attacks, with approximately 870,000 records hacked in January 2026 and another 600,000 in March 2026.
*   **Impact:** Affected users may receive "unauthorized application requests" and need to contact their credit card companies.
*   **Response:** Times Car has established a support center and is providing compensation for damage claims.
*   **Legal Action:** Times Car has filed a police complaint against the hacker responsible for the breach.

### 2. GMO Leaked Information Trading Platform Shutdown
*   **Entity:** A leaked information trading platform operated by GMO (GMO Information Security).
*   **Action:** The platform was shut down on 2026-10-29 after being exposed as a primary marketplace for stolen data.
*   **Scale of Operation:** The platform, named after a mythical creature representing chaos, operated for over two months. It had 5,000 registered users and recorded 7,000 transactions, generating approximately 3 billion KRW ($21 million) in revenue.
*   **Data Sold:** Approximately 368 billion bytes (368 GB) of leaked information, including personal data from government agencies, military units, private companies, and educational institutions.
*   **Victims Mentioned:** The Seoul Metropolitan Fire Headquarters, South Korea's defense agency, and an unnamed national military unit were among those affected.
*   **Investigation:** The Korean National Police Agency (KNPA) identified GMO as the operator. GMO confirmed the shutdown and stated it had reported the incident to authorities. Law enforcement is pursuing legal action against GMO and its employees.
