## Event Overview

This EventUnit synthesizes reports regarding the ongoing Ebola virus disease outbreak in Central and East Africa, as of late October 2026. The primary focus is on the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC), which is experiencing a massive epidemic driven by the Bundibugyo virus variant. Key developments include the escalation of cases in the DRC, the spread of the virus to neighboring Kenya and Nigeria (input cases), and coordinated cross-border response efforts involving the Africa Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (Africa CDC) and the World Health Organization (WHO).

## Core Facts

**Epidemic Scale and Status in DRC**
*   As of October 25, 2026, the DRC reported 10,060 confirmed Ebola cases and 4,887 deaths, resulting in a case fatality rate of 48.6% (Source: Article #573).
*   The outbreak, declared in May 2026, is characterized as the "second-largest Ebola epidemic on record" by the WHO (Source: Article #573).
*   The pathogen is identified as the Bendiibugyo (Bundibugyo) serotype of the Ebola virus (Source: Article #573).
*   The virus has spread to 8 out of the DRC's 26 provinces (Source: Article #573).
*   The epidemic curve showed peaks in late July and mid-August 2026, a decline in late September, and a resurgence in early October 2026 (Source: Article #573).

**Vaccine and Treatment Status**
*   There are currently no approved vaccines or specific therapeutic treatments for the Bendiibugyo serotype (Source: Article #573).
*   Control measures rely on early case detection, isolation, contact tracing, and community mobilization (Source: Article #573).
*   On October 23, 2026, Phase II/III clinical trials for a candidate vaccine against this serotype began in Bunia, DRC, with initial participants enrolled (Source: Article #573).

**Cross-Border Spread**
*   **Kenya:** Reported one new case of the Bendiibugyo variant on October 20, 2026. As of October 26, Kenya had a total of 9 cases, all linked to the same transmission chain, with 2 deaths and 7 patients in stable condition (Source: Article #573). Contact tracing identified 118 individuals requiring monitoring; the first batch of 20 contacts was released from isolation after 21 days on October 26 (Source: Article #573).
*   **Nigeria:** Reported one imported case on October 20, 2026, making Nigeria the fourth African country to report cases. The patient, a woman arriving from Uganda, was isolated in Abuja (Source: Article #573). The WHO warned that the delay between symptom onset and diagnosis increases the risk of undetected community transmission (Source: Article #573).
*   **Uganda:** While not reporting new cases in the provided text, Uganda is cited as the origin of the Nigerian input case. Uganda resumed mandatory traveler information registration on October 23 to facilitate contact tracing (Source: Article #573).

**International and Regional Response**
*   On October 28, 2026, the Africa CDC convened a high-level video conference of African Ministers of Health to assess the situation and strengthen coordination (Source: Article #573).
*   The DRC and South Sudan agreed to cross-border health monitoring, including shared information, joint investigations, and border controls (Source: Article #573).
*   Tanzania and Ethiopia have enhanced health screenings at air and land ports (Source: Article #573).
*   The WHO has designated Djibouti, Somalia, and Sudan as priority support countries for capacity building (Source: Article #573).

## Event Conclusion

The Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of the Congo, caused by the Bendiibugyo serotype, represents a critical public health emergency in Central Africa as of October 2026. With over 10,000 cases and nearly 5,000 deaths, it stands as the second-largest Ebola epidemic on record. The virus has crossed international borders into Kenya and Nigeria, triggering regional alarm and coordinated response efforts led by the Africa CDC and WHO. While no specific treatment or licensed vaccine exists for this serotype, clinical trials for a candidate vaccine have begun in the DRC. The response faces significant challenges due to the security situation in the DRC and the need for rigorous cross-border contact tracing and surveillance.
