## Core Facts

### Urban Management: Pocket Parks and "Rebuilding over Maintaining"
*   **Source:** Reader Chen Jiaye (Qingdao, Shandong).
*   **Issue:** Despite the expansion of "pocket parks" (green spaces created from vacant urban corners), many suffer from "rebuilding over maintaining" (重建轻管).
*   **Observed Problems:** Fitness equipment parts detached and unrepaired for over half a year; noise pollution from广场舞 (square dancing) groups occupying parks for extended periods; remote locations turning into hygiene blind spots.
*   **Suggestions:**
    1.  Clarify management responsibility: Issue specific management measures to define the responsible主体 (entity) for maintenance, cleaning, and order, resolving jurisdictional disputes among landscaping departments, sub-district offices, and community committees.
    2.  Ensure fiscal guarantee: Include maintenance funds in annual fiscal budgets and explore diversified financing channels involving enterprises and公益 organizations.
    3.  Guide resident participation: Implement a "Citizen Park Director" volunteer system and QR code feedback mechanisms for rapid reporting of facility damage or hygiene issues.

### Fire Safety: Construction Waste Blockage
*   **Source:** Unspecified reader submission (reported under "Around Us").
*   **Issue:** Construction waste piled at building entrances is blocking fire channels in residential communities.

### Rural Infrastructure: Farmland Production Roads
*   **Source:** Netizen on People's Daily Online "Leader Message Board."
*   **Issue:** Farmers are gradually encroaching on roadside and ridge areas to expand planting boundaries, narrowing production roads.
*   **Consequences:**
    *   Inconvenience for large-scale agricultural machinery (harvesters, rototillers).
    *   Poor drainage leading to muddy, slippery conditions in rainy weather.
    *   Blocked emergency channels for fire trucks or irrigation equipment during droughts/fires.
    *   Increased neighborhood disputes.
*   **Suggestions:**
    1.  Unified确权 (confirmation of rights): Use land registration archives to remeasure and mark road boundaries with lime or stakes.
    2.  Clarify ownership: Emphasize that production roads are collective public land, not private contracted fields.
    3.  Concentrated clearance: Village committees and grid workers should catalog and persuade farmers to remove crops occupying roads.
    4.  Propaganda and guidance: Use village meetings and WeChat groups to explain the public interest in maintaining road width.

### Education: Inclusive Education for Special Needs Children
*   **Source:** Reporting by Ding Yasong (*People's Daily*); commentary by experts Ding Meizhen and Zheng Xuan.
*   **Legal Context:** The Compulsory Education Law mandates that ordinary schools accept disabled school-age children who can receive ordinary education.
*   **Problem:** "随班就读" (learning in regular classrooms) faces significant obstacles due to insufficient resources.
    *   *Case:* A parent in Tongliao, Inner Mongolia, reported that schools were reluctant to accept his child, who has attention deficits but can read and calculate, despite retaining the student's registration status.
    *   *Conflict:* A case in Nanchang, Jiangxi, saw parents collectively demanding the removal of a student with behavioral disorders who disrupted class, highlighting the tension between inclusive rights and classroom order.
*   **Expert Analysis (Ding Meizhen, Shanghai Hongkou Special Education Guidance Center):**
    *   Schools cannot deny education due to lack of resources nor use long-term suspension as a solution.
    *   Severe behavioral issues require on-site safety protection and short-term isolation/regulation first.
    *   Placement adjustments must follow professional assessment processes involving multiple parties.
*   **Suggested Solutions:**
    *   **"Itinerant Special Ed Teacher + Regional Resource Center":** District/county-level coordination of professionals to visit multiple schools, reducing the burden on individual schools lacking full-time special ed staff.
    *   **Government-purchased services:** Introduce rehabilitation therapists and special ed teaching assistants.
    *   **Parent Accompaniment:** Regulated as a temporary "scaffold" for school adaptation, not a long-term norm. Requires standardized assessment and written agreements on goals and exit strategies.
    *   **Assessment Reform:** Evaluations must consider whether the school provides adequate support, not just the child's capacity. Placement decisions must be made by interdisciplinary teams (special ed, rehab, psychology, regular ed) commissioned by the county education department, not unilaterally by schools.
    *   **Teacher Training:** Integrate inclusive education content into mandatory continuing education for ordinary teachers.
