G20 trade ministers deadlocked over industrial overcapacity, says US trade chief

Economy

G20 trade ministers failed to agree on tackling excess industrial capacity and eliminating forced labour from supply chains, US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer said Thursday. Speaking during talks in Milwaukee, Greer highlighted overcapacity, a criticism often directed at China, which rejects it. The United States holds this year's rotating presidency.

US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer speaks during a press conference, Oct. 1, 2026, G20 Trade Ministerial, Milwaukee © Kayla Wolf, AP

G20 trade ministers are at an impasse on countering excess industrial capacity, top US trade official Jamieson Greer said Thursday, an accusation often levied at

China

which rejects the criticism.

His comments came as trade ministers from the Group of 20 major economies met for a second day in the Midwestern US city of Milwaukee. The United States holds the

G20

's rotating presidency this year.

Ministers failed to find agreement across the grouping on overcapacity, seen as driving down prices unfairly, and on eliminating forced labor in supply chains, Greer said.

"Nearly all countries agreed that this is an issue that requires action," Greer told a press conference, referring to excess capacity. He did not respond to questions on whether this was due to China's objection.

"And nearly all countries agreed that our current system of trade remedies or responses is inadequate to solve this problem," he said. He added that this justified unilateral US action.

There are US investigations into 16 economies on overcapacity, targeting China, the

European Union

,

Japan

,

India

and others.

Greer warned that countries which overproduce risk "creating an

economy

that rests on a house of cards."

Trump also recently slapped

tariffs
