# Event Name
Transformation of Art-Driven Rural Construction from Visual Beautification to Empowerment

## Event Overview
On September 13, 2026, *People's Daily* published an article discussing the evolving practice of art-driven rural construction ("art village building") in China. The core argument is that this field is shifting from external "delisting beauty to the countryside" (visual beautification by outsiders) to "cultivating beauty in the countryside" (empowering villagers with creative and aesthetic capabilities). The article highlights specific case studies in Hubei and Zhejiang provinces, identifying current deficiencies such as over-reliance on visual aesthetics, lack of sustainable operation, and the use of generic "internet-famous" templates that fail to激发 local vitality.

## Core Facts
*   **Publication Date:** September 13, 2026.
*   **Source Medium:** *People's Daily*, Page 08 (Supplement/Arts Section), Article ID 30180701.
*   **Author:** Tian Xue.
*   **Case Study 1:** In Xiongfancun Village, Dawu County, Hubei Province, an idle rural primary school was transformed by the Aesthetic Education Team from China Agricultural University into a "cultural living room" integrating fine arts teaching and pottery experience.
*   **Case Study 2:** In Geshucun Village, Ninghai County, Zhejiang Province, art teams guided villagers to independently design cultural creative products and shared spaces, turning them into "indispensable" (non-departable) rural construction artists.
*   **Identified Problems:** Some practices prioritize visual beautification over internal empowerment; others prioritize the completion of public spaces over long-term operational sustainability; some apply generic "influencer" templates, failing to stimulate local vitality.
*   **Proposed Direction:** The goal is to leave the "ability to create beauty" in the countryside, focusing on "human construction" (empathy, cultural identity, and participation) rather than just physical transformation.

## Event Conclusion
The event represents a significant intellectual and practical shift in China's rural development strategy. While visual arts have been widely used for rural beautification, the 2026 discourse, as articulated in *People's Daily*, argues that this approach is insufficient. The emerging standard for success is no longer the physical appearance of the village, but the retention of **creative and aesthetic agency within the local community**. The cited cases from Hubei and Zhejiang serve as proof-of-concept for this "human-centered" model, where university partnerships and guided autonomy allow villagers to become the primary agents of their cultural renewal. This marks a maturation of the "Art in Rural" field from a top-down decorative project to a bottom-up empowerment process, though challenges regarding sustainability and avoidance of superficial templates remain active areas of critique.
