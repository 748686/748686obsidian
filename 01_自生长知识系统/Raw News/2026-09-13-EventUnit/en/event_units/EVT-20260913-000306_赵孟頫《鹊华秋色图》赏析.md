## Event Overview

On September 13, 2026, *People's Daily* (paper.people.com.cn) published a cultural commentary article titled "Mist-Shrouded Ridges Entering the Scroll, Autumn Colors Entering the Heart (Reading Painting)" on its 8th page (Supplement). Authored by Yin Dan, a professor at the Sichuan Fine Arts Institute, the piece provides an in-depth artistic analysis of Zhao Mengfu’s *Yuan Dynasty* masterpiece, *Autumn Colors on the Magpie and Hua Mountains* (*Que Hua Qiu Se Tu*). The analysis explores the painting's historical context, its role as a model for "reviving the old to open the new" and "integrating calligraphy into painting," and its unique spatial composition techniques that blend Tang Dynasty realism with Neo-Confucian aesthetics.

## Core Facts

*   **Artwork Identity**: *Autumn Colors on the Magpie and Hua Mountains* (*Que Hua Qiu Se Tu*) by Zhao Mengfu.
*   **Creation Date**: Created in the first year of the Yuan Zhen era (1295).
*   **Commissioning Context**: The mainstream scholarly view reported in the source is that Zhao Mengfu painted the work to comfort his friend Zhou Mi, who had ancestral ties to Jinan, Shandong Province, but had never visited his hometown, suffering from persistent homesickness.
*   **Subject Matter**: The painting depicts Queshan (Magpie Mountain) and Hua Bushuzhu Shan (Flower Bud Mountain) along with surrounding waters, painted from Zhao Mengfu's memory during his tenure in Jinan.
*   **Artistic Style**: The work is cited as a paradigm of "reviving the old to open the new" (*fu gu kai xin*) and "integrating calligraphy into painting" (*yi shu ru hua*).
*   **Techniques Used**:
    *   **Color**: Used *hua qing* (flower blue) mixed with *shi qing* (stone blue) for the mountains to replicate the high-ancient style of Tang Dynasty blue-green landscapes. Small amounts of cinnabar, ochre, and Chinese yellow were used for trees and houses to create an autumnal atmosphere.
    *   **Brushwork**: Used *pi ma cun* (pampered hemp texture) for Queshan and low slopes; *he ye cun* (lotus leaf texture) and *jie suo cun* (unraveled rope texture) for the rugged Hua Bushuzhu Shan; and seal script/bronze script brush techniques for the trees.
*   **Composition**: The painting utilizes a horizontal "flat distance" (*ping yuan*) perspective with a three-part structure: Hua Bushuzhu Shan (front/right), slopes/banks/reefs (middle), and Queshan (back/left).
*   **Public Interpretation**: Art historian Fang Wen is cited as arguing the work represents a new spatial expression where the ground extends continuously backward, creating a more realistic spatial depth than previous generations. However, the author notes this "reality" is contradicted by intentional proportion errors (e.g., a boatman being smaller than a distant goat), which may be an homage to the naive style of Jin and Tang landscape paintings.

## Event Conclusion

The event consists of the publication of a critical appreciation of Zhao Mengfu’s *Autumn Colors on the Magpie and Hua Mountains* in *People's Daily* on September 13, 2026. The article serves to re-evaluate the painting not just as a scenic landscape, but as a pivotal moment in Chinese art history where calligraphic principles were rigorously applied to painting, marking a deliberate "revival" of Tang Dynasty aesthetics.
