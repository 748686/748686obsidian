## Event Overview

On September 13, 2026, *People's Daily* (Page 08) published an article titled "Visual Echoes of 'Ten Farewells to the Red Army'" under the column "Journey Painted in Pictures: Stories of the Red Army's Long March." Authored by He Hongzhou, a member of the Art Committee for National Major Themes in Fine Art Creation of the China Artists Association, the article provides a critical appreciation of three representative works of Chinese art depicting the historical event of the Central Red Army's departure from Yudu, Jiangxi, in 1934. The works analyzed include an oil painting by Jin Shangyi, an oil painting by Shi Shaochen, and a Chinese ink painting by Wu Xiansheng. The article contextualizes these artworks within the broader narrative of the Long March and their role in preserving collective memory and conveying the spirit of the revolution.

## Core Facts

*   **Publication Details**: The article was published in *People's Daily* on September 13, 2026, on Page 08 (Supplementary Publications).
*   **Author**: He Hongzhou, identified as a member of the Art Committee for National Major Themes in Fine Art Creation of the China Artists Association.
*   **Historical Context**:
    *   In 1934, following the failure of the Fifth Anti-Encirclement Campaign, the main force of the Central Red Army gathered at the Yudu River in Yudu, Jiangxi, to begin its strategic transfer (the Long March).
    *   Local residents in Yudu assisted the Red Army by dismantling wooden beds and doors to build floating bridges for the river crossing.
    *   The departure and the farewell between the Red Army and the masses was immortalized in the song "Ten Farewells to the Red Army."
    *   In 2019, Xi Jinping visited Yudu to commemorate the Long March's 85th anniversary, emphasizing the importance of remembering the history of the red regime.
*   **Analyzed Artworks**:
    1.  **"Farewell" by Jin Shangyi**: An oil painting created in 1959. It uses a narrative style to depict a specific moment of farewell at dawn, featuring various pairs (mother/son, wife/husband) to emphasize the bond between the military and the people.
    2.  **"Tears of Relatives, Endless Gan River" by Shi Shaochen**: An oil painting created roughly 20 years after Jin's work (implied timeline in text). It uses a diptych (two-panel) format without plot-specific narrative, focusing on the emotional exchange and gaze between civilians and soldiers to create a spiritual dialogue across time.
    3.  **"Ten Farewells to the Red Army" by Wu Xiansheng**: A Chinese ink painting (scroll format) created to commemorate the 80th anniversary of the victory of the Long March. It employs a long-scroll narrative style depicting scenes of farewell and departure, characterized by revolutionary romanticism and progressive emotional flow.
*   **Artistic Evolution**: The article outlines a trajectory from realistic, plot-driven depiction (Jin) to psychological/emotional dialogue (Shi) to narrative scrolling and romantic symbolism (Wu), reflecting changes in artistic media and historical contexts over time.
