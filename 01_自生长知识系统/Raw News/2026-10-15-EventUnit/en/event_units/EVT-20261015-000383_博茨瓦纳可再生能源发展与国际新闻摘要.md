## Core Facts

- Botswana’s electricity supply has historically relied heavily on coal, with the country spending approximately 3.4 billion pula (approx. 163 million RMB) annually on electricity imports.
- Botswana plans to increase the share of renewable energy in its electricity mix to 50% by 2036, as outlined in its “Vision 2036.”
- The goal is to transform Botswana from a net electricity importer into a net exporter.
- The Juwan (Zhuhua Neng) 100-megawatt (MW) photovoltaic (PV) power station, built with Chinese enterprise involvement, has commenced commercial operations. It is one of Botswana’s first 100-MW centralized PV plants.
- Total national PV installed capacity has reached over 200 MW, according to Edwin Ketilewe, an official at Botswana’s Ministry of Minerals and Energy.
- In April 2026, the Maun 500-MW PV and 500-MWh energy storage project was laid in northwestern Botswana. It is described as the country’s largest grid-scale PV project and Africa’s second-largest energy storage project, expected to enter service in 2029.
- The Maun project uses a “PV + storage” design to address grid stability challenges associated with renewable energy intermittency.
- The Juwan project was developed by a consortium including China Harbour Engineering Company and local Botswanan partners, operating under an integrated investment-construction-operation model.
- Construction of the Juwan plant and its配套 132-kV substation and transmission facilities was completed in just 11 months.
- Equipment availability rate at Juwan has remained stable at above 99%.
- Local communities report improved electricity reliability; previously frequent power outages affecting clinics, schools, and residences have significantly decreased.
- Chinese engineers conducted on-site training for local youth in photovoltaic panel installation, equipment inspection, and tracker maintenance.
- Botswana President Duma Boko expressed confidence in China-Botswana cooperation, stating that China’s technology and experience in renewable energy and green industries are precisely what Botswana needs, and affirming that Botswana remains open to Chinese enterprise participation.
