## Event Overview
This event synthesizes reporting from the *People's Daily* (October 26, 2026) regarding the transformation of Ulanqab (Inner Mongolia). The coverage highlights two distinct narratives: the economic shift of Ulanqab from agriculture ("growing potatoes") to the digital infrastructure sector ("growing tokens") through green computing power, and a broader theoretical analysis of converting grassroots organizational advantages into tangible rural revitalization results. While merged based on the premise that both articles discuss regional transformation methodologies, the sources address different subjects: one focuses on industrial economic transition, and the other on political governance and agricultural community organization.

## Core Facts

### Ulanqab Green Computing Transformation (Source: Article #495)
*   **Location:** Ulanqab.
*   **Transformation Narrative:** The region is transitioning from an agricultural base (historically associated with potato farming) to a green computing power industry base (metaphorically described as "growing tokens").
*   **Methodology:** The shift is attributed to adhering to a methodology of "adapting to local conditions" (因地制宜).
*   **Source Verification:** Reported in *People's Daily*, Page 18 (Technology section), under the title "From 'Growing Potatoes' to 'Growing Tokens' (Deep Observation)."

### Grassroots Organization and Rural Revitalization (Source: Article #496)
*   **Theoretical Framework:** Rural revitalization success depends on strengthening grassroots party organizations, viewing them as the "nerve endings" of the party body and the foundation for good governance and wealth creation.
*   **Organizational Strategy:**
    *   **Talent Acquisition:** Recruiting wealth creators, returning youth, and veterans into village "two committees" (party branch and villagers' committee) to strengthen the "head goose" (leadership) team.
    *   **Decision Making:** Establishing party-led deliberation and decision-making mechanisms to unite scattered farmers and business entities into development communities.
    *   **Industrial Model:** Promoting the "Party Branch + Cooperative + Farmhouse" model. Party branches are built on industrial chains, and party members are placed in key positions. This allows the party to coordinate idle resources, connect with leading enterprises/research institutions, and unify standards/brands.
*   **Specific Applications:**
    *   Village parties lead cooperatives in特色种植 (specialty planting), under-forest economy, and agro-cultural tourism.
    *   Coordination of policy implementation, technical services, and market docking.
    *   Skill training (agricultural tech, handicrafts, e-commerce) and labor docking platforms to attract returning workers.
*   **Governance:** Utilizing villagers' councils and supervision mechanisms for major decisions. Activating village representatives and volunteers for dispute resolution. Using village rules and marriage/funeral councils to shift customs.
*   **Author:** Du Zhixiong (杜志雄).
*   **Source Verification:** Reported in *People's Daily*, Page 19 (New Countryside section), titled "Turning Organizational Advantages into Practical Results for Rural Revitalization ('Three Rural Issues' Observation)."
