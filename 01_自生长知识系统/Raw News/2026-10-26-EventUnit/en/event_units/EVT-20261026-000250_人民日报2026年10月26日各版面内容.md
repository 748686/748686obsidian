### 2. Consumer Protection: Water Purifier Scams Targeting Elderly (Page 07)
*   **Source:** 对净水器“坑老”骗局说不 (Say No to Water Purifier "Pitting the Elderly" Scams).
*   **Trend:** Complaints about water purifiers surged in 2026. According to the China Consumers Association's smart 315 platform, complaints in the first 7 months of 2026 exceeded the total for all of 2024. July complaints rose **40.17%** month-on-month from January.
*   **Scam Tactics:**
    *   False experiments using electrolysis rods to turn clear water "rusty" to制造 anxiety.
    *   Claims that purified water can treat "three highs" (hypertension, hyperglycemia, hyperlipidemia) or cure cancer.
    *   Targeting rural left-behind elderly via "free gifts," "lottery prizes," and on-site lectures.
    *   Selling "free" machines but charging thousands for filters/accessories (e.g., 5,400 RMB in Hohhot case).
*   **Product Issues:**
    *   Many products are "three-no" items (no manufacturer, no hygiene license, no CCC certification).
    *   Fake labeling, mixed models, and missing mandatory national standard info (water efficiency grade, rated净水量).
    *   Risks include heavy metal leaching and bacterial growth.
*   **Legal/Regulatory Challenges:**
    *  维权难 (Difficulty in rights protection): Sellers are often mobile with no fixed store.
    *   Price fraud: Selling price (e.g., 2,980 RMB) vs. online price (e.g., 500 RMB).
    *   "Electronic contract" loopholes: Sellers provide fake links showing high prices to justify cost.
    *   Evidence destruction: Elderly victims often record themselves "voluntarily" purchasing.
*   **Expert Recommendations (Renmin University Law School):**
    *   Liu Junhai: Establish cross-departmental risk warning mechanisms; use QR code traceability for water purifiers; impose qualification penalties (license revocation) and industry bans; consider fraud charges for severe cases.
    *   Chen Yinjiang (China Law Society): Regular patrols for community/rural sales; strengthen consumer education.
