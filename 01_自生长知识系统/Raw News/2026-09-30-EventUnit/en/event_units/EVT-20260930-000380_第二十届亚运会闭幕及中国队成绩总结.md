## Event Overview
The 20th Asian Games concluded around September 29–30, 2026. The event was characterized by a significant overall improvement in Asian sports standards, with numerous competitors entering the world's top tier. The competition saw a record-breaking 30 world records rewritten across weightlifting and shooting events alone. The Chinese delegation secured a dominant position in the medal tally, finishing with 143 gold medals, 62 silver medals, and 49 bronze medals as of the conclusion of competition on September 29.

## Core Facts
*   **Event Status:** The 20th Asian Games concluded with final events taking place on September 28 and 29, 2026.
*   **Chinese Delegation Total:** As of September 29, the Chinese delegation won 143 gold, 62 silver, and 49 bronze medals (Source: Article #576).
*   **World Records:** 30 world records were broken or equaled in weightlifting and shooting. Specifically, 25 world records were broken in weightlifting, and the Chinese shooting team had broken 5 world records and equaled 1 prior to the conclusion of shooting events (Source: Article #574).
*   **Asian Sports Development:** The level of competition across multiple disciplines (badminton, weightlifting, shooting, table tennis, gymnastics, athletics, diving, swimming) indicated that Asian athletes' overall strength is at the forefront of the world (Source: Article #574).

### Key Results by Sport
*   **Diving:**
    *   Women's 10m Platform: Chen Yuxi (China) won Gold; Lu Wei (China) won Silver (Source: Article #576).
    *   Men's 3m Springboard: Wang Zongyuan (China) won Gold (his 3rd of the Games); Jeong Su-won (South Korea) won Silver (Source: Article #576).
*   **Badminton:**
    *   Mixed Doubles: Jiang Zhenbang/Wei Yaxin (China) won Gold (Source: Article #576).
    *   Women's Doubles: Liu Shengshu/Tan Ning (China) lost to Lee So-hee/Baek Ha-na (South Korea) in 3 sets (28-26, 1-2 final score); Liu/Tan won Silver (Source: Articles #574, #576).
    *   Men's Doubles: Liang Weikeng/Wang Chang (China) won Silver (Source: Article #576).
    *   Note: Article #574 highlights that the top 5 in all 5 badminton events were almost exclusively Asian, signaling the development of the global sport.
*   **Weightlifting:**
    *   Team Result: China won 5 gold, 2 silver, and 3 bronze medals; all 10 Chinese competitors won medals (Source: Article #576).
    *   Women's +86kg: Li Wenwen (China) won Gold (Source: Articles #574, #576).
    *   Women's 86kg: Wu Yan (China) won Gold (Source: Article #576).
    *   Men's 110kg: Liu Huanhua (China) won Bronze (Source: Article #576).
    *   World Record Detail: Yang Liuyue (China) broke records in snatch and total in the Women's 61kg event; Park Jin-hai (North Korea) broke the clean and jerk world record in the same event (Source: Article #574).
*   **Shooting:**
    *   Women's 25m Pistol: Xiao Jiaxuixuan (China) won Gold, defeating world record holder Esha Sing (India) after two extra rounds (Source: Article #574).
    *   Women's Trap Team: Sun Yunong, Liu Shanshan, and Zhu Jingyu (China) won Gold; Sun Yunong also won Silver in the individual event (Source: Article #576).
    *   Men's Trap: Qi Ying (China) won Bronze (Source: Article #576).
*   **Athletics:**
    *   Team Result: China won 17 gold, 14 silver, and 8 bronze medals (Source: Article #576).
    *   Women's 800m: Wu Hongjiao (China) won Gold with a time of 2:04.49 (Source: Article #576).
    *   Women's Hammer Throw: Zhang Jiale (China) won Gold (setting an Asian Games record); Zhao Jie (China) won Silver (Source: Article #576).
    *   Mixed 4x100m Relay: Chen Jinfeng, Liu Guoyi, Shi Junhao, and Chen Yujie (China) won Gold with a time of 40.78 seconds (Source: Article #576).
    *   Men's Pole Vault: Yao Jie (China) won Silver; Li Chenyang (China) won Bronze (Source: Article #576).
    *   Women's 4x400m Relay: China won Bronze (Source: Article #576).
    *   Men's Javelin: Palleegra (Sri Lanka) won Gold with a throw of 88.55m; Yadav (India) was also in the top 5 globally (Source: Article #574).
    *   Women's Javelin: Yan Ziyi (China) won Gold; Kitaguchi Kanka (Japan) was the Olympic and World Championships double champion (Source: Article #574).
*   **Cycling (Track):** China won Gold in both Women's and Men's Team Sprint events, setting new Asian Games and Asian records (Source: Article #576).
*   **Table Tennis:** China won 6 gold and 4 silver medals out of 7 events, sweeping the top two spots in Mixed Doubles, Women's Singles, and Men's Singles. Sun Yingsha and Wang Manyu (China) dominate the world rankings (Source: Article #574).
*   **Gymnastics:** Zhang Boheng (China) won the Men's Individual All-Around title, defending against Tokyo champion Daiki Hashimoto and Paris champion Shinjo Okano (Source: Article #574).
