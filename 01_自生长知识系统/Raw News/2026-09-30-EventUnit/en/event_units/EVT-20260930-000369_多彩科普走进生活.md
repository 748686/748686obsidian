# Event Unit Synthesis: "Colorful Science Popularization Enters Daily Life"

## Event Overview

On September 30, 2026, *People's Daily* published a special report titled "Colorful Science Popularization Enters Daily Life" (大数据观察 - Big Data Observation), authored by reporter Yu Sinan. The report analyzes the status of China's National Science Popularization Month activities during the second full month following the implementation of the revised Science Popularization Law. The article highlights three major trends in science popularization (Kepu) in China: the expansion of science study tour (Yanxue) scenarios, the decentralization of scientific resources to grassroots communities and rural areas, and the integration of Artificial Intelligence (AI) into content creation. The report also addresses emerging challenges, including talent shortages in educational interpreting and the spread of pseudoscience via digital platforms.

## Core Facts

**National Context and Statistics:**
*   **Timeline:** September 2026 marks the second National Science Popularization Month since the revised Science Popularization Law took effect [Source: Article #561].
*   **Citizen Scientific Literacy:** According to data from the National Bureau of Statistics of China, the proportion of citizens possessing scientific literacy reached **16.74%** in 2025 [Source: Article #561].
*   **Trend:** The "14th Five-Year Plan" period has seen a significant提升 (increase) in scientific literacy levels and a stronger social atmosphere advocating innovation [Source: Article #561].

**Sector 1: Science Study Tours (Yanxue)**
*   **Case Study:** In Deyang, Sichuan Province, students from Deyang No. 5 Middle School visited the State First Heavy Machinery Group Co., Ltd. to observe a 16,000-ton hydraulic press [Source: Article #561].
*   **Impact:** The visit demonstrated the practical application of Pascal's Law from textbooks and inspired students (e.g., student Zhang Zixuan) to consider careers in scientific research [Source: Article #561].
*   **Scale:** From January to August 2026, Deyang held **358** science study tour events, serving over **67,800** young people [Source: Article #561].
*   **Key Observation:** Zheng Nian (Chairman of the Science Education Professional Committee of the China Association for Science and Technology Writers) noted a shift from "knowledge transmission" to "literacy cultivation" through real-scene observation [Source: Article #561].
*   **Constraint:** Wang Lijuan (Head of Science Popularization Department, Deyang Association for Science and Technology) identified a lack of **composite science study tour mentors**—professionals who possess both technical engineering knowledge and public speaking/pedagogical skills [Source: Article #561].

**Sector 2: Grassroots Resource Decentralization**
*   **Agricultural Application:** In Bayin'guole City, Inner Mongolia, the team of Li ShaoKun (Chinese Academy of Agricultural Sciences) conducted "field classrooms" for corn farmers during the milky ripening stage.
    *   **Reach:** Li ShaoKun’s team has promoted "precision regulation technology for high-yield maize dense planting" across **240 million mu** nationwide.
    *   **Output:** This has increased grain output by over **27.12 million tons** and increased farmer income by nearly **30 billion RMB** [Source: Article #561].
    *   **Digital Spread:** Short videos of these training sessions have achieved millions of views on online platforms [Source: Article #561].
*   **Urban Community Application:**
    *   **Zhangdian District, Zibo, Shandong:** Communities installed informational boards on Heimlich maneuver and carbon monoxide poisoning prevention, targeting aging populations [Source: Article #561].
    *   **Linzhi District, Zibo, Shandong:** The Sangyang Community utilized immersive fire drills to educate residents on the dangers of e-bike battery charging in residential buildings [Source: Article #561].
*   **Governance Integration:** Chen Xiaoyan (Head of Science Popularization Department, Shandong Association for Science and Technology) described a service model where residents "order" science topics, communities "assign" them, and volunteer teams "fulfill" them, making science popularization a "flexible grip" for grassroots governance [Source: Article #561].

**Sector 3: AI Empowerment**
*   **Case Study:** A popular micro-documentary titled *"The Science Being意外 (Accidentally) Handled"* utilized AI for visual effects, animation, and dialogue, focusing on accidental scientific discoveries [Source: Article #561].
*   **Expert Analysis:** Li Honglin (Researcher, Chinese Academy of Science and Technology for Development) stated that AI assists in inspiration,资料 organization, and content checking, lowering the barrier for public participation in science creation [Source: Article #561].
*   **Risks:** A "gray industry chain" has emerged where entities produce pseudoscience for traffic monetization ("account building, pushing streams, selling goods"). Li Honglin recommends building authoritative knowledge bases based on peer-reviewed journals and textbooks, supplemented by technical and expert verification [Source: Article #561].

## Event Conclusion

The synthesis of the September 30, 2026, report from *People's Daily* indicates that China's science popularization efforts are entering a phase of structural diversification. Driven by the revised Science Popularization Law, the sector is moving beyond traditional lectures toward immersive "study tours" in industrial sites, practical "field classrooms" in agriculture, and integrated community governance models. While national scientific literacy has reached 16.74% (2025 data), significant bottlenecks remain, specifically a shortage of qualified interpretive educators for study tours and the unchecked spread of AI-generated pseudoscience. The report advocates for strengthening talent pipelines and establishing authoritative, AI-verified knowledge bases to ensure the quality of information dissemination.
