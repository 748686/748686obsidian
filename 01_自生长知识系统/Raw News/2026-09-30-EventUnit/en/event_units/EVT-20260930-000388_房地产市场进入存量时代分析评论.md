## Event Overview

On September 30, 2026, Professor Tian Xuan, Dean of the Guanghua School of Management at Peking University, published a commentary titled "Let Real Estate Truly Return to 'Living'" in the *People's Daily* (Page 18). The article synthesizes official statements from the Ministry of Housing and Urban-Rural Development (MOHURD) and market data to argue that China's real estate sector has fundamentally shifted from a growth-oriented phase to a stock-dominated phase. The piece outlines the drivers of this transition, the changing nature of demand, and the policy requirements for sustainable development centered on居住 (living) quality rather than investment speculation.

## Core Facts

**1. Official Declaration of Phase Transition**
*   On September 2026, an official from MOHURD stated during a State Council Information Office press conference that China’s real estate market has entered a stock phase (Source: Article #600).

**2. Market Data Evidence**
*   Second-hand home transaction share rose from 27% in 2020 to 46% in 2025.
*   In the first eight months of 2026, second-hand transactions accounted for 52% of the market, surpassing new homes as the primary channel for housing transactions (Source: Article #600).
*   From January to August 2026, newly built commercial housing sales area decreased by 12.1% year-on-year.
*   In the same period, second-hand home transaction net-signing area increased by 10.6% year-on-year (Source: Article #600).

**3. Supply-Side Indicators**
*   Urban per capita housing construction area exceeds 40 square meters.
*   Average household housing ownership exceeds 1.1 units.
*   These metrics indicate that the basic problem of "having a place to live" has been substantially resolved for the population (Source: Article #600).

**4. Demand-Side Indicators**
*   China’s permanent resident urbanization rate has reached 67%.
*   Urban development is shifting from large-scale incremental expansion to quality improvement of existing stock.
*   Residential demand is transitioning from "whether there is a house" to "whether the house is good," with improvement-driven exchanges ("selling old to buy new," "small to large") becoming the mainstream (Source: Article #600).

**5. Policy and Regulatory Developments**
*   MOHURD is promoting the construction of "good houses" (好房子).
*   A new version of the *Residential Project Standards* (住宅项目规范) has been introduced, proposing multiple requirements for improving housing quality.
*   These standards serve dual purposes: guiding new residential development and providing a basis for the renovation/upgrading of existing housing stock (Source: Article #600).

**6. Economic Repositioning**
*   The real estate industry’s contribution to the macroeconomy is shifting from incremental growth dominance to stock enhancement.
*   Urban renewal projects—including shantytowns, dangerous/old housing, and old residential community renovations—are identified as significant forces for stabilizing investment (Source: Article #600).

## Known Current Impact

**1. Structural Market Shift**
The data indicates a definitive "gear shift" in demand structure. The market is no longer defined by total supply shortages but by structural supply issues where consumers prioritize quality and living experience over asset appreciation potential.

**2. Investment Behavior Change**
The commentary suggests a psychological shift among residents: moving away from "chasing price rises and selling on dips" (speculative behavior) toward "rational replacement" (utility-driven behavior). The value anchor of housing is reportedly shifting from "will it appreciate?" to "is it good quality?"

**3. Policy Direction**
The article reinforces the government’s dual focus on:
*   **Risk Prevention & Livelihood Protection:** Ensuring basic housing needs are met while managing industry risks.
*   **Quality Improvement:** Using standards like the *Residential Project Standards* to drive the "good house" initiative.

**4. Macroeconomic Contribution**
Real estate’s role in stabilizing the economy is redefined. Instead of driving growth through new construction volume, its contribution will come from urban renewal and stock optimization, specifically targeting old communities and dangerous housing.

## Event Conclusion

The publication represents a high-level policy narrative confirming that China's real estate market has structurally transitioned from an incremental expansion phase to a stock optimization phase. The core argument, advanced by Professor Tian Xuan in an official capacity, posits that this shift is characterized by a dominance of second-hand transactions, a saturation of basic housing supply, and a consumer preference for quality over speculation. The event underscores a strategic pivot in economic policy toward urban renewal, stricter quality standards, and the redefinition of real estate’s role as a livelihood service rather than a primary investment vehicle.
