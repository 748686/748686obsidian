## Event Overview
On September 29, 2026, the China Meteorological Administration (CMA) held a press conference in Beijing, releasing the latest monitoring and prediction data regarding the El Niño event. According to the National Climate Center, sea surface temperatures (SST) in the equatorial central and eastern Pacific have continued to warm since September 2026. This development is predicted to form the strongest "Super El Niño" event in the history of observational records, significantly exceeding previous historical peaks.

The event originated in May 2026, with rapid warming trends observed throughout the summer and autumn. The peak intensity is projected for November 2026, with severe climatic implications expected to extend through the winter of 2026–2027 and into the spring and summer of 2027.

*   **Historical Context:**
    *   Previous historical maximum peak was **2.8°C** during the 2014–2016 Super El Niño event.
    *   There have been three recorded Super El Niño events in history: 1982–1983, 1997–1998, and 2014–2016.
    *   Current SST monitoring indices and warming rates are significantly higher than those during the same periods of the three historical Super El Niño events.
*   **Classification Criteria (National Climate Center):**
    *   El Niño State: SST anomaly index ≥0.5°C for 3 consecutive months.
    *   El Niño Event: Lasts 5 months or longer.
    *   Super El Niño Event: Peak intensity ≥2.5°C.
*   **Climatic Impact Timeline:**

High risk of extreme precipitation, mountain floods, landslides, and debris flows. Western China autumn rain will be above average ("more in south, less in north").     *   **Winter 2026–2027 (Dec–Feb):** Temperatures generally higher than normal, but with more frequent cold waves and significant temperature fluctuations. Precipitation generally above average. Key risks include strong cold waves and low-temperature ice/snow disasters in southern China.
    *   **Spring/Summer 2027:** The El Niño event is expected to decay rapidly and end. However, impacts will remain significant, including increased extreme weather events,明显 higher temperatures, and stronger/wider summer heatwaves.
