# Event: Formalism in Rural Environmental Governance Leads to "Pollution by Treatment"

## Event Overview

On September 30, 2026, *People's Daily* published a commentary in its "People Sharp Commentary" (人民锐评) section titled "Strict Pollution Control Becomes Pollution Causing" (严防治污成“致污”). The article addresses reports from recent media exposing乱象 (chaotic phenomena) in rural environmental pollution governance in China. It highlights how administrative formalism,粗放 (extensive/crude) management, and a lack of adherence to objective realities have resulted in governance efforts inadvertently causing new pollution, rather than alleviating it.

The commentary argues that while China has achieved significant results in rural environmental整治 (remediation)—with rural domestic sewage treatment rates exceeding 55% and large areas of black and odorous water bodies eliminated—the countryside remains a weak link. The article posits that without respecting local conditions and implementing scientific, systematic management, increased governance efforts can yield counterproductive outcomes ("going in the opposite direction").

## Core Facts

1.  **Incidents in Anhui Province:** Personnel applying biogas slurry to farmland failed to understand application norms. Over a period of two days, they poured more than 3,300 tons of biogas slurry into over 10 mu (approximately 0.67 hectares) of farmland, far exceeding normal usage levels.
2.  **Incidents in Jiangsu Province:**
    *   Sewage treatment stations in some villages were running empty (equipment idling without processing actual wastewater).
    *   In other cases, unreasonable pipe network designs caused domestic sewage to backflow and flood into households, creating new pollution sources.
3.  **National Context (Source-Reported):** According to information disclosed by the Ministry of Ecology and Environment, China's rural domestic sewage treatment rate has exceeded 55%, representing a doubling compared to the end of the "13th Five-Year Plan" period. Most large-area rural black and odorous water bodies have been basically eliminated, and the level of resource utilization of agricultural waste is continuously improving.
4.  **Systemic Analysis:**
    *   **Root Cause:** Management is extensive and characterized by formalism. Governance efforts are driven by "visible" metrics (e.g., number of facilities built) rather than actual effectiveness.
    *   **Constraints:** Rural areas face large regional differences, complex and dispersed pollutant sources, limited funding (preventing universal equipment deployment), and relatively weak environmental awareness among some residents.
    *   **Solutions Proposed:**
        *   **Adherence to Reality:** Avoid one-size-fits-all approaches. For sparsely populated areas with low per-capita sewage generation, centralized collection is difficult; distributed, simple treatment methods (e.g., converting sewage to fertilizer) are more effective.
        *   **Systematic Concept:** Pollution control requires managing the entire chain. In the biogas slurry case, while on-farm harmless treatment was strictly controlled, off-farm transport and precision application监管 (supervision) and guidance were neglected, turning a "resource" back into a "pollutant."
        *   **Performance Evaluation:** Change the "baton" (evaluation metrics) from focusing on infrastructure construction to focusing on actual pollutant treatment results and群众满意度 (public satisfaction).

## Event Conclusion

The event highlights a critical critique of rural environmental governance in China as of September 30, 2026. *People's Daily* identifies a paradox where rigorous pollution control measures, when executed through formalistic and non-scientific means, result in *increased* pollution (e.g., biogas slurry over-application, sewage backflow). The core conclusion is that effective governance requires adhering to reality, employing systematic management across the entire pollution chain (from production to application/disposal), and reforming official performance evaluations to prioritize actual environmental outcomes and public satisfaction over the construction of visible infrastructure.
