## Event Overview

On September 30, 2026, *People’s Daily* published a commentary titled “What is an Elite? What Should an Elite Do?” (何谓精英，精英何为) in its “Warm News Hot Review” (暖闻热评) section. The article addresses public discourse surrounding Gong Baodong (龚宝冬), a post-00s grid worker (网格员) who died saving constituents during a landslide in Pengshui, Chongqing, in July 2026. The commentary was triggered by Southwest University of Political Science and Law (SWUPL) President Lin Wei’s affirmation during the university’s 2026 freshman orientation that Gong Baodong, despite his humble occupation as a county-level grid worker, was indeed an elite cultivated by the institution. The piece argues against narrow,功利 (utilitarian) definitions of “elite” and redefines the term around duty, public service, and the integration of personal fate with the era’s needs.

## Core Facts

1.  **Gong Baodong’s Heroic Act:** In July 2026, during a山体崩塌 (mountain collapse/landslide) incident in Pengshui, Chongqing, Gong Baodong and his colleagues evacuated over 60 residents from the core danger zone. Gong Baodong perished in the process.
2.  **Educational Background:** Gong Baodong was a student/alumnus of Southwest University of Political Science and Law (SWUPL). His motto from his student days was: “I think we should all be doers; we don’t need those beautiful words to decorate” (“我想我们都应该当个实干家，不需要那些美丽的词汇来修饰”).
3.  **University Response:** SWUPL President Lin Wei publicly affirmed Gong Baodong’s status as an elite during the 2026 freshman opening ceremony. The university honored him through multiple measures:
    *   Converting its official website to black-and-white color scheme.
    *   Publishing “Letters from New Students” in his memory.
    *   Displaying his photo on a Martyrs’ Wall (英烈墙).
    *   Alumni association initiating the establishment of a scholarship in his name.
4.  **Media Commentary:** *People’s Daily* (September 30, 2026, Page 05) published an editorial questioning why society hesitates to label a grid worker an “elite,” attributing this to a distorted perception of the term. The article contrasts superficial markers of elite status (high income, glamour, fame) with substantive virtues (responsibility, courage, selflessness, practical action).
5.  **Broader Context of Institutional Values:** The article cites examples of other Chinese universities reinforcing similar values:
    *   Nanjing University named Wang Tongge (王同歌), who spent decades at a coastal meteorological station in Guanyun County, Lianyungang, Jiangsu, as an “Outstanding Alumni.”
    *   Tsinghua University established the “Qihang Award” (启航奖) to encourage graduates to serve in key regions, grassroots areas, and arduous industries/positions.
6.  **Authorship:** The commentary was written by Chen Bingxu (陈炳旭).
