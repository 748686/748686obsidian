## Event Overview
On November 13, 2026, the 07th page (Horizon section) of *People's Daily* published two thematically linked articles under the banner "Forging a Strong Sense of Community for the Chinese Nation." The first is a field report on the left-officiale Zuo Zongtang (Zuo Gong) cultural sites in Hami, Xinjiang, highlighting their role in patriotic education and national identity. The second is a review by prominent historian Xing Guangcheng of the newly published academic work *Chinese History of the Xinjiang Region*, analyzing its contributions to understanding Xinjiang's historical integration into China.

## Core Facts

**1. Zuo Zongtang Cultural Presence in Hami**
*   **Location:** Hami City, Xinjiang, specifically the Left-Officiale Zuo Zongtang Cultural Park (Zuo Gong Wen Hua Yuan) along the Hami River and the Phoenix Terrace Historical and Cultural Park (opened June 2025).
*   **Historical Context:** 140+ years ago, Zuo Zongtang stayed in Hami for 162 days, leading military and civilian efforts in canal construction, farming, and planting mulberry and willow trees.
*   **Physical Remnants:** Over 2,000 "Left-Officiale Willows" remain in Hami, including over 200 ancient trees with a century-old age. This is described as the largest and best-preserved native cluster of such willows in China.
*   **Visitor Impact:** The Cultural Park has received over 500,000 visitors for guided tours since opening. Reports note that visitors, including children, express strong identification with national unity and patriotism (e.g., vows to "defend the country" and "not yield an inch of territory").
*   **Educational Integration:** Since 2024, Hami has implemented "Left-Officiale Willow" ideological and political study tours in its education system. Coverage across primary/secondary schools and vocational colleges reached 60%. The curriculum includes role-playing, student docent programs, and on-site research.
*   **Symbolic Activities:** In April 2026, representatives from seven cities (Fuzhou, Tianjin, Yili, Hotan, Kashgar, Urumqi, Hami) gathered at Phoenix Terrace. They mixed soil from their respective regions in a "Mountain-River Cauldron" and planted "Unity Willows" (Tong Xin Liu) propagated from centenary willow cuttings.

**2. Publication of *Chinese History of the Xinjiang Region***
*   **Publication:** A seven-chapter academic work titled *Chinese History of the Xinjiang Region* (*Zhongguo Xinjiang Diqu Lishi*) has been published.
*   **Author:** Reviewed by Xing Guangcheng (Academician of the Chinese Academy of Social Sciences, Chief Expert of the China Western Regions Research Center).
*   **Structural Framework:**
    *   **Chapters 1–4 (Ancient to 1949):** Divided into four periods emphasizing integration:
        1.  Pre-Qin to Han: "Heaven Under One Roof: Xinjiang Has Been an Inseparable Part of China Since Antiquity."
        2.  Wei-Jin-Northern-Southern Dynasties to Sui-Tang: "Four Seas Returning to the Heart: Xinjiang's Atmosphere in the Chinese Golden Age."
        3.  Liao-Song-Xia-Jin to Yuan-Ming-Qing: "One Family in China: Xinjiang in the Consolidation of a Unified Multi-ethnic State."
        4.  1912–1949: "Moving Toward Light: Xinjiang Merging into the New Democratic Revolution Torrent."
        5.  1949–1978: "A Changed World: Xinjiang's Years of Socialist Revolution and Construction."
        6.  1978–2012: "Keeping Pace with the Times: Xinjiang's Path on Socialism with Chinese Characteristics."
        7.  2012–Present: "Rushing Toward Rejuvenation: Building a Beautiful Xinjiang in Chinese Modernization."
    *   **Special Chapter:** Dedicated to the "New Era Party's Strategy for Governing Xinjiang," characterized by the total goal of social stability and long-term security, the "Eight Adherences," and the five governance principles (rule of law, unity, cultural nourishment, prosperity, long-term construction).
*   **Key Historical Arguments:**
    *   **Territorial Integrity:** Claims the Han Dynasty established the Protectorate of the Western Regions in 60 BCE as the start of effective central jurisdiction. Asserts all subsequent dynasties viewed Xinjiang as inseparable territory.
    *   **Ethnic Integration:** Argues Xinjiang has always been a multi-ethnic聚居 (gathering) area. Populations moved in and out since Qin/Han, and all were co-developers. Ethnic relations are characterized by unity and shared struggle against aggression and separatism.
    *   **Cultural Identity:** Posits that Xinjiang cultures bear the imprint of Chinese civilization's "diversity within unity" from the start. Religious coexistence is described as the historical mainstream.
    *   **Reinterpretation of Nomadic Entities:**
        *   **Xiongnu:** Views Han-Xiongnu conflicts as clashes between agricultural and nomadic economies, not ethnic oppositions. Notes Xiongnu rule in the Western Regions laid foundations for Han unification.
        *   **Turkic:** Defines the Turkic Khaganate (est. 552 CE) as a multi-tribal nomadic regime subordinate to Sui/Tang. States Turks dissolved in the 8th century; modern ethnic groups formed in Central/West Asia are essentially different from ancient Turks.
        *   **East Turkestan:** Explicitly denies the historical existence of "East Turkestan" as a name for Xinjiang or a state entity.
*   **Core Conclusion:** Unification is the prerequisite for stability; national strength is the foundation for development; CPC leadership is the guarantee for long-term peace.
