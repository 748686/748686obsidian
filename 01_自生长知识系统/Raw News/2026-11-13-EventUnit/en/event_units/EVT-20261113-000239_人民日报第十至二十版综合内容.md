## Core Facts

### 1. Legislative Milestone: Anti-Cross-Border Corruption Law
*   **Event:** The Standing Committee of the 14th National People's Congress adopted the "Law of the People's Republic of China on Anti-Cross-Border Corruption" on November 6, 2026.
*   **Source:** Article #398 (Full Text), Article #399 (Interpretation).
*   **Details:**
    *   Signed into law by President Xi Jinping via State President’s Order No. 89.
    *   It is China’s first specialized anti-corruption law involving foreign elements.
    *   Structure: 6 chapters, 47 articles.
    *   **Definition of Cross-Border Corruption (Article 2):** Includes bribery of foreign public officials/international organization officials by Chinese entities; bribery of Chinese officials by境外 (foreign) entities; embezzlement/bribery committed by Chinese citizens/entities abroad; asset transfer across borders; and corruption-related crimes with partial execution or result abroad.
    *   **Mechanism:** Establishes a national anti-cross-border corruption working mechanism led by the National Supervisory Commission.
    *   **Compliance:** Introduces a dedicated chapter on "Integrity Compliance" (Chapter 4), legally mandating enterprises engaged in cross-border business to establish compliance management systems. This marks the first time corporate integrity compliance has been elevated from a policy requirement to a legal obligation in China.
    *   **Effective Date:** Upon promulgation (November 6, 2026).

### 2. Diplomatic Action: Sanctions on Taiwan "Diplomatic" Officials
*   **Event:** On October 12, 2026, the Ministry of Foreign Affairs announced counter-measures against two ministers from Taiwan’s so-called "diplomatic partners."
*   **Targets:**
    *   **Villalta** (Minister of Communications and Technology, Paraguay).
    *   **Fonseca** (Minister of Foreign Affairs, Belize).
*   **Measures:** Freezing of assets in China, prohibition of transactions/cooperation with Chinese organizations/individuals, and entry bans for mainland China, Hong Kong, and Macau.
*   **Context:** Cited remarks by Paraguay’s President Peña and Belize’s Prime Minister Briceño at the UN General Assembly supporting Taiwan’s participation in the UN, and visits by Taiwan’s foreign minister to these countries.
*   **Source:** Article #402 (Op-ed by Lu Daxin), Article #403 (Page editor content).

### 3. International Historical Commemoration

*   **Criticism:** The statement criticized Japan’s right-wing forces for denying aggression and the Japanese government for accelerating "re-militarization" (increasing military spending, revising security documents).
*   **Source:** Article #404.
