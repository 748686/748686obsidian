# Event Name

**Xinjiang Daoxiang Village: Development of Rice-Based Rural Tourism Economy**

## Event Overview

This event unit focuses on the socioeconomic transformation of Daoxiang Village (formerly Ayakedun) in Hotan County, Xinjiang, China. The village has transitioned from traditional subsistence rice farming to a concentrated agricultural and rural tourism model. Key developments include the standardization of farmland, the creation of a local "Gold Silk Rice" brand, and the integration of cultural tourism elements such as agritainment, music venues, and sightseeing activities. This transformation is supported by the "Hundred Counties, Thousand Towns, Ten Thousand Villages" (百千万工程) initiative and Beijing’s assistance program for Xinjiang. By 2025, these efforts resulted in significant income growth for both the village collective and individual households, positioning Daoxiang as a notable example of rural revitalization on the margins of the Taklamakan Desert.

## Core Facts

*   **Location & History:** Daoxiang Village is located in Baihe Town, Hotan County, Xinjiang. Its former name, Ayakedun, means "last sand mound" in Uyghur. The village sits near the Taklamakan Desert and benefits from natural water sources from the Karakash and Yulongkash rivers, which have supported rice cultivation for over a century despite surrounding sand dunes 5–6 meters high.
*   **Agricultural Transformation:**
    *   **Land Standardization:** Abandoned lands were reclamation and leveled to create 1,800 acres of concentrated, contiguous rice paddies.
    *   **Yield Increase:** Average rice yield increased from approximately 300 kg/acre to 600 kg/acre due to improved seed selection and soil improvement.
    *   **Branding:** The village established the "Gold Silk Rice" (金丝米) brand.
    *   **Land Leasing:** Villagers lease land to agricultural companies at 600 yuan/acre annually, which is roughly 400 yuan/acre more profitable than self-cultivation.
*   **Economic Impact (Reported):**
    *   **Village Collective Income:** Increased by more than 1.1 million yuan annually due to rice production and land leases.
    *   **Household Income:** Average per-capita net income rose from 9,800 yuan (pre-renovation) to over 14,800 yuan by 2025.
    *   **Tourism:** In 2025, the village received approximately 35,000 tourists.
*   **Tourism & Cultural Development:**
    *   **Support Mechanism:** In 2021, the Beijing Xinjiang Assistance Taskforce provided funding and strategic positioning, designating the village as a "rural complex cultural village" focusing on "rice field +" tourism.
    *   **Facilities:** New amenities include courtyard restaurants (agritainment), a courtyard music venue, a public cultural space called "Daoxiang Academy" (library), and hot-air-balloon sightseeing.
    *   **Infrastructure:** Village roads were renamed to reflect rice themes (e.g., Daoxiang Road, Rice Field Road) to enhance brand identity.
*   **Policy Context:** The project aligns with the national rural revitalization strategy and specifically the "Hundred Counties, Thousand Towns, Ten Thousand Villages" (百千万工程) high-quality development project launched in late 2022, which aims to address urban-rural imbalances in Guangdong and other regions, though the specific application in Xinjiang leverages Beijing’s assistance programs.

## Event Conclusion

The development of Daoxiang Village in Xinjiang represents a successful model of "agritainment" and rural revitalization on the desert frontier. By combining high-standard farmland construction (yield doubling) with Beijing-funded cultural tourism infrastructure, the village has significantly improved economic indicators. The shift from subsistence rice farming to a branded "Gold Silk Rice" product and a 35,000-visitor annual tourism destination demonstrates effective integration of agricultural output with service-sector income. The event highlights the efficacy of inter-provincial assistance programs in transforming remote, geographically challenging regions into economically vibrant rural hubs, with measurable improvements in household income and collective assets between 2021 and 2025.
