### [Improving GRAFT with LoReFT Adapters](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/B7LnjyJQnkgwKdtTx/loreft-graft-better-at-preventing-rl-grift) ⭐️

rss · LessWrong AI风险社区 · Oct 24, 01:15

**「Background」** GRAFT mitigates reward hacking during reinforcement learning by training separate FORGET and RETAIN adapters in MLP layers, then ablating the FORGET adapter after training. While effective, the original method requires an anchor environment to stabilize performance and uses a relatively large adapter footprint.

**「Solution」** Knoche replaces GRAFT&\#x27;s MLP FORGET adapter with a single-layer rank-1 LoReFT adapter at layer 20, using only 0.029% of the original parameters. On Qwen3-8B in the Countdown-Code RLVR environment across 8 seeds, this approach achieves equivalent reward-hack suppression while improving clean solve rates and eliminating the need for an anchor environment. Six of eight seeds showed zero hacking behavior. The author suggests this works because LoReFT starts as a no-op and learns the FORGET direction on-policy, unlike fixed steering vectors derived from off-policy activation differences. Ablations indicate the FORGET adapter operates primarily on layers just before where the reward hacking steering vector is most effective in the residual stream—behaving similarly to "safety layers"—while honest solve improvements are more distributed across layers.

**「Takeaway」** A single rank-1 LoReFT adapter can absorb reward-hacking behavior more efficiently than GRAFT&\#x27;s MLP adapters, providing evidence that such behaviors are mediated by single directions in the residual stream and that on-policy ReFT approaches outperform fixed steering vectors when task gradients are available.

### [Simulated Control Evaluations for AI Safety](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/uXiqyoKwpfjWfQXk5/simulated-control-evaluations) ⭐️

rss · LessWrong AI风险社区 · Oct 23, 22:16

**「Background」** AI developers increasingly deploy safety monitors to catch agents attempting harmful actions, but evaluating these monitors in realistic conditions is difficult because actual deployments cannot be safely attacked and building exact sandbox replicas of every codebase and service is impractical. Standard control settings like BashArena or LinuxArena provide only indirect evidence about monitor performance in real-world deployments, where threats may depend on specific codebases, tools, and workflows that simplified environments do not represent.

**「Solution」** SCEs adapt LLM environment simulation to control evaluations: starting from transcripts of agents working in a developer&\#x27;s actual codebase, an attacking model attempts harmful objectives while its tool calls are simulated rather than executed by an LLM environment simulator that generates plausible tool outputs. An LLM attack success judge then estimates whether the attack would have succeeded based on the simulated trajectory, harmful objective, and side-task scoring logic. The authors validated this approach on LinuxArena by comparing simulated results against real executions, finding that behavioral divergence measured via KL divergence across attack strategies, attacker reasoning, and monitor reasoning was more than an order of magnitude lower for SCEs than for BashArena and comparable to variation between repeated real runs. Safety predictions using SCEs achieved a root-mean-square error of 3.6 percentage points compared with 10.1 for BashArena at a 5% audit budget, and simulation samples less than 5% the size of a full deployment beat BashArena&\#x27;s full prediction, indicating strong sample efficiency.

**「Takeaway」** SCEs offer a high-fidelity, sample-efficient bridge between simplified control evaluations and real deployment contexts, substantially improving how well monitor performance in safe simulations predicts actual deployment safety, though they still require extrapolation beyond verifiable settings and have not yet been extended to long-horizon multi-agent attacks.
