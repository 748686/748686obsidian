### [South Africa&\#x27;s rooibos: from wildcraft to cultural heritage](http://paper.people.com.cn/rmrb/pc/content/202611/06/content_30189737.html) ⭐️ ?/10

A 2026-11-06 People&\#x27;s Daily feature traces rooibos, a caffeine-free botanical drink from South Africa&\#x27;s Cederberg region, from wild-harvested traditional knowledge to modern commercial cultivation and diversified consumption. Originally used by the indigenous Khoisan, the plant was first recorded by Swedish botanist Carl Peter Thunberg in the 1770s and later commercialized by Russian immigrant Benjamin Ginsberg, who introduced it internationally in London in 1907. A 1920s breakthrough in seed treatment enabled the first commercial plantations in the 1930s, after which production shifted from wild collecting to farming. The article also notes that South African government executive chef Brett Lazare prepared rooibos for Nelson Mandela, and that a 2019 agreement requires processors to pay an annual fee equal to 1.5% of farm purchase prices to support Khoisan knowledge preservation and community development.

**「Background」** Rooibos is made from Aspalathus linearis, a leguminous shrub native to a small part of the Western Cape, and its infusions resemble tea but are not made from Camellia sinensis. The Cederberg&\#x27;s nutrient-poor, well-drained sandstone soils and summer-dry climate favor this hardy plant, while traditional processing involves bruising, fermenting, and drying the shoots.

**「Impact」** The piece highlights how rooibos production now reaches beyond bulk teas into café formats such as rooibos espresso concentrates and caffeine-free red cappuccinos, reflecting growing consumer demand for tea alternatives. It also documents a structured benefit-sharing mechanism that channels industry revenue to Khoisan heritage conservation, though the scale of measurable community impact is not quantified in the article.
