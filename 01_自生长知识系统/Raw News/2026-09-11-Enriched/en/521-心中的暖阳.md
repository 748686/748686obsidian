### [Chinese Bamboo Flute Secures Prominent International Festival and Market Presence](http://paper.people.com.cn/rmrb/pc/content/202609/11/content_30180481.html) ⭐️ ?/10

In July 2026, Chinese bamboo flute players performed alongside the Kyrgyz State Symphony Orchestra at the SCO International Film Festival in Kyrgyzstan, and in August the 14th Cévennes Mountain Music Festival in La Salce, France, included the bamboo flute in its core performance program for the first time. These milestones cap a long international trajectory: the instrument traces back over 8,000 years to the Jiahu bone flutes in Henan, was depicted in Dunhuang Cave 112 murals, won a gold medal at the 1915 Panama-Pacific International Exposition, and has appeared in Western media such as the opening theme of "Kung Fu Panda." The production hub in Zhijing Village, Hangzhou—home to fewer than 3,000 residents and more than 160 enterprises—manufactures roughly 4.5 million flutes annually and holds approximately 85 percent of the global performance-bamboo-flute market. The article, written by Zhong Xuezheng of the Hangzhou Branch of the National Version Library, frames the flute&\#x27;s journey from "going out" to "staying" through the growth of overseas Chinese traditional-music academies.

rss · 人民日报\(People&\#x27;s Daily\) · Sep 11, 00:26

**「Background」** The Chinese bamboo flute \(zhudì\) has a documented lineage stretching from the Jiahu bone flutes of approximately 8,000 years ago and the Warring States-era "Records of Lüshi" account of the music official Ling Lun cutting bamboo to establish the twelve-tone scale, through Dunhuang cave paintings showing cross-piped flutists, to the modern period in which it has become a recognized voice in both classical concert and popular global music.

**「Impact」** The first-time inclusion of the bamboo flute in the core program of a long-standing French classical-music festival indicates that Chinese traditional wind instruments are being integrated into established Western concert programming rather than confined to cultural-exhibit or novelty slots, reinforcing the commercial and artistic position of the Zhijing production cluster that supplies roughly 85 percent of global performance flutes.
