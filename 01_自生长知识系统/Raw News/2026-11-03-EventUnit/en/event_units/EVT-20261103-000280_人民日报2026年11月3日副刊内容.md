**People's Daily Supplement Section Discourse on Media, Traditional Arts, and Acting (November 3, 2026)**

## Event Overview
On November 3, 2026, the 20th page (Supplement) of *People's Daily* published a collection of six articles responding to a recent essay by writer Wang Meng titled "Running a Lively Supplement" (办气韵生动的副刊). The articles collectively explore the current state, challenges, and future directions of Chinese cultural journalism, traditional performing arts (specifically puppet and shadow theater), film criticism, short drama creation, and actor training standards. The event marks a significant public discourse on "New Mass Literature and Art" (新大众文艺) and the implementation of the policy of "Creative Transformation and Innovative Development" (两创).

## Core Facts

**1. Discourse on Newspaper Supplements (Sub-Genre: Media Criticism)**
*   **Trigger:** Wang Meng's article "Running a Lively Supplement" published recently in *People's Daily*, which sparked widespread discussion across media.
*   **Key Insight:** The core argument is that the "root" of a supplement lies with its readers. The concept of "vivid aura" (气韵生动) is defined as the resonance between editors, authors, and readers, who must all be seen as "living people."
*   **Reader Feedback:** Netizen comments highlight that supplements are like "salt in cooking" or "the refrain in a song," providing lingering aftertastes that hard news does not. Readers expressed nostalgia for famous supplements such as *Dadi*, *Zhaohua*, *Yeguangbei*, and *Wuse Tu*.
*   **Critiques:** There is dissatisfaction with current trends, including homogeneous writing styles, limited subject matter (often restricted to seasonal produce), and a reliance on a closed circle of familiar authors.
*   **Digital Transition:** In the digital intelligence era, supplements must evolve from static reading to "co-creation" (读编共创), providing interfaces for reader expression and participation.

**2. Puppet and Shadow Theater Revitalization (Sub-Genre: Traditional Culture)**
*   **Event:** The "Hundred Regional Puppet Rhymes, Artistic Alliance Across Miles" (百域偶韵·艺联万方) fine performance exhibition, featuring five major categories of shadow and puppet arts from across China.
*   **Case Studies:**
    *   *Sichuan Zizhong County Puppet Troupe:* "The Adventures of Young Zhang Daqian" expanded themes from fantasy to historical/social commentary (Republic of China era).
    *   *Yangzhou Puppet Research Institute:* "Classic of Mountains and Seas: The Dream of Juling" merged classical texts with sci-fi narratives for all-age audiences.
    *   *Huazhou Shadow Puppetry:* "Justice-Doing Nezha" utilized large screens and multimedia while retaining traditional镂空敷彩 techniques; supported by provincial troupes and National Arts Fund projects.
    *   *Quanzhou Thread Puppet:* "Zhong Kui Drunk" and "Taming Monkeys"; achieved ~800 performances annually through公益演出 (public welfare performances), subtitle integration, and classic repertoire restoration.
    *   *Sichuan Giant Puppet Theatre:* "Dragon Gate Legend" showcased 1.8-meter giant puppets, a style previously featured in the 2018 PyeongChang Winter Olympics "Beijing 8 Minutes."
*   **Industry Challenges:** Aging inheritors, scarcity of composite talents, regional imbalance, and insufficient self-sustaining capacity for some troupes.

**3. Film Criticism: *The Secret Archives* (Sub-Genre: Film Review)**
*   **Subject:** A review of the spy thriller film *The Secret Archives* (密档) by critic Gong Jinping.
*   **Analysis:** The film breaks from the tradition of *The Eternal Wave* (1958) by focusing on the domestic, daily lives of spies in a Shanghai Shikumen residence rather than just high-stakes action.
*   **Technique:** Uses montage to foreshadow tragedy, making mundane interactions appear more precious and fragile. It portrays a spectrum of political attitudes among tenants (compromisers, escapists, radicals) during the Anti-Japanese War.
*   **Significance:** Represents a "daily life-ization" (日常化) approach to the spy genre, expanding the boundaries of the genre through social realism.

**4. Short Drama Critique: *Iron Blood Messenger* (Sub-Genre: Drama Review)**
*   **Subject:** A review of the red-themed short drama *Iron Blood Messenger* (铁血信使) by Zhang Dexiang.
*   **Plot:** Based on the historical figure Xiang Yunian, a traffic officer for the Central Special Investigation Section carrying the "Iron Bucket Plan" intelligence to Ruijin.
*   **Critique:** The drama relies on suspense and reversals. The reviewer notes that while the design of conflicts is strong, the ultimate success lies in character creation and the revelation that revolutionary victory depends on the support of the people.

**5. Acting Industry Commentary: The "Huang Pangzi" Dilemma (Sub-Genre: Opinion)**
*   **Author:** Feng Yuanyuan, President of the Beijing People's Art Theatre.
*   **Issue:** Difficulty in casting the role of "Huang Pangzi" from the play *Teahouse*, reflecting a broader trend where art academy graduates are uniformly tall and handsome, lacking physical diversity.
*   **Argument:**
    *   **Anti-Homogenization:** Over-emphasis on "颜值" (looks) leads to aesthetic uniformity. Diversity in physical appearance is necessary to match diverse character personalities (e.g., Liang Guan Hua’s unique humor in playing Wang Lifa).
    *   **AI vs. Human Performance:** AI-generated short drama characters are visually perfect but "lack breath/life." Real acting requires the weight of experience and physical presence (cited Li Xuejian’s expressionless but powerful performance).
    *   **Professional Cultivation:** Advocates for "cultural accumulation" beyond academic scores. Described methods used at the Beijing People's Art Theatre, such as reading scripts 24 times over 12 days to internalize nuances (e.g., the word "蹭蹬" vs. "坎坷") and studying historical etiquette/archaeology.
