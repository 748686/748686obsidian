# Event Name

China's Continued Promotion of International Disaster Prevention and Mitigation Cooperation

## Event Overview

In November 2026, Chinese media reported on China's sustained efforts to deepen international cooperation in disaster prevention and mitigation. The coverage highlights specific operational collaborations with Djibouti, Thailand, and Pacific Island nations, alongside high-level policy commentary emphasizing the need for global synergy, technological empowerment, and the shift from reactive rescue to proactive risk governance.

## Core Facts

*   **Strategic Direction:** China is advancing international disaster prevention cooperation through practical collaboration in monitoring and early warning, emergency response, scientific and technological research, technology sharing, and talent training (Article #410).
*   **"Mazu" Smart Meteorological Solution:**
    *   Launched in 2025 by the China Meteorological Administration (CMA) to support the UN's "Early Warnings for All" initiative (Article #409).
    *   **Djibouti Deployment:**
        *   Version 1.0 was donated in July 2025 and deployed by the Shanghai Meteorological Bureau, going live on December 18, 2025. It provides 24-hour advance warnings for extreme weather using AI and Fengyun satellite data (Article #409).
        *   Version 2.0 was delivered in July 2026. It features a forecast spatial resolution improvement from 9km to 3km, an extended forecast lead time of 3 days, and hourly updates every 6 hours. It integrates monitoring terminals to create an integrated "monitor-report-warning" solution (Article #409).
    *   **Solomon Islands Application:** The "Mazu" customized version has also been applied in the Solomon Islands, covering the full chain of monitoring, forecasting, and warning (Article #409).
*   **China-Thailand Coastal Zone Cooperation:**
    *   A decade-long partnership (since 2016) between China’s Third Institute of Marine Geology (under the Ministry of Natural Resources) and Thailand’s Department of Mineral Resources (Ministry of Natural Resources and Environment) (Article #409).
    *   Activities include 11 joint surveys in Chonburi Province, establishment of a geomagnetic monitoring station at Mahidol University’s Kanchanaburi campus, and environmental geological monitoring in Satun World Geopark (Article #409).
    *   Recent expansion includes explorations with the Nanjing Center of China Geological Survey for urban geological investigations in Bangkok to address land subsidence and waterlogging (Article #409).
*   **Pacific Island Nations Collaboration:**
    *   **Institutional Platform:** The Innovation Center for Climate Change Applicable Technologies for Pacific Island Countries was established in April 2026 during a cooperation week in Fiji (Article #409).
    *   **Specific Projects:**
        *   **Fiji:** Promotion of Pampas grass (巨菌草) ecological slope protection technology to prevent landslides (Article #409).
        *   **Vanuatu:** Operation of a joint marine observation station built by China; construction of emergency shelters in outer island communities (Article #409).
        *   **Satellite Support:** Continuous provision of Fengyun satellite data for tracking volcanic ash (Tonga), earthquakes (Vanuatu), landslides (Papua New Guinea), and tropical cyclones (Article #409).
    *   **Policy Framework:** The 2025 initiative released at the 3rd China-Pacific Island Countries Foreign Ministers' Meeting proposes high-frequency meteorological disaster monitoring via Fengyun satellites (Article #409).
*   **Emergency Response & Satellite Remote Sensing:**
    *   China has provided over 1,000 pre- and post-disaster satellite remote sensing images for emergencies in Laos (forest fires), Pakistan (floods), Tonga (volcanic eruption), Indonesia (floods/landslides), and Venezuela (earthquake) (Article #410).
    *   In August 2026, China provided satellite imagery for the Nepal debris flow disaster to support search and rescue (Article #410).
*   **Regional Infrastructure:**
    *   The South China Sea Tsunami Warning Center, built by China, has been in operational business for 8 years, providing 24/7 earthquake and tsunami warnings (Article #410).
    *   A visualized system for marine environmental forecasting and disaster prevention in South Asia (Sri Lanka, Bangladesh) supports maritime search and rescue and pollution tracking (Article #410).
*   **Technological Products:** Public products such as the "Mazu" series ocean numerical prediction models, "Jingwei" Beidou multi-parameter intelligent buoys, and the "Kanhai" AI ocean large model have been promoted to neighboring and island nations (Article #410).

## Different Country / Regional Perspectives

*   **China:** Positions itself as a provider of technical solutions, infrastructure, and capacity-building. Emphasizes "blood creation" (capacity building/training) over mere "blood transfusion" (aid). Highlights the integration of AI, big data, and satellite technology into these partnerships.
*   **Djibouti:** Reports tangible benefits in port safety and economic loss reduction due to improved extreme weather forecasting. Values the transfer of operational knowledge, not just hardware.
*   **Thailand:** Focuses on long-term scientific partnership and the application of geological data for urban planning and ecological protection. Seeks to address specific local risks like land subsidence and coastal erosion through joint research.
*   **Pacific Island Nations:** View China as a critical partner for climate resilience given their vulnerability to sea-level rise and extreme weather. Value the dual approach of hard infrastructure (observation stations, shelters) and soft power (training, technology centers). The Pacific Island Development Forum deputy secretary-general noted that mechanized cooperation enhances disaster response capabilities.

## Known Current Impact

*   **Port Safety:** In Djibouti, the "Mazu" system enabled timely warnings during heavy rainfall events in March 2026, ensuring the safety of operations at the Doraleh Multi-Purpose Port.
*   **Scientific Output:** China-Thailand collaboration has produced a series of coastal geoenvironment research results applied to spatial planning and ecological protection over the past decade.
*   **Capacity Building:** Training programs have equipped meteorological personnel from multiple countries with skills in AI-based early warning and urban multi-hazard monitoring.
*   **Disaster Response:** Rapid provision of satellite imagery assisted search and rescue and post-disaster assessment in Nepal (August 2026) and other recent disaster zones.
*   **Infrastructure:** Operational tsunami warning centers and marine observation stations now provide continuous monitoring services in the South China Sea region and Pacific Islands.

## Event Conclusion

China is actively deepening its international cooperation in disaster prevention and mitigation through a multi-vector strategy combining advanced technology (AI, satellites), infrastructure development, and capacity building. Key operational outcomes include the deployment of the "Mazu" smart meteorological system in Djibouti and the Solomon Islands, a decade of coastal geological cooperation with Thailand, and the establishment of new institutional platforms for Pacific Island nations. These efforts reflect a broader shift from reactive aid to proactive, technology-driven risk governance, although structural gaps in global disaster coordination remain.
