# EVT-20261103-000260: Latin American Countries Excavate Potential of the Artificial Intelligence Industry

## Event Overview
On November 3, 2026, *People's Daily* published an "International Perspective" report titled "Latin American Countries Excavate Potential of the Artificial Intelligence Industry," authored by correspondent Shi Yuanhao. The report details how multiple Latin American nations are implementing support policies and initiatives to broaden AI application scenarios, expand digital infrastructure investment, and strengthen regional and international cooperation. The event highlights the region's push toward technological upgrade, economic growth, and increased autonomy in AI development, with significant involvement from Chinese technology partners.

## Core Facts

**1. Economic Potential and Scope**
*   **Growth Projection:** According to a report released by the Inter-American Development Bank (IDB) in 2026, titled "Artificial Intelligence in Latin America and the Caribbean: Development and Application," AI applications are expected to drive a **5.6% increase** in the region's Gross Domestic Product (GDP) by 2030.
*   **Application Domains:** AI applications in the region cover healthcare, education, environmental management, security, and administrative efficiency.

**2. Sector-Specific Applications**
*   **Healthcare:** Brazil announced the establishment of a "National Smart Hospital and Precision Medicine Network" in early 2026. Goals include building smart emergency systems, smart hospitals, and remote nursing systems. Smart intensive care units and medical systems are already operational in some Brazilian cities.
*   **Education:** Uruguay’s "Sebal Plan" is integrating AI into its education system, leveraging nearly 20 years of digital education experience. A teacher training program was launched to help educators use AI for content generation and assessment. As of May 2026, approximately **three-quarters of Uruguayan teachers** reported using AI in teaching practices.
*   **Environmental Management:** In Amazon-covered nations, AI is used for biodiversity protection and detecting environmental changes. For example, the Colombian Air Force partnered with the Hydrological, Meteorological, and Environmental Institute (IDEAM) to integrate satellite imagery with AI algorithms for early warning of deforestation. Similar efforts are active in Brazil and Peru.
*   **Public Administration:** Honduras launched its first municipal AI agent in Central District in August 2026. The agent classifies resident complaints (e.g., potholes, drainage blockages) via chat software and routes them to relevant departments. It is planned to integrate tax payment functions in the future. Mayor Juan Serrano emphasized that AI must meaningfully improve people's lives.

**3. National Policy and Investment**
*   **Leaders:** A 2025 report by the UN Economic Commission for Latin America and the Caribbean (ECLAC) and the Chilean National AI Center places Brazil, Chile, and Uruguay in the "first tier" of AI ecosystem maturity in the region.
*   **Brazil:** Launched the "Brazil AI Strategy" in 2021 and the detailed "Brazil AI Plan 2024–2028" in 2024. The plan allocates **23 billion reais** over four years for supercomputing, Portuguese language model development, and infrastructure to enhance technological autonomy.
*   **Chile:** Released its first "National AI Policy" in 2021 and a revised version in 2024, featuring **177 specific measures** across 14 government departments (including curriculum updates by the Ministry of Education and ethics training by the Ministry of Finance). Chile held its first National AI Awards in early 2026.
*   **Peru:** Approved the "National AI Strategy 2026–2030" in April 2026. Its four pillars are talent/capability building, innovation/entrepreneurship, ethical/regulatory frameworks, and civic participation in technology use.
*   **Other Nations:** Colombia, Costa Rica, Argentina, and Mexico are also actively integrating AI into industry. Mexico and China signed a Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) on scientific and technological innovation cooperation, highlighting AI and supercomputing as key areas.

**4. Regional and International Cooperation**
*   **Latam-GPT:** In February 2026, Chile’s National AI Center led the launch of **Latam-GPT**, the region’s first open-source large language model built on Latin American data. It received support from **65 institutions across 15 countries**, including Uruguay, Brazil, and Colombia.
*   **Institutional Frameworks:** The Southern Common Market (MERCOSUR) established a Digital Agenda Group in 2017. In June 2026, the Third Ministerial and Senior Official Summit on AI Ethics in Latin America and the Caribbean passed the **"2026–2027 AI Ethics Roadmap,"** focusing on governance, talent, digital literacy, and environmental sustainability.
*   **China-Latin America Collaboration:**
    *   Chinese companies **Huawei** and **Alibaba Cloud** are deeply involved in data center and 5G network construction in Chile and Peru.
    *   Brazil announced an investment of **2.5 billion reais** for AI model development infrastructure, including computing centers built in collaboration with **Huawei** and **iFlytek**.
    *   At the 2026 World AI Conference, China announced the construction of an **International AI Application Cooperation Center** for CELAC (Community of Latin American and Caribbean States).
    *   Brazil, Cuba, Venezuela, and Nicaragua joined the World AI Cooperation Organization as founding member states.

## Different Country / Regional Perspectives

*   **Brazil:** Focuses on large-scale infrastructure investment (23 billion reais) and technological autonomy, including Portuguese language models and supercomputing. It is a leader in healthcare AI implementation and a key partner for Chinese tech firms in the region.
*   **Chile:** Positions itself as a policy and governance leader, having established a National AI Center early (2021) and creating a comprehensive framework of 177 measures. It leads regional technical initiatives like Latam-GPT.
*   **Uruguay:** Emphasizes educational integration, leveraging its long-standing digital literacy foundation ("Sebal Plan") to achieve high adoption rates of AI among teachers.
*   **Honduras:** Demonstrates grassroots administrative innovation through the deployment of municipal AI agents for public service and infrastructure reporting.
*   **Peru:** Focuses on strategic planning with a strong emphasis on ethics, regulation, and civic participation in its 2026–2030 strategy.
*   **Regional/Small States:** The IDB report highlights that Caribbean island nations, low-income, and middle-income countries face infrastructure deficits (lack of high-performance computing and broad connectivity) and suggests they rely on neighboring countries' infrastructure services in the short term.

## Known Current Impact
*   **Policy Activation:** Multiple countries have moved from strategy formulation to concrete implementation plans (Brazil, Chile, Peru).
*   **Infrastructure Development:** Significant investments are being directed toward supercomputing and data centers, often involving international partnerships (notably with China).
*   **Regional Integration:** The creation of Latam-GPT and the passing of the 2026–2027 AI Ethics Roadmap indicate a shift toward regional cooperation and shared technological standards.
*   **Public Sector Transformation:** AI is already operational in public services in countries like Honduras (municipal management) and Brazil (smart hospitals), suggesting early-stage societal impact.
*   **Educational Integration:** Uruguay reports widespread adoption of AI in teacher workflows, indicating a rapid shift in educational practices.

*   **Long-term Economic Outcomes:** While the IDB projects a 5.6% GDP increase by 2030, the actual realization of this growth depends on future implementation success and is not yet verified.
*   **Effectiveness of AI Agents:** The long-term efficacy and user satisfaction of municipal AI agents (e.g., in Honduras) are not yet quantified.
