# EVT-20261103-000255: Cultural Resonance Surrounding the Long March Theme

## Event Overview

On November 3, 2026, in conjunction with the 90th anniversary of the victory of the Chinese Workers' and Peasants' Red Army's Long March, significant cultural phenomena were reported in *People's Daily*. The event encompasses two complementary dimensions: the successful broadcast of the major revolutionary historical television drama *The Great Long March* and a journalistic exploration of the spiritual legacy of the "留守" (留守/rear-guard) forces. Together, these reports illustrate how the Long March is understood not merely as a historical geographic expedition, but as a continuous spiritual lineage influencing contemporary social behavior and economic development in China.

## Core Facts

**1. Broadcast Success of *The Great Long March***
*   **Production:** Produced by China Media Group and others, the series is a major revolutionary historical drama.
*   **Narrative Approach:** It is described as the first "panoramic" presentation of the Long March, covering the period from January 1934 to January 1937. It focuses on the Central Red Army while including the First, Second, and Fourth Front Armies, the Twenty-Fifth Army, the Northwest Resistance Army, Southern guerrillas, and the Northern Shaanxi Red Army.
*   **Performance Metrics:** As of early November 2026, live television ratings remained above 3%, with a peak of 3.5%. It held the highest rating among revolutionary题材 dramas in the past two years and led in its time slot across all program types.
*   **Production Scale:** Filming lasted 457 days with six large-scale cross-regional moves. The crew restored over 700 buildings, used more than 300,000 props and 30,000 costumes, and involved over 40,000 extras. The Zunyi Conference site was replicated at a 1:1 scale with embedded life-like scenes (rice shops, noodle shops, etc.).
*   **Preparation:** The scriptwriting team spent over five years, reviewing over 30 million characters of materials and producing six drafts totaling over 700,000 characters.

**2. Audience Engagement and Youth Interaction**
*   **Digital Participation:** Topics such as "Danmu check-in at Zunyi Conference" and "Young people taking notes while watching" frequently trended on social media.
*   **Behavioral Shifts:** Young viewers (including Post-00s) moved from passive reading to active participation, characterized as "online attending meetings" via danmu, self-organizing historical research, and visiting red tourism sites (e.g., Zunyi, Guizhou).
*   **Emotional Impact:** Episodes depicting the Xiang River Campaign and Zunyi Conference triggered widespread emotional responses on social platforms, with comments describing the content as "tear-jerking" and "shocking."

**3. The Spirit of the "留守" (Rear-Guard) Forces**
*   **Historical Context:** Following the main force's departure,留守 troops, guerrilla units, and party organizations remained in base areas (notably Southern Jiangxi and Zhejiang) to fight under extremely difficult conditions, mask the main force's retreat, and preserve revolutionary seeds.
*   **Key Figures/Stories:**
    *   **Zhang Ligui:** Former deputy commander of the Shoutai Jingqing County Independent Battalion; captured in 1937, he refused to yield under torture and was executed at age 27.
    *   **Liu Bojian:** Political Director of the Gannan Military Region; captured and executed after refusing to surrender.
    *   **Chen Yulan:** A 106-year-old veteran of the Qiongya Column in Hainan, recalling her service as a courier and the support received from local villagers.
*   **Modern Continuations:** The article draws parallels between historical坚守 (perseverance) and modern efforts:
    *   **Qingyuan County, Zhejiang:** The local pencil factory transformed into a leading eyebrow pencil manufacturer (exporting to Europe and Africa) after overcoming technical bottlenecks, attributed to the "resilience" of the North East Resistance Army that passed through the area.
    *   **Xunwu County, Jiangxi:** Closure of mining operations in 2008 to protect the Dongjiang River source for Shenzhen and Hong Kong, involving the relocation of nearly 2,300 people.

**4. Official and Academic Commentary**
*   **Xi Jinping:** Cited regarding the contribution of留守 forces to the Long March victory and the four sources of the Long March's success (Party leadership, revolutionary will, independence/pragmatism, mass support).
*   **Chen Jin (Chief Screenwriter):** Emphasized "setting the grand pattern from a macro perspective, seeing true feelings in details" and the concept of "filming the Long March with the Long March spirit."
*   **Niu Mengdi (Beihang University):** Noted that the drama reflects the "Four Reliances" and that the young age of Red Army soldiers facilitates resonance with contemporary youth.
*   **Dai Qing (Communication University of China):** Analyzed the drama's aesthetic achievement in combining revolutionary realism with revolutionary romanticism through imagery and artistic conception.
