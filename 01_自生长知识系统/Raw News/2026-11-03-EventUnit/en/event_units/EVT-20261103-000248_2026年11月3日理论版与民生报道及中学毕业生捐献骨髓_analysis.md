## Event Analysis: The 22-Year Journey of Life from Shanghai Jinyuan Senior High School

Based on the news reports from *People's Daily* on November 3, 2026, this analysis reconstructs the narrative arc of Shanghai Jinyuan Senior High School's bone marrow donation initiative using the **Hero's Journey** and **Five-Act Structure** frameworks. The "hero" in this context is not a single individual, but the collective community of students, alumni, and the institution itself, embarking on a sustained mission to save lives through hematopoietic stem cell donation.

### Part I: The Hero’s Journey (Narrative Arc of the Collective Protagonist)

**1. The Ordinary World (Establishment of the Status Quo)**
The story begins in the ordinary world of Shanghai education and social responsibility. Before the adventure, the "common world" was defined by the typical high school experience: academic pressure and personal future planning. The baseline for heroism was unknown to the general public; stem cell donation was often shrouded in medical mystery and fear (e.g., the misconception of spinal extraction).

**2. The Call to Adventure (The 2004 Initiative)**
The inciting incident occurred in **2004**. Twenty-eight senior students at Jinyuan Senior High School received a call to transcend their ordinary roles. They joined the China Stem Cell Donors Registry as part of a "coming-of-age ceremony." This was the moment the collective protagonist accepted the challenge to become "saviors" for strangers, marking the end of their passive existence and the beginning of their active mission.

**3. Refusal of the Call (Internal and External Resistance)**
The journey was not without its initial refusals and fears. The story of **Zheng Qinwen (Class of 2007)** illustrates this phase vividly. When he received the match notification in 2010, his father initially opposed the donation. This represents the universal human instinct to reject danger and preserve one's safety. Additionally, the "low probability of matching" mentioned by **Lu Xiaoyou** (Putuo District Red Cross Society) serves as a systemic barrier, making the call seem distant and perhaps futile to many.

**4. Meeting the Mentor (Guidance and Knowledge)**
The "mentors" in this narrative are the institutional and medical guides who empower the heroes. **Zhang Yi**, Deputy Director of the Shanghai Red Cross Society and Director of the Shanghai Sub-depot of the China Stem Cell Donors Registry, acts as a key mentor, providing the organizational framework. Furthermore, **Nurse Lu Guihua** from Changhai Hospital serves as a scientific mentor, demystifying the procedure by explaining that "there is no needle in the spine," thereby equipping the students with the knowledge needed to overcome their fears.

**5. Crossing the First Threshold (The Decision to Donate)**
The point of no return is crossed when the match is confirmed and the donation proceeds. For **Zheng Qinwen**, this threshold was crossed after his sister asked his father, "Would you support me if I needed bone marrow?" This emotional catalyst moved him from hesitation to action. For the collective, every successful donation since 2004 represents a crossing of this threshold into the "special world" of life-and-death intervention.

**6. Tests, Allies, and Enemies (The Ongoing Struggle)**
Over 22 years, the protagonists have faced numerous tests. **He Yishan and Liu Yuhao (Class of 2026)** face the test of balancing urgent personal plans (postgraduate exams) with the urgency of saving a life. The "enemy" is not a villain, but the disease itself and the logistical challenges of time management. Their "allies" include their parents, employers (like **Meilong Experimental Middle School** supporting **Lu Yuxin**), and the medical teams.

**7. Approach to the Innermost Cave (Preparation for Critical Donation)**
As donors prepare for the actual procedure, they enter the "innermost cave" of vulnerability. This involves medical screenings, mobilization of stem cells, and the psychological preparation to undergo a procedure that, while safe, is inherently invasive and carries risk. The case of **Lu Yuxin (Class of 2019)**, the 32nd donor, represents the culmination of this approach, where personal sacrifice meets professional responsibility.

**8. The Ordeal (The Climax of Sacrifice)**
The ordeal is the donation event itself. For Zheng Qinwen, it was becoming the 182nd successful donor in Shanghai. For each of the 32 alumni, the ordeal is the moment they give a part of themselves to save a stranger. It is a symbolic death of the self-centered individual and a rebirth into a role of profound social contribution. The "death" of their own comfort for the "life" of another.

**9. Reward (The Seed of Life)**
The reward is the successful transplantation of "seeds of life" (hematopoietic stem cells) into patients with blood diseases. Beyond the physical act, the reward is spiritual: the affirmation of value, the strengthening of community bonds, and the recognition of Jinyuan Senior High School as a model for cultivating correct values.

**10. The Road Back (Integration and Reflection)**
After the donation, the heroes return to their ordinary lives—students to their studies, alumni to their careers. However, they are changed. They carry the experience of having saved a life. The "road back" involves integrating this heroic identity into their everyday existence, as seen in Lu Yuxin’s continued service as a teacher.

**11. Resurrection (Final Transformation of Society)**
The ultimate resurrection is the transformation of the social fabric. The story has evolved from 28 students to **5,603** registry members and **32** successful donations. This is not just an individual achievement but a societal rebirth where altruism and scientific literacy become ingrained in the school’s culture. The "resurrection" is visible in the daily reality of Shanghai, where 800+ successful non-relative donations have occurred since 1996.

**12. Return with the Elixir (Sharing the Gift)**
The "elixir" is the proven model of Jinyuan High School, which can be replicated elsewhere. The elixir is also the awareness and courage instilled in every donor. By sharing their stories, these graduates offer hope and practical knowledge to others, encouraging a new generation to join the cause.

### Part II: The Five-Act Structure (Dramatic Progression of the Event)

**Act I: Exposition (The Foundation)**
*   **World Building:** The setting is established as Shanghai, specifically Putuo District, with a focus on Jinyuan Senior High School. The social environment values traditional virtues combined with modern scientific understanding.
*   **Character Introduction:** We are introduced to key figures: Zheng Qinwen (the pioneer), Lu Yuxin (the current graduate), and He Yishan/Liu Yuhao (the new recruits). The institutional characters, such as Zhang Yi and Lu Xiaoyou, are also established as supports.
*   **Potential Conflict:** The underlying tension between individual convenience and the moral imperative to save lives is hinted at through the father’s initial opposition and the students’ exam pressures.

**Act II: Rising Action (The Expansion of the Mission)**
*   **Conflict Manifestation:** As more students join the registry, the complexity of the mission grows. It is no longer just about signing up; it is about staying ready for a call that could come at any time.
*   **Complicating Factors:** The narrative details the specific hurdles: parental doubts, the fear of medical procedures, and the clash with personal academic schedules.
*   **Raising the Stakes:** The stakes are life and death. With 5,603 people registered but only a small fraction needing a match at any given time, the tension lies in the readiness and the rarity of the event. The success of Zheng Qinwen in 2010 proved it was possible, raising the stakes for subsequent donors to prove that the path was repeatable.

**Act III: Climax (The Peak of Achievement)**
*   **Decisive Moment:** The publication of this article on November 3, 2026, marks the climactic revelation of the full scope of the achievement: **32 successful donations** over 22 years. This number represents the peak of the narrative’s emotional impact.
*   **Maximal Conflict Resolution:** The ultimate conflict—between the fear of donation and the desire to save a life—is resolved by the collective action of 32 individuals. The story of Lu Yuxin, who balanced her job and donation, serves as the final, decisive example that resolves any remaining doubt about the feasibility of the act.
*   **Irreversible Change:** The school is forever changed from a regular high school to a recognized beacon of humanitarianism. The data point of 5,603 registrants is an irreversible change in the school’s history.

**Act IV: Falling Action (Processing the Consequences)**
*   **Handling Aftermath:** The article reflects on the "how" and "why." It explains the medical safety (peripheral blood collection vs. spinal extraction) and the institutional support systems.
*   **Tightening Plot Lines:** The narrative connects the personal stories (Zheng, Lu, He, Liu) with the macro data (Shanghai’s 800+ donations, national registry stats). It also links this grassroots livelihood story to the broader theoretical context of the day’s newspaper (Party building, cultural transmission), showing how individual virtue aligns with national values.
*   **Foreshadowing the End:** The mention of new graduates (Class of 2026) discussing the balance between exams and donation foreshadows the continuation of this tradition.

**Act V: Denouement (New Order Established)**
*   **Final Resolution:** The conflict is fully resolved. The "myth" of bone marrow donation being dangerous or impossible is dispelled. The model of Jinyuan High School is validated.
*   **Revealing Destiny:** The destiny of the 32 donors is sealed as heroes, and their fate is intertwined with the strangers they saved.
*   **Emotional Release:** Readers experience a sense of warmth and pride. The story concludes with the image of these young people not just as students, but as lifesavers.
*   **Establishing New Order:** A new normal is established where stem cell donation is seen as a standard rite of passage for responsible youth in this community, and where the intersection of traditional education and modern medical charity is celebrated as a core cultural value.
