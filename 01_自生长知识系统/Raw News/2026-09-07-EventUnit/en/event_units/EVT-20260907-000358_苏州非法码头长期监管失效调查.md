# Event Name

Investigation into the Long-Term Regulatory Failure of an Illegal Dock in Suzhou

## Event Overview

On September 7, 2026, *People's Daily* published an investigative report titled "Multiple Regulations, Illegal Dock Cannot Be Closed for 4 Years," based on a tip from a netizen via the People.com.cn "Message Board for Leaders." The report investigates Panfeng Renewable Resources Recovery Co., Ltd. (hereinafter "Panfeng Company"), which has been operating an illegal dock at No. 7 Chuyun Road, Gusu District, Suzhou, Jiangsu Province, for years. The facility is accused of secretly transporting untreated construction waste and recycled aggregate. Despite numerous law enforcement actions by the Suzhou Transportation Bureau, the dock has remained operational, highlighted by incidents of violent resistance against officials and a regulatory framework described as fragmented across multiple departments.

## Core Facts

**Operational Details and Evidence of Violations:**
*   **Location and Entity:** Panfeng Company operates at the southern bank of the Beijing-Hangzhou Grand Canal in the Baiyangwan area of Suzhou.
*   **Illegal Status:** The Suzhou Transportation Bureau explicitly stated that Panfeng Company never obtained a port business license for loading/unloading operations, never passed shoreline approval, and conducted no port construction acceptance procedures. The site is classified as an "illegal loading point."
*   **Inspection Findings:** On August 6, 2026, reporters followed a dump truck delivering construction waste to the site. The waste was dumped directly onto a yard next to the dock and loaded onto a vessel ("Yu Xin Huo 11661") via conveyor belt with only superficial removal of plastic bags, lacking the required sorting, crushing, and screening processes defined as "aggregate" by provincial guidelines.
*   **Fraudulent Documentation:**
    *   Vessel "Xiang Yun 69" (stopped in Jingjiang, Taizhou on August 14) presented a product certificate dated August 7 but a third-party test report dated April 16–17, nearly four months prior to production.
    *   Vessel "Li Hang Ji 1662" (caught dumping in Nanxun, Huzhou on August 18) presented a product certificate dated August 15 with a test report dated August 3. Panfeng Company attributed this to a "management loophole" and claiming the wrong report was used.
*   **Scale and Profitability:** Panfeng Company claims to process over 1 million tons of construction waste annually with revenue of approximately 110 million RMB and taxes of 12 million RMB. Industry insiders estimate daily income at ~360,000 RMB based on water transport costs (8 RMB/ton) versus road transport (~45 RMB/ton).

**Regulatory and Legal Context:**
*   **Zone Restrictions:** The dock is located within the "Core Monitoring Zone" of the Grand Canal Jiangsu Section, established by a January 2021 Jiangsu Provincial Government regulation, which strictly protects and utilizes shoreline resources.
*   **Law Enforcement Resistance:** On August 2, 2024, the Port Administration Brigade set up warning signs (iron pillars) 3 meters from the shore. Panfeng Company employees allegedly obstructed enforcement by jumping onto engineering boats, grabbing machinery, and using excavators to remove the pillars. Panfeng’s management admitted to the incident but claimed they were not notified.
*   **Penalties:** Since December 2022, 85 violations involving vessels at this location have been recorded. Only 300,000+ RMB in fines has been collected (average <5,000 RMB per case), which operators describe as a low cost of business.

## Different Country / Regional Perspectives

*   **Central Government Perspective:** The Central Party School (Party School of the Central Committee of the CPC)第九生态环境保护督察组 (9th Central Ecological Environment Protection Inspection Team) had previously flagged issues with the Grand Canal, citing "inadequate ecological space control" and "failure to completely dismantle取缔 certain ports and docks."
*   **Local Administrative Conflict:**
    *   **Suzhou Transportation Bureau:** Claims jurisdiction is limited to vessel behavior (entering ports without reporting) and that they lack authority over shoreline activities, which should be handled by local government.
    *   **Gusu District Urban Management Committee:** Claims jurisdiction is limited to the transport of waste *to* the factory gate, arguing that internal processing is a product quality issue under Market Regulation departments.
    *   **Jiangsu Provincial Authorities:** A provincial staff member stated that the Urban Management Committee *should* regulate the full process of construction waste transfer and processing.
    *   **Gusu District Government:** Deputy District Mayor Wang Jin stated that Gusu District lacks transportation management functions and that the matter should be handled by the Suzhou Municipal Transportation Bureau.

## Information Differences and Conflicts

**1. Responsibility for Regulation:**
*   **Conflict:** There is a disjointed attribution of responsibility among local agencies. The Transportation Bureau deflects to local government for shoreline issues; the District Urban Management Committee deflects to Market Regulation for product quality; the District Government deflects to the Municipal Transportation Bureau for enforcement.
*   **Resolution Status:** The report highlights this as a systemic failure ("multi-headed regulation, split duties") rather than resolving it. A provincial official suggests Urban Management *should* have full-process oversight, but local practice contradicts this.

**2. Validity of Port License:**
*   **Panfeng Company Claim:** Asserted they borrowed the port business license of Suzhou Materials Storage Co., Ltd. from 2018 to 2022.
*   **Suzhou Transportation Bureau Rebuttal:** Stated that port business qualifications cannot be rented/lent and that Panfeng Company never held a valid license. They also noted the underlying land lease explicitly prohibited port operations.

**3. Product Classification:**
*   **Panfeng Company Claim:** All waste is sorted and processed;资质的齐全 (qualifications are complete).
*   **Reporter Observation & External Standards:** The waste shown lacked proper crushing/screening. Provincial guidelines define "aggregate" as requiring specific processing, which was not observed. The presence of plastic bags dumped into the river further contradicts the "processed aggregate" claim.

**4. Timeline of Inspections:**
*   **Reported Discrepancy:** Product certificates showed production dates of August 7 and August 15, while third-party test reports were dated April 16–17 and August 3, respectively. Panfeng attributed this to using the "wrong report," a claim the article presents as insufficient to explain the procedural irregularity.

## Known Current Impact

*   **Environmental Risk:** The illegal dock is located in the Grand Canal Core Monitoring Zone. Untreated waste, including potential hazardous materials like paint residue, risks severe pollution if dumped in water bodies or underground.
*   **Operational Continuity:** Despite being identified as illegal and facing multiple enforcement actions, the dock remains operational as of the report date (September 2026).
*   **Accountability Gap:** The fragmented regulatory environment has allowed the company to operate with minimal financial penalty (300,000 RMB total fines vs. millions in profit), creating a perverse incentive to continue violations.

## What Cannot Currently Be Determined

*   **Ultimate Regulatory Authority:** It remains unresolved which specific local entity has the binding authority to shut down the facility, given the circular deflection between municipal transportation, district urban management, and district government.
*   **Internal Processing Capacity:** While reporters observed superficial handling, it is not definitively confirmed whether Panfeng Company has *any* functional processing capability elsewhere or if the entire operation is purely transshipment of raw waste.
*   **Criminal Liability:** The report notes administrative fines but does not disclose whether any criminal charges have been filed against the company management for obstruction of justice or environmental crimes.

## Event Conclusion

The investigation reveals a persistent case of illegal port operation in Suzhou, China, characterized by a failure of multi-agency coordination. Panfeng Renewable Resources Recovery Co., Ltd. has operated an unauthorized dock on the Grand Canal for at least four years, utilizing fraudulent documentation to disguise raw construction waste as recycled aggregate. The persistence of the facility is attributed to jurisdictional ambiguities between transportation, urban management, and local government bodies, coupled with low penalty costs and active resistance to enforcement. As of September 7, 2026, the dock remains operational, and the report indicates that the *People's Daily* will continue to track the resolution.
