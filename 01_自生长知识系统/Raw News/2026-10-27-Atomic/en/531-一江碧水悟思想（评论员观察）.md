### [China&\#x27;s Heritage &\#x27;Grand Protection&\#x27; Framework Takes Shape](http://paper.people.com.cn/rmrb/pc/content/202610/27/content_30187789.html) ⭐️ ?/10

China&\#x27;s 2026-2030 "15th Five-Year Plan" for cultural heritage preservation aims to establish a comprehensive "grand protection" framework by 2030, according to an October 27, 2026 People&\#x27;s Daily commentary. This approach shifts from isolated monument maintenance to integrated conservation of material and intangible heritage alongside natural landscapes, requiring coordinated action across multiple government departments. Key mechanisms include legal enforcement through administrative public interest litigation, as demonstrated in the Shandong Quyang Grottoes paint contamination case, and technology-driven monitoring systems like Hangzhou&\#x27;s "Liangzhu Ruins 5000+" platform covering 162 square kilometers with digital twin simulation capabilities. The framework emphasizes making heritage sites publicly accessible—as seen in Jiujiang&\#x27;s open, free Yangtze River cultural park section—and promoting living transmission of traditional crafts to everyday communities.

rss · 人民日报\(People&\#x27;s Daily\) · Oct 27, 00:26

**「Background」** China has increasingly emphasized systematic cultural heritage preservation under the concept of "grand protection," moving beyond individual monument conservation to holistic ecosystem-based approaches. The country established national cultural parks along major heritage corridors, including the Grand Canal and Yangtze River, requiring coordination among provincial and ministerial authorities through joint conference mechanisms. Recent high-profile cases of heritage damage, such as unauthorized construction activities contaminating stone carvings, have underscored the need for stronger legal frameworks and proactive judicial intervention in preservation enforcement.

**「Impact」** The grand protection framework establishes binding 2030 targets for integrated heritage conservation while mandating technological modernization of monitoring systems across approximately 162-square-kilometer protected zones. Government agencies must now coordinate across departmental boundaries and ensure open public access to cultural sites, while courts gain expanded authority through administrative public interest litigation to enforce preservation standards.

On October 27, 2026, People&\#x27;s Daily published a photo report marking the 80th anniversary of the Yellow River&\#x27;s transformation from a perilous waterway to a sustainable resource. The piece highlights the establishment of the Jilu Yu Yellow River Water Conservancy Committee by the Communist Party of China in 1946 and notes that the strategy of ecological protection and high-quality development in the Yellow River Basin has been a national priority for seven years. Challenges such as river drying, water pollution, and soil erosion have been addressed, turning the historically difficult "Yellow Dragon" into a source of prosperity and ecological stability.

**「Background」** The Yellow River, often called China&\#x27;s Sorrow due to historical flooding and course changes, is one of the most important yet challenging water systems in the world. The Jilu Yu Yellow River Water Conservancy Committee was a precursor to the modern Yellow River Conservancy Commission, representing the beginning of systematic, state-led management of the river.

**「Impact」** The anniversary coverage underscores the Chinese government&\#x27;s long-term commitment to ecological civilization and infrastructure management, serving as a milestone narrative for policymakers and the public regarding environmental governance achievements.
