### [Drawing on ancient Chinese urban governance wisdom](http://paper.people.com.cn/rmrb/pc/content/202610/27/content_30187813.html) ⭐️ ?/10

A People&\#x27;s Daily opinion piece by Tsinghua University professor Wu Tinghai argues that China should draw on ancient urban construction and governance wisdom to advance the goal of building modern &\#x27;innovative, livable, beautiful, resilient, civilized, and smart&\#x27; people&\#x27;s cities. The article identifies current shortcomings—including imbalanced urban-rural coordination, weak industrial diversification, insufficient cultural integration, inadequate resilience to extreme risks, and gaps in public participation—and proposes five historical principles as guides: hierarchical governance \(&\#x27;层级有序&\#x27;\), promoting commerce and industry \(&\#x27;通商惠工&\#x27;\), nurturing culture and customs \(&\#x27;涵风养俗&\#x27;\), harmonizing humanity with nature \(&\#x27;人与天调&\#x27;\), and ensuring安居乐业 \(&\#x27;peaceful and prosperous livelihoods&\#x27;\). Published on October 27, 2026, the piece connects these traditional strategies to contemporary policy priorities such as urban renewal, the 15th Five-Year Plan, and grassroots digital governance.

**「Background」** China&\#x27;s urbanization is shifting from rapid expansion to stable, quality-focused development, with a policy emphasis on optimizing existing urban stock rather than large-scale territorial growth. The July 2025 Central Urban Work Conference set the target of building modern people&\#x27;s cities that are innovative, livable, beautiful, resilient, civilized, and smart, framing current reforms within both contemporary governance needs and China&\#x27;s historical urban tradition.

**「Impact」** The article provides a policy-oriented framework for urban planners and local governments to integrate historical governance principles into modern urban renewal, industrial upgrading, cultural preservation, ecological planning, and participatory governance initiatives.
