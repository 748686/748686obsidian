Global Disaster Risk Reduction Cooperation and China's Contributions (2026)

## Event Overview
On November 3, 2026, *People's Daily* published an opinion piece titled "Join Hands to Build a More Sustainable Global Safety Security Barrier" in the International Section. The article analyzes the global context of disaster risk reduction under climate change, detailing China's specific cooperative practices in emergency response, monitoring, scientific research, and capacity building, while also critiquing structural shortcomings in the current international governance system.

## Core Facts
Based on the source material from *People's Daily* (Article #400), the following facts are established:

**1. Global Context and Governance Shift**
*   **Context:** Global warming has led to frequent extreme weather and natural disasters. Geological, marine, and cryosphere derivative disasters exhibit high concealment, suddenness, and chain-reaction effects that cross geographical boundaries.
*   **Shift:** Global disaster risk reduction cooperation is transitioning from passive post-disaster rescue to proactive source prevention, normalized monitoring/warning, and systematic comprehensive governance.

**2. China's Cooperative Practices**
*   **Emergency Response:** China has established emergency response mechanisms with African and ASEAN countries via satellite remote sensing. It has provided over 1,000 scenes of pre- and post-disaster satellite imagery for disasters including:
    *   Laos forest fires
    *   Pakistan floods
    *   Tonga volcanic eruption
    *   Indonesia floods/landslides
    *   Venezuela earthquake
    *   **Nepal (August 2026):** Rapidly provided satellite imagery data for landslide support搜救 and post-disaster assessment upon request.
*   **Monitoring and Warning:**
    *   **South China Sea Regional Tsunami Warning Center:** Constructed by China, operating business-normal for 8 years, providing全天候 (all-weather) earthquake/tsunami warning services.
    *   **Pacific Islands:** Continued provision of disaster remote sensing monitoring services.
    *   **South Asia:** Built a visualization system for marine environment forecasting and disaster prevention/mitigation for Sri Lanka and Bangladesh, serving marine forecasting, sea search/rescue, oil spill drifting, and pollutant transport warnings.
*   **Joint Research:**
    *   **ASEAN:** Conducted marine geology basic research and geological disaster comparative studies; deployed mobile seismic stations and land subsidence monitoring stations.
    *   **Central Asia:** Conducted research on glacier change monitoring in the Pamir Plateau based on multi-source satellite observations.
*   **Knowledge and Technology Sharing:**
    *   **China-Pacific Island Countries Marine Disaster Prevention and Mitigation Cooperation Center:** Continuously released reports on "Sea Level Rise in Small Island States under Climate Change."
    *   **Capacity Building:** Collaborated with Maldives and Madagascar on sea level dynamic observation and coastal erosion assessment, shifting focus from "technology blood transfusion" to "talent blood generation" through professional training.

**3. Technological Contributions**
*   China has developed a "space-air-ground-sea" integrated observation and monitoring system.
*   **Key Public Products Promoted to Neighbors/Island Nations:**
    *   "Mazu" series marine numerical prediction models.
    *   "Jingwei" Beidou multi-parameter intelligent buoys.
    *   "Kanhai" AI marine large model.
*   Alignment with UN initiatives: "Ocean Decade," "Decade of Cryosphere Science Action," and "Decade of Ecosystem Restoration." Specific projects include the "Seamless Ocean and Climate Forecast System" and the "Island Governance Toolkit."

**4. Critique of Current Governance**
*   **Structural Shortcomings:** Existence of a North-South technology gap.
*   **Inequalities:** Disparate levels of monitoring equipment, data accumulation, and warning system construction among countries.
*   **Barriers:** Cross-border data sharing barriers remain; unified international monitoring standards and risk assessment norms have not been fully established.
*   **Deficiency:** Existing mechanisms lack the foresight, systematization, and synergy required to adapt to new disaster risk evolution.
