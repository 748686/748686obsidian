## Event Analysis: "Staying Behind is Also an 'Expedition'"

Based on the synthesis of the People's Daily article and the narrative frameworks of the Three-Act Structure and the Hero's Journey, the following analysis interprets the historical and contemporary narrative of the "Staying Behind" (LiuShou) troops as a collective hero's journey.

### I. Three-Act Structure Analysis

The narrative arc follows the classic three-act structure, mapping the historical sacrifice of the Long March era onto the contemporary governance and social transformation in modern China.

**Act I: Setup (The Foundation of Sacrifice)**

*   **Establishing the World:** The story begins in 1934, establishing the historical context of the Central Red Army's departure and the perilous environment of the revolutionary base areas. It introduces the concept of the "Northern Route Anti-Japanese Vanguard" entering Zhejiang and the broader strategy of "staying behind."
*   **Introducing the Protagonist (The Collective Hero):** The protagonist is not a single individual but the collective entity of the *LiuShou* forces—guerrilla units, underground Party organizations, and specific martyrs like Zhang Ligui and Liu Bojian. Their initial state is one of isolation, facing enemies dozens of times their size, cut off from the main force.
*   **Setting the Normal (The Status Quo):** The "normal" for these troops was one of extreme hardship, blockade, and constant threat of death, yet characterized by an unwavering commitment to the revolutionary cause.
*   **Inciting Incident:** The strategic decision and necessity to remain behind after the main Long March force departed. This event disrupted their lives, forcing them into a孤军奋战 (solo fighting) scenario that defined their destiny.
*   **First Plot Point (Crossing the Threshold):** The narrative marks the point of no return for these troops, exemplified by Zhang Ligui’s capture and execution in 1937 and Liu Bojian’s refusal to renounce the Party. They fully committed to their sacrifice, entering the "valley of death" from which there was no return.

**Act II: Confrontation (The Struggle and Transformation)**

*   **Rising Action (Historical Trials):** The narrative details the brutal trials faced by these units. The Northern Route Anti-Japanese Vanguard marched over 140 kilometers and fought for seven days, tying down enemy forces. The Southern Jiangxi guerrillas endured a three-year war against overwhelming odds.
*   **Midpoint (The Spiritual Bridge):** The story shifts from pure historical recounting to a thematic midpoint: the definition of the "Long March Spirit." It is redefined not just as the geographical march, but as a continuous spiritual lineage of resilience, sacrifice for the greater good, and close ties with the people. This redefinition serves as the turning point where history becomes a template for the present.
*   **Allies and Enemies:** The "enemies" are the KMT forces and Japanese invaders; the "allies" are the local people who hid and protected the troops, such as villager Huang Yunfa who aided Chen Yulan.
*   **Second Plot Point (The Crisis/Climax of History):** The ultimate crisis is the near-total annihilation of these forces. However, their survival and eventual contribution to the victory of the revolution represent the overcoming of this crisis. The narrative transitions to how this historical spirit persists today.
*   **Contemporary Confrontation:** The second half of Act II presents modern challenges that mirror the historical ones: economic isolation (Qingyuan’s pencil factory struggling before 2002), ecological pressure (Xunwu’s mining economy vs. environmental health), and social welfare gaps.

**Act III: Resolution (The Legacy and New Normal)**

*   **Climax (The Victory of Resilience):** The resolution is found in the tangible outcomes of the "Staying Behind" spirit. Qingyuan County transforms its industry to dominate 50% of the European eyeliner market. Xunwu County sacrifices economic gain (closing mines) for ecological health, restoring vegetation coverage from 10.2% to 95%.
*   **Falling Action:** The narrative connects these successes back to the historical martyrs. The state’s provision of subsidized elderly care for veterans like Chen Yulan is framed as a reciprocal act of loyalty and care, fulfilling the promise made during the revolution.
*   **Resolution:** The conflict between historical sacrifice and modern prosperity is resolved by establishing that the "Long March" is a perpetual spiritual state. The "Staying Behind" troops are validated as equal heroes to those who marched.
*   **New Normal:** The new normal is a China where the "Long March Spirit" drives both revolutionary memory and contemporary governance—industrial upgrading, ecological stewardship, and social welfare. The theme is升华 (sublimated): loyalty to the Party and people is a continuous expedition.

### II. Hero's Journey Analysis

The narrative of the *LiuShou* troops and the modern regions they inhabit maps closely onto Joseph Campbell’s Hero's Journey, framing their experience as a mythic cycle of departure, initiation, and return.

**Phase 1: Departure**

1.  **The Ordinary World:** The ordinary world for the revolutionary soldiers was the pre-1934 base area life, and for modern residents, it was the traditional economic structures of counties like Qingyuan and Xunwu.
2.  **The Call to Adventure:** For the historical troops, the call was the strategic necessity to remain behind and fight after the main force departed. For the modern narrative, the "call" is the imperative to drive economic transformation and ecological protection as a continuation of revolutionary duty.
3.  **Refusal of the Call:** While not explicitly stated as individual refusal, the inherent danger and likelihood of death served as a massive deterrent. The "refusal" is overcome by the ideological conviction of the participants.
4.  **Meeting the Mentor:** The "Mentor" figures are the historical leaders and the ideological framework itself (Chen Yi’s poetry, Xi Jinping’s commemoration). The mentorship is validated by the citation of Salisbury’s observation that these troops were an "Army of Death," acknowledging their extraordinary status.
5.  **Crossing the First Threshold:** The troops crossed the threshold into the "unknown" when they engaged in the three-year guerrilla war in Southern Jiangxi or when Zhang Ligui was captured and executed. There was no going back to safety; they were fully committed to the path of martyrdom and resilience.

**Phase 2: Initiation (Trial)**

6.  **Tests, Allies, and Enemies:**
    *   *Tests:* Surviving blockades, fighting superior forces, enduring torture (Zhang Ligui), and hiding injuries (Chen Yulan).
    *   *Allies:* Local villagers like Huang Yunfa, who provided critical support, embodying the "fish and water" relationship between the army and the people.
    *   *Enemies:* KMT troops, Japanese forces, and the harsh natural environment.
7.  **Approach to the Innermost Cave:** The "innermost cave" is the ultimate sacrifice. For Zhang Ligui and Liu Bojian, this was their capture and execution. For the modern narrative, it is the difficult decision to close mines and relocate populations (Xunwu) for the greater good of the Pearl River Delta.
8.  **The Ordeal:** The central ordeal for the historical troops was death and resurrection of the cause. Liu Bojian’s statement, "Life is for China, death is for China," encapsulates this moment of facing mortality. For the modern era, the ordeal is the painful but necessary economic and ecological restructuring.
9.  **The Reward (Seizing the Sword):** The reward for the historical troops was the preservation of the revolutionary seeds and the containment of enemy forces, contributing to the overall Long March victory. The reward for the modern narrative is the successful industrial transformation (Qingyuan’s global market share) and ecological restoration.

**Phase 3: Return**

10. **The Road Back:** The narrative brings the historical sacrifice into the present day. The "road back" is the connection between the 1930s martyrs and the 2026 recipient of state care, Chen Yulan. The journey loops back to the origin.
11. **Resurrection:** The "resurrection" is the validation of the *LiuShou* troops' sacrifice. They are not forgotten but are re-integrated into the national consciousness as equal partners in the Long March achievement. The spirit is reborn in the modern achievements of Zhejiang, Jiangxi, and Hainan.
12. **Return with the Elixir:** The "elixir" is the *Long March Spirit* itself—specifically the aspect of "staying behind" for the greater good. This spirit is brought back to the ordinary world (modern China) to nourish it. It manifests as:
    *   *Economic resilience:* The pencil factory’s success.
    *   *Ecological wisdom:* The restored mines in Xunwu.
    *   *Social care:* The meal delivery service for the elderly veteran.

The article ultimately posits that the Hero's Journey never truly ends; the "expedition" continues in every generation, whether through armed struggle or through peaceful construction and sacrifice for the collective good.
