## Event Overview
On October 13, 2026, the People's Daily (Paper edition) published content in its "Comments" section (Page 05) featuring a triptych of commentary and visual reports. The synthesis focuses on two distinct but thematically linked articles regarding national industrial and cultural development:

1.  **Cultural Heritage:** A photograph report titled *"People Are Here, The Opera Remains, The Soul Endures"* (人在，戏在，魂在), highlighting the transmission of traditional opera (specifically Dianju/云剧 as implied by the context of "Dian" in the first-layer merge reason, though the text itself is a caption) and the role of inheritors.
2.  **Manufacturing & Classic Industries:** An editorial *"Let Historical Classic Industries Remain Evergreen"* (让历史经典产业常新长红) discussing the transition of traditional industries (silk, tea, porcelain) and a separate photo report on industrial digitization.

This EventUnit synthesizes the factual claims regarding the policy environment for "Historical Classic Industries," the metrics of manufacturing digitalization, and the cultural commentary on traditional arts.

## Core Facts

### 1. Manufacturing Digitalization Metrics
*   **Index Definition:** The level of integration between informatization and industrialization is defined as a comprehensive index measuring the maturity of manufacturing digitalization, networking, and intelligence transformation.
*   **Growth Data:** According to statistics cited in the report, China's index for this integration rose from **59.6 in 2022** to **67.1 by the end of June 2026** [Source: Article #477].
*   **Technological Trends:** Digital-intelligent technologies are accelerating their penetration into the full chain and all scenarios of manufacturing, fostering new formats such as digital twins and intelligent production.
*   **Visual Evidence:** A photograph from Rizhao, Shandong, shows workers handling automotive coverage orders on a智能化 (intelligent) production line [Source: Article #477].

### 2. Historical Classic Industries Policy & Status
*   **Policy Action:** The Ministry of Industry and Information Technology (MIIT) and five other departments jointly issued the *"Opinions on Promoting the High-Quality Development of Historical Classic Industries"* [Source: Article #478].
*   **Industry Scale:** Unconfirmed statistics indicate that representative enterprises in historical classic industries number **25,000** (above-scale), with approximately **3.5 million** employees and an annual revenue of roughly **8 trillion RMB** [Source: Article #478].
*   **Industry Definition:** "Historical Classic Industries" include silk, tea, porcelain, Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM), brewing, arts and crafts, and the "Four Treasures of the Study."
*   **Key Challenges:** The industry faces issues such as having output but no voice, techniques but no standards, demand but a lack of inheritance, and an inability to command high prices. Brand authority in international high-end markets is insufficient.

### 3. Cultural Commentary on Traditional Arts
*   **Theme:** The segment *"People Are Here, The Opera Remains, The Soul Endures"* emphasizes the human element in cultural transmission, aligning with the theme "Everyone Is a Protagonist" in the context of the Chinese Dream [Source: Article #477].
*   **Global Recognition:** Specific examples of traditional crafts gaining international traction include Hainan Li Brocade and Guangdong Xiangyunsha appearing on international fashion weeks, and Ningxia hemp weaving and Zhejiang Hubi brushes becoming World Cup official authorized products [Source: Article #478].

## Event Conclusion

The October 13, 2026 *People's Daily* commentary section presents a unified narrative of China's industrial and cultural advancement. On one hand, heavy manufacturing continues its digital metamorphosis, evidenced by a rise in the integration index to 67.1 and the proliferation of smart factories. On the other hand, traditional "Historical Classic Industries" are undergoing a strategic repositioning through the newly issued six-department *"Opinions,"* aiming to transform them from low-value raw material exporters into high-value cultural and experiential brands. The synthesis of these two topics underscores a broader national strategy: leveraging technology to modernize tradition, thereby enhancing both economic competitiveness and cultural soft power.
