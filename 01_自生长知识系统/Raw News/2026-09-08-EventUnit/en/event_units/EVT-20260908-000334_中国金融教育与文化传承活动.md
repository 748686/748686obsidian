# Event Name

**2026 China Financial Education and Cultural Heritage Promotion Activities**

## Event Overview

On September 7, 2026, China initiated two significant national-level campaigns focused on financial literacy and the revitalization of traditional cultural industries.

First, the National Financial Regulatory Administration, the People's Bank of China, and the China Securities Regulatory Commission jointly launched the **2026 Financial Education Publicity Week**. The campaign aims to enhance public financial literacy, improve risk prevention capabilities, and safeguard the rights of financial consumers and investors. Its theme is "Clear Financial Network, Guarding Peaceful Consumption."

Second, on the same date, the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology (MIIT) and five other departments released the **"Opinions on Promoting the High-Quality Development of Historical Classic Industries."** This policy explicitly targets the protection, inheritance, and innovative development of traditional Chinese特色 industries with deep cultural roots, specifically silk, tea, and porcelain.

These dual events reflect a broader governmental strategy to integrate economic modernization with cultural preservation, often referred to in official discourse as "Humanistic Economics" (*Renwen Jingjixue*).

## Different Country / Regional Perspectives

The sources highlight three distinct regional models for heritage activation:

1.  **Jiangsu Suzhou (Silk):** Focuses on **industrial transformation and digitalization**. Old factory spaces are converted into cultural tourism destinations and AI/digital innovation zones. The model emphasizes "using economy to nurture culture" and "using culture to stimulate economy" through spatial renovation and tech empowerment.
2.  **Fujian Wuyishan (Tea):** Focuses on **ecological tourism and experiential consumption**. The model leverages natural landscape (Danxia landforms) and intangible cultural heritage (Da Hong Pao processing) to create a "Four Seasons Tea" experience. It combines traditional craftsmanship with modern technology (robotics) for standardization.
3.  **Jiangxi Jingdezhen (Porcelain):** Focuses on **creative IP and global cultural exchange**. The model relies on a robust intellectual property protection framework to attract young domestic and international creatives. It bridges traditional craftsmanship with modern pop culture (e.g., collaborations with major toy brands like Pop Mart).

## Event Conclusion

The September 7, 2026 events mark a coordinated push by Chinese authorities to strengthen both financial consumer protection and the economic valorization of traditional cultural industries. The financial education campaign addresses immediate risks in the digital economy, while the cultural industry policy leverages historic assets (silk, tea, porcelain) in key regions (Suzhou, Wuyishan, Jingdezhen) to drive local economic growth through tourism, technology, and creative IP. These initiatives are unified under the ideological framework of "Humanistic Economics," aiming to demonstrate that cultural heritage and economic development are mutually reinforcing.
