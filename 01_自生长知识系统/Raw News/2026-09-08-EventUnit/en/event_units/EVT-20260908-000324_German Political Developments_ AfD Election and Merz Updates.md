## Event Overview
On September 7, 2026, the far-right Alternative for Germany (AfD) party achieved a historic breakthrough in the Saxony-Anhalt state election, nearly doubling its previous vote share to approximately 44%. This result marks the closest a far-right party has come to regional power in Germany since World War II. The victory coincides with a significant collapse in support for the mainstream governing parties, particularly the Christian Democratic Union (CDU), led by Federal Chancellor Friedrich Merz, whose party fell to roughly 17% of the vote. The event has triggered widespread international attention, political instability concerns, and ongoing coalition talks.

## Core Facts

**Electoral Results in Saxony-Anhalt:**
*   The AfD won approximately 43.8% to 44% of the vote, a figure that more than doubled its previous standing in the region (Guardian, Sep 7).
*   Chancellor Friedrich Merz’s CDU fell to 17.2%, less than half its result from five years prior (Guardian, Sep 7).
*   The Social Democratic Party (SPD) also collapsed, receiving only 9.3% of the vote (Guardian, Sep 7).
*   The liberal-democratic federal coalition lost significant support, reflecting a voter desire for change that does not favor measures strengthening Germany's international competitive position (FAZ, Sep 7).

**Political Reactions and Consequences:**
*   **Chancellor Merz:** Vowed to remain in office despite the landslide victory for the AfD and the shock it dealt to the CDU (Guardian, Sep 7).
*   **Coalition Talks:** The AfD has initiated coalition talks following the victory (France 24, Sep 7).
*   **Business Community:** Business leaders have warned that political instability in Saxony-Anhalt and the AfD’s populist economic proposals could hinder federal reform efforts (FAZ, Sep 7).
*   **International Reaction:** The result has triggered significant international attention due to its implications for German and European politics, with some describing the potential for "absolute chaos" (France 24, Sep 7). Critics have labeled supporters as "idiots and traitors" (France 24, Sep 7).
*   **AfD Context:** The AfD is widely regarded as extremist, with allegations of dabbling in Nazi imagery and promoting divisive definitions of German identity (Guardian, Sep 7).
