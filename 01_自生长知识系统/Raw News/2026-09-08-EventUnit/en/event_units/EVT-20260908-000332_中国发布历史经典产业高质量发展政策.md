# Event Name

China Issues First National Policy for Historical Classic Industries; Concurrent Diplomatic and Infrastructure Developments (EVT-20260908-000332)

## Event Overview

On September 7, 2026, six Chinese ministries, led by the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology (MIIT), issued the "Opinions on Promoting High-Quality Development of Historical Classic Industries." This marks the first national-level policy specifically targeting the innovation and development of China's historical classic industries, which include silk, tea, ceramics, traditional Chinese medicine, brewing, and craftsmanship. The policy aims to modernize these sectors while preserving their cultural essence, with concrete targets for enterprise growth and brand recognition by 2028 and 2030.

Concurrently on September 7, 2026, China's Ministry of Transport released a draft amendment to the Highway Law for public comment to address funding gaps as early toll roads expire. Additionally, from August 30 to September 3, 2026, Chinese President Xi Jinping attended the SCO summit in Bishkek and conducted state visits to Kyrgyzstan and Egypt, drawing extensive foreign media coverage focused on China's diplomatic stance and economic partnerships. China also served as the guest of honor at the 39th Moscow International Book Fair (September 2–6, 2026). German Federal Digital Minister Karsten Wildberger visited the IFA consumer electronics trade show, discussing China's tech lessons for German digital policy.

## Core Facts

*   **Policy Issuance:** On September 7, 2026, six Chinese ministries (including MIIT) issued the "Opinions on Promoting High-Quality Development of Historical Classic Industries."
*   **Scope:** The policy covers traditional industries such as silk, tea, ceramics, traditional Chinese medicine, brewing, and craftsmanship.
*   **Industry Scale:** These historical classic industries collectively account for approximately 25,000 above-scale enterprises, nearly 3.5 million employees, and annual revenues of about 8 trillion yuan.
*   **Targets:**
    *   By 2028: Cultivate 50 billion-yuan-scale leading enterprises and shape 100 iconic Chinese consumer brands.
    *   By 2030: Move the industry toward the mid-to-high end of the global value chain.
*   **Framework:** The policy integrates industrial planning, technological upgrading, cultural-tourism fusion, brand building, and international market expansion.
*   **Highway Law Amendment:** Released on September 7, 2026, for public comment. It addresses funding gaps after early toll roads expire by upgrading toll fee approval authority to provincial governments and clarifying that expired highways may continue charging fees for maintenance if not incorporated into municipal systems or fully covered by fiscal budgets. Terminology updates replace "poor areas" with "less developed areas" and "loans or fundraising" with "incurring debt in accordance with the law."
*   **SCO and State Visits:** From August 30 to September 3, 2026, President Xi attended the SCO summit in Bishkek and visited Kyrgyzstan and Egypt. Foreign media highlighted China's calls for a fairer global governance system, greater Global South representation, and multilateral cooperation on AI, green industry, and digital platforms. Flagship projects like the China-Kyrgyzstan-Uzbekistan railway were emphasized.
*   **Moscow International Book Fair:** Held September 2–6, 2026. China was the guest of honor. Over 30 Chinese publishing houses showcased more than 2,000 titles, including official works by President Xi and "The Governance of China" series in Russian. A five-year agreement was signed to co-translate and publish 50 classic Chinese and Russian works.
*   **German Tech Dialogue:** German Federal Digital Minister Karsten Wildberger visited IFA and discussed learning from China's technology sector. He argued that AI requires both rules and regulatory frameworks rather than regulation alone.
