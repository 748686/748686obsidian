# Event Name
**AI Era Teacher Role and Educational Philosophy Discussion**

## Event Overview
On September 8, 2026, *People's Daily* published a special report titled "In the AI Era, Why are Teachers Irreplaceable?" as part of its series "Four Questions on 'Artificial Intelligence + Education'" focusing on high-quality and balanced educational development. The article features a dialogue with education experts and frontline teachers—including Yu Zheng (Academician, President of Huazhong University of Science and Technology), Wang Shuaiguo (Director of Tsinghua University Center for Online Education), Lu Xiaoyun (Professor, Xi'an Jiaotong University), and Zhao Yong (Principal, Beijing Hongzhi Middle School)—to explore how teachers should adapt to new teaching forms when students can proficiently use AI for learning. The accompanying editorial note, "Accompanying Students from 'Knowing' to 'Understanding'," emphasizes that education requires humanistic care and warmth rather than just speed, positioning the cultivation of character and critical thinking as the irreplaceable role of educators.

## Core Facts
*   **Shift in Teacher Role**: Teachers are no longer merely providers of knowledge or "smart answerers" competing with AI. Their primary role is shifting to being guides for thinking methods and value systems (Source: Yu Zheng).
*   **The "Omitted" Learning Process**: AI models provide complete answers but often omit the struggle and steps necessary for true understanding. Teachers must reconstruct this "omitted" growth process by questioning students and making them confront confusion (Source: Wang Shuaiguo).
*   **From "Answering" to "Solving"**: There is a distinction between obtaining knowledge via AI and the ability to apply it in real-world situations. Teachers design tasks that guide students from merely knowing how to answer questions to being able to solve problems (Source: Lu Xiaoyun).
*   **Evaluation Reform**: Simple AI detection rates are considered insufficient and potentially misleading. Evaluation is shifting toward process-oriented assessment, including examining the student's interaction with AI (prompting, questioning, verifying) and their ability to function independently when AI is absent (Source: Lu Xiaoyun, Zhao Yong, Yu Zheng, Wang Shuaiguo).
*   **Two Types of Competency**: Wang Shuaiguo proposes evaluating two distinct abilities: (1) independent understanding/judgment when AI is absent, and (2) the ability to propose good questions, verify evidence, and take responsibility for human-AI collaboration when AI is present (Source: Wang Shuaiguo).
*   **Policy Context**: The Ministry of Education released the "Guidelines for Primary and Secondary School Students on the Use of Generative AI (2025 Edition)," advising against simple copying of AI content and urging the development of digital intelligence literacy and critical thinking (Source: Zhao Yong).
*   **Upcoming Ministry Initiatives**: The Ministry of Education announced plans to implement an "AI + Teacher Development" action, including a "Ten Thousand AI Literacy Pioneers Plan" and courses on the national smart education platform (Source: Article #504).
*   **Humanistic Core**: The editorial stresses that while AI can generate explanations and visuals rapidly, the "temperature" of education and humanistic guidance are superior to the mere speed of acquiring answers (Source: Article #505).

## Cross-Source Verification
*   **Consensus on Irreplaceability**: All four experts and the editorial team agree that AI cannot replace the fundamental educational role of teachers. The consensus is that AI handles information retrieval and standard answers, while teachers handle value guidance, critical thinking, and emotional/intellectual growth (Sources: #504, #505).
*   **Concern about AI Detection**: Multiple sources explicitly criticize the reliance on AI detection tools ("cat and mouse game"). Lu Xiaoyun, Zhao Yong, Yu Zheng, and Wang Shuaiguo all argue that detection alone is insufficient and that evaluation must focus on the thinking process (Source: #504).
*   **AI as a Tool, Not a Substitute**: Zhao Yong and Lu Xiaoyun both emphasize that AI should serve as a "teaching assistant" or "scaffolding" for exploration, not a replacement for teacher creativity or student effort (Source: #504).
*   **Distinction between Knowing and Understanding**: The gap between "having access to answers" (AI) and "achieving understanding" (Education) is a central theme validated by both the expert dialogue and the editorial note (Sources: #504, #505).

## Different Country / Regional Perspectives
*   **Chinese Educational Policy Context**: The sources reflect a distinctively Chinese policy response to AI in education, balancing technological adoption with ideological and moral formation ("Lide Shuren" - fostering virtue through education).
*   **Institutional Responses**:
    *   *Huazhong University of Science and Technology*: Combines institutional norms (AI detection) with process-oriented evaluation (defenses focusing on raw data reliability).
    *   *Tsinghua University*: Focuses on broad integration, with 500+ courses using AI across 10 functional scenarios (preparation, teaching, Q&A, evaluation, etc.).
    *   *Ministry of Education*: Taking a top-down approach with national platforms, "seed teacher" programs, and standardized literacy training.

## Information Differences and Conflicts
*   **Conflict on AI Detection**: There is a nuanced difference in stance. While all agree detection is insufficient alone, Yu Zheng and Zhao Yong (citing the 2025 Guidelines) support using detection as a boundary-setting tool within a broader system. In contrast, Wang Shuaiguo is more critical, stating that for daily assignments, relying on AI generation rates is not recommended because a low rate does not prove serious thinking, and it creates a "technical攻防" (attack and defense)偏离教育目标 (deviating from educational goals). Lu Xiaoyun also specifically warns against the "cat and mouse game." This represents a spectrum from "regulatory boundary setting" to "active rejection of detection as a primary metric."
*   **No Major Factual Conflicts**: There are no direct contradictions regarding dates, names, or core arguments. The sources are complementary.

## Known Current Impact
*   **Pedagogical Shift**: Teachers are actively redesigning assignments to require the submission of AI interaction logs and focusing on in-class performance to ensure authentic learning (Source: #504).
*   **Policy Implementation**: The Ministry of Education has announced concrete plans to roll out AI literacy training nationwide, indicating an immediate strategic focus on teacher adaptation (Source: #504).
*   **Public Discourse**: The publication in *People's Daily* signals that the debate on AI in education has moved from technical implementation to philosophical and pedagogical redefinition at the national level in China (Source: #504, #505).

## Event Conclusion
The synthesis of the provided articles indicates that in the Chinese educational context as of September 2026, the dominant view is that AI cannot replace teachers because the essence of education lies in value guidance, critical thinking cultivation, and the reconstruction of the learning process that AI algorithms often skip. Experts and policymakers advocate moving beyond simplistic AI detection toward holistic, process-oriented evaluation and the integration of AI as a "thinking scaffold." The official stance, reinforced by the Ministry of Education's upcoming "AI + Teacher Development" action, is that technology must serve the fundamental goal of "fostering virtue through education," ensuring that teachers remain the essential human element providing "warmth" and character formation in an increasingly automated learning environment.
