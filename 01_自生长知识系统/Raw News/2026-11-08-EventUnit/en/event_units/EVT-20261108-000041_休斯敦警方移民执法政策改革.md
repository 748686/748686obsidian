Harris County Sheriff's Office Immigration Enforcement Policy Restructuring

## Event Overview

On November 5, 2026, Harris County Sheriff Ed Gonzalez announced a significant restructuring of the Harris County Sheriff's Office (HCSO) immigration enforcement policies. The new policy, scheduled to take effect on November 13, 2026, limits the HCSO's role in federal immigration enforcement. Key changes include the prohibition of using tax dollars to transport individuals to federal detention centers and forbidding deputies from determining whether individuals are undocumented immigrants. Additionally, the policy requires warrants backed by probable cause and a judicial order for any detainer requests made by U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE).

The announcement was prompted by the federal shutdown, which has left the HCSO liable for legal fees if immigration-related lawsuits proceed while courts are closed. The policy shift follows a national trend of local jurisdictions reducing cooperation with federal immigration enforcement, contrasting with states like Florida that have expanded such cooperation through legislation such as the Parental Rights in Education Act and the Stop WOKE Act.
