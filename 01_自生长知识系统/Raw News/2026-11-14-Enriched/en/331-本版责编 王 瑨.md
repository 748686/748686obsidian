Skies above America’s busiest airports were eerily empty on Friday, while terminals and baggage claim areas overflowed with confused passengers as flights were grounded across the country.

Chicago’s O’Hare, one of the nation’s busiest hubs, was a morass of confusion and frustration. Swarms of passengers attempted to rebook flights on their phones, and airline gate agents pleaded for patience as they repeated that they had no information to share.

Travelers sprawled out on the floor, settling in for what could become a long, uncomfortable night. Some headed to the city for the night, or home, abandoning their travel plans altogether.

More than 1.5 million people were scheduled to board flights departing after the Federal Aviation Administration announced a rare nationwide ground stop on Friday afternoon, according to Cirium, an aviation data firm.

Frank Foto, 34, worried that his family would not be able to leave Chicago in time to board a cruise ship on Saturday in Miami. The trip had been planned months ago to celebrate his mother-in-law’s 60th birthday, he said.

“On the bright side, I suppose it’s better than being stuck at sea — especially with him,” Mr. Foto said, gesturing toward his infant son.

In northern Mexico, Luisa Jiménez quickly realized that the $200 tickets she had bought for a trip to see the Golden State Warriors, her favorite basketball team, play in San Antonio would go to waste. An airline agent confirmed that her flight out of Monterrey International Airport had been canceled.

“People are really upset because there’s no information,” said Ms. Jiménez, 30, as she weighed whether to fly home to southern Mexico, where she lives.

In Orlando, Fla., a family of nine heading back to Cincinnati after a trip to Disney World returned their rental car and checked out of their Airbnb, only to have to get off their flight after boarding.

“He’ll be 8 tomorrow,” Mary Hughes, 44, said of her son, Josiah. “So we need to be home, so he can have his birthday cake — not stuck in Florida.”

She didn’t know how the family could afford to extend its trip. “We don’t have any more money,” she said.

At LaGuardia Airport, passengers aboard a flight to Portland, Maine, were stuck on the ground for about four hours on Friday evening before they were assigned a gate, Becca Freeman, 40, said in a call from the plane.

This was Ms. Freeman’s second attempt to fly to Maine this week. Her first flight on Thursday was canceled because of bad weather.

The outage comes about two weeks before Thanksgiving, one of the busiest times of the year for air travel. On average, U.S. airlines are expected to fly about 2.7 million passengers each day over the holiday period, according to the industry trade group Airlines for America.

In Atlanta on Friday, a line of travelers waiting to retrieve their checked bags extended past three airport entrances at Hartsfield-Jackson International Airport. Many in the line had checked bags before their flights were delayed or canceled. Some had been waiting for over an hour to get them back.

Ashley Van Mills, 45, had arrived at the airport hoping to catch an evening flight to Chicago.

“We’ve been flying for I don’t know how many years, and this doesn’t happen,” she said. “It’s just the unknown that gives you an uneasy feeling.”
