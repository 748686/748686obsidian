## Event Overview

On September 21, 2026, *People's Daily* published two distinct items on its Section 7 ("Reader Letters") page. The first was an investigative report detailing widespread grievances regarding intrusive pop-up advertisements and misleading app mechanics (notably "shake-to-jump" features and fake close buttons) across various digital and physical service scenarios. The second was an official feedback response from the Hezhang County People's Government, confirming the resolution of a previously reported issue involving idle public rental housing in Bijie City, Guizhou Province, triggered by a prior investigative report published on July 20, 2026.

## Core Facts

**Incident 1: Pop-up Advertising Governance Issues**
*   **Nature of Complaint:** Users reported significant inconvenience and safety hazards caused by aggressive advertising practices in mobile applications and IoT devices.
*   **Specific Mechanics Cited:**
    *   **QR Code Traps:** Scanning codes for parking fees, express cabinets (Fengchao, Yun Gui), and vending machines forces users to watch 3–5 second ads before accessing the service. Mis-tapping "skip" often redirects users to shopping apps.
    *   **"Shake-to-Jump":** Apps utilize smartphone gyroscopes with low sensitivity thresholds, causing unintended navigation to ads when users walk or hold their phones.
    *   **Fake Close Buttons:** Many ads lack functional close icons; apparent close buttons trigger further ads, and some require following accounts or downloading apps to dismiss.
*   **Safety and Economic Impacts:**
    *   **Traffic Safety:** Drivers navigating intersections have experienced sudden AI voice prompts and video ads, leading to near-accidents (e.g., a case in Tianjin involving a map software).
    *   **Elderly Exploitation:** Older adults have been tricked into subscribing to insurance or making payments via hidden "contactless payment" activation prompts (e.g., cases in Beijing and Wuhan involving losses of 1,000+ RMB and 199 RMB respectively).
    *   **Child Exposure:** Educational apps (e.g., "Smart Tree") display inappropriate ads and auto-jump to shopping/game apps, disrupting classroom communication.
*   **Economic Drivers:** Lawyers note that pop-up ads have near-zero development costs (using third-party SDKs) but generate high revenue through CPC, CPA, and CPS models. Deliberately poor user experience is sometimes used to force users into paid subscriptions.
*   **Regulatory Landscape:** The *Internet Advertising Management Measures* (effective May 2023) mandate clear one-click closure and fine violators 5,000–30,000 RMB. However, enforcement is hindered by the low penalty relative to profits, complex liability chains between APP operators and ad SDK providers, and the difficulty of cross-platform evidence collection.
*   **Government Action:** On June 9, 2026, China’s Ministry of Industry and Information Technology (MIIT) convened a meeting urging platforms and device manufacturers to standardize information window presentation and promising normalized monitoring and penalties (including app takedowns).

**Incident 2: Revitalization of Idle Public Rental Housing in Hezhang County**
*   **Background:** A *People's Daily* report on July 20, 2026, exposed that over 100 units of public rental housing in Zhu Ming Township, Hezhang County, Bijie City, Guizhou Province—funded by central government grants and completed in 2015—had remained vacant for eight years.
*   **Government Response:** Upon publication, local authorities (Guizhou Province, Bijie City, and Hezhang County) established a special task force under the guidance of the Ministry of Housing and Urban-Rural Development.
*   **Resolution Status (as of September 2, 2026):**
    *   Infrastructure repairs (roads, fire safety, maintenance) were completed.
    *   Building safety was appraised at level Bsu.
    *   Of the 104 vacant units:
        *   10 units were repurposed as temporary resettlement housing for disaster-prone areas in Zhu Ming Town.
        *   94 units were leased via public competitive bidding to local workers, students’ families, and individual business owners.
    *   Selection was completed by August 19, 2026, with leases signed and rent collected.
