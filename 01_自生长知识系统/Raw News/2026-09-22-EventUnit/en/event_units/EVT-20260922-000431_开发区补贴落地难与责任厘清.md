# Event Name
**District Development Zone Subsidy Implementation Difficulties and Responsibility Clarification**

## Event Overview
In September 2026, *People's Daily* (Page 07, "Readers' Letters" section) reported on the failure of national elderly care subsidies to reach disabled seniors in the Jiamusi National Agricultural High-Tech Demonstration Zone (NHDZ) in Heilongjiang Province. The core issue is a structural mismatch between the zone’s status as a non-administrative development zone and its obligation to deliver social welfare policies. While the national subsidy for moderately to severely disabled elderly people exists, local implementation has stalled due to unresolved jurisdictional coordination between the NHDZ Management Committee and the local Fumin City government. This case highlights a broader systemic issue affecting development zones across China, where economic functions are prioritized over social governance, leading to policy paralysis and conflicting administrative mandates.

## Core Facts
*   **Location:** Jiamusi National Agricultural High-Tech Demonstration Zone (NHDZ), located within Fumin City, Heilongjiang Province.
*   **Policy Subject:** National subsidy for moderately to severely disabled elderly persons (approx. 800 RMB/month for consumer subsidies).
*   **Affected Party:** A family in the NHDZ where the father is completely paralyzed and resides in a nursing home. The monthly care cost is approx. 3,000 RMB.
*   **Implementation Status:** Subsidies cannot be utilized in NHDZ nursing homes. Data channels between the NHDZ and Fumin City finance departments remain unconnected as of mid-September 2026.
*   **Institutional Barrier:** The NHDZ Management Committee is a dispatched agency of the Jiamusi Municipal Government, not an administrative division with independent fiscal authority.
*   **Broader Context:** Development zones in China often face a dichotomy of having to manage social affairs without corresponding administrative power or fiscal resources, or being assigned tasks (e.g., safety production, awards applications) for which they lack legal authorization.

## Event Conclusion
The incident in Jiamusi NHDZ exemplifies a structural flaw in China's development zone governance: **权责错位** (misalignment of power and responsibility). Development zones are tasked with social service delivery (subsidies) and regulatory enforcement (safety, certifications) but lack the administrative status, fiscal independence, and legal authority to execute these functions effectively. 

Key conclusions from the reporting:
1.  **Subsidy Blockage:** Central transfer payments for social welfare stall at the interface between non-administrative development zones and territorial governments due to unresolved data and fiscal coordination.
2.  **Systemic Pattern:** This is not isolated. Similar issues exist in Fujian (unauthorized task assignment) and Inner Mongolia (slow functional divestiture).
3.  **Proposed Solutions:** Experts recommend either municipal direct management of social affairs in zones (the "who dispatches, who is responsible" principle) or a clear fiscal redistribution mechanism that compensates territorial governments for assuming social duties previously borne by zones.
4.  **Accountability Gap:** Without explicit legal mandates and performance metrics tied to livelihood issues, coordination between development zones and local governments remains ad-hoc and ineffective.
