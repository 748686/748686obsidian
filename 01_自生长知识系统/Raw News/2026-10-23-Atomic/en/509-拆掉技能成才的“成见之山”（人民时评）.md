### [Art moves from venues to public spaces in China](http://paper.people.com.cn/rmrb/pc/content/202610/23/content_30187293.html) ⭐️ ?/10

A 23 October 2026 People&\#x27;s Daily commentary argues that China is shifting high-quality performances from enclosed theaters into parks, riversides, historic sites and neighborhoods so ordinary residents can encounter art during routine activities. The article cites long-running outdoor programs such as Tianjin&\#x27;s Bridge-side Music Assembly, Beijing&\#x27;s park art season, Tang-dynasty performances in Xi&\#x27;an, and Kunqu in Suzhou, while also noting community-driven stages in Shaanxi, Guizhou, and Shanghai. It frames this as an expansion of public cultural services in which professional artists perform outdoors and residents take part directly, with commercial and nonprofit offerings intended to operate together. The piece concludes that keeping shows running across weekends, seasons and festivals helps art become a durable part of daily life rather than a one-off event.

rss · 人民日报\(People&\#x27;s Daily\) · Oct 23, 00:26

**「Background」** China has been expanding its public cultural service system over recent years, encouraging cultural institutions and local governments to bring performances, exhibitions and heritage activities into neighborhoods, industrial heritage sites and tourist areas. ‘Public cultural services’ in this context refers to state-supported or subsidized cultural programming intended to be accessible to all residents, often emphasizing equity, regular operation, and participation by both professional troupes and amateur community artists.

**「Impact」** The described shift is likely to increase casual exposure to opera, classical music, folk theatre and drama for urban and rural residents who do not routinely attend ticketed theater. The main uncertainty is whether these outdoor and community formats will remain consistently funded and professionally sustained beyond seasonal or promotional cycles.

### [China Import Fair celebrates 70th anniversary](http://paper.people.com.cn/rmrb/pc/content/202610/23/content_30187294.html) ⭐️ ?/10

The China Import and Export Fair \(Canton Fair\), marking its 70th anniversary in October 2026, has grown from an initial 9,600 square meters and 1,200 buyers to cover 1,550,000 square meters with over 300,000 buyers across its 140 uninterrupted editions. It remains China&\#x27;s largest, longest-running, and most comprehensive international trade exhibition, featuring the widest product range and the highest volume of transactions. The milestone highlights the event&\#x27;s enduring role as a critical platform for global trade and economic cooperation.

rss · 人民日报\(People&\#x27;s Daily\) · Oct 23, 00:26

**「Background」** The Canton Fair, established in 1957, was originally designed to promote Chinese exports during a period of limited international engagement. Over seven decades, it has expanded significantly in scale and scope, reflecting China&\#x27;s transition into a major global trading nation.

**「Impact」** The anniversary milestone reinforces the fair&\#x27;s status as a key indicator of China&\#x27;s trade health and global supply chain integration. For exporters and importers, it signals continued access to a concentrated marketplace for sourcing and business development.

### [Jiangsu Games showcase frugal venue strategy with generous tourism incentives](http://paper.people.com.cn/rmrb/pc/content/202610/23/content_30187295.html) ⭐️ ?/10

The 21st Jiangsu Provincial Games recently opened in Lianyungang, utilizing existing venues through renovation rather than new construction and adhering to a "borrow over rent, rent over buy" equipment policy. While this frugal approach may appear stingy, the city compensated with substantial hospitality: over 40 A-level tourist attractions offered free first-time entry to participants, nearly 800 businesses joined consumer promotion activities, and close to 100 second-site viewing locations were established. The editorial argues this combination represents responsible public resource management that prioritizes long-term utility and economic multipliers over short-term spectacle.

rss · 人民日报\(People&\#x27;s Daily\) · Oct 23, 00:26

**「Background」** China has promoted frugal approaches to organizing large-scale sporting events as part of broader efforts to optimize public spending. The Jiangsu Provincial Games is a major regional multi-sport competition held every four years, with Lianyungang serving as host for the 21st edition.

**「Impact」** This model could serve as a reference for future Chinese provincial games and other large-scale event organizers seeking to balance fiscal responsibility with community benefit. The post-games conversion of renovated venues into multi-use sports complexes demonstrates how upfront frugality can generate sustained public value.

### [Rural doctor&\#x27;s backpack carries medicine and trust for 20+ years](http://paper.people.com.cn/rmrb/pc/content/202610/23/content_30187296.html) ⭐️ ?/10

People&\#x27;s Daily published a profile on October 23, 2026, of Bai Huali, a village doctor in Dazhao Village, Duncao Township, Changshun County, Guizhou Province, who has spent over 20 years making daily mountain rounds for dispersed elderly and child residents. The article describes how her fifth wicker basket has progressed from basic tools like mercury sphygmomanometers and reused steel needles to electronic devices and disposable supplies, reflecting improvements in rural healthcare infrastructure and villagers&\#x27; willingness to seek modern medical care rather than relying on traditional spiritual remedies. Bai emphasizes that consistent presence—delivering babies, treating pneumonia, keeping health records, and occasionally advancing medical payments—was essential to overcoming initial community suspicion and building lasting trust.

rss · 人民日报\(People&\#x27;s Daily\) · Oct 23, 00:26

**「Background」** Guizhou&\#x27;s mountainous terrain has historically limited healthcare access for remote villages, prompting China&\#x27;s expansion of rural cooperative medical insurance and village-level health services over the past two decades. The term &\#x27;basket doctor&\#x27; refers to rural clinicians who carry medical supplies on foot through difficult terrain to reach isolated patients.

**「Impact」** The article illustrates how sustained community-based medical outreach can overcome geographic isolation and cultural barriers to improve rural health outcomes in China&\#x27;s poorest regions.
