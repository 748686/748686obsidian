**Article 1: "Autumn Heroism Surpasses Spring Mornings" by Cai Danjun**
- **Author Affiliation:** Professor, School of Chinese Language and Literature, Renmin University of China.
- **Content:** Analyzes the structural and aesthetic role of the Mid-Autumn Festival in *Dream of the Red Chamber* (*Honglou Meng*) by Cao Xueqin.
- **Key Details:**
    - The novel contains three Lantern Festivals and three Mid-Autumns, forming six major narrative milestones.
    - The first two Mid-Autumns are explicitly written in the first 80 chapters: one at Zhen Shiyin’s home and one at the Jia residence (Chapters 75–76).
    - Describes the Jia family’s grand celebrations, including "Douxiang" (tower-shaped incense), female-only moon worship rituals, and specific food customs (melons cut into lotus petals, nut-filled mooncakes).
    - Highlights a scene where Jia Mu weeps upon hearing flute music from beneath the osmanthus trees, sensing the decline of her family.
    - Contrasts this with Daiyu and Xiangyun composing poetry at the凹晶馆 (Concave Crystal Pavilion), and Miaoyu joining them, representing a spiritual connection among "outsider" women.
    - Excerpt provided from *Dream of the Red Chamber* (Qing Dynasty).

**Article 2: "Nostalgia in Tastes" by Yuan Yonglin**
- **Author Affiliation:** Professor, School of Chinese Language and Literature, Fujian Normal University.
- **Content:** Analyzes the works of Taiwanese writer Qi Jun (琦君), focusing on her essays "Moonlight Cake" (*Yueguang Bing*) and "One Cake for Mid-Autumn" (*Yi Bing Du Zhongqiu*).
- **Key Details:**
    - Qi Jun’s childhood was spent in Wenzhou, Zhejiang.
    - Describes the custom of hanging large "moonlight cakes" (larger than a face basin) with red ribbons under eaves during Mid-Autumn.
    - Analyzes the sensory details of eating the cake ("snatching," "breaking," "smelling," "chewing") and keeping the illustrated paper wrappers.
    - Recounts a childhood conflict with a cousin over the cake, resolved by worshipping the moon.
    - Discusses the theme of cross-strait separation and nostalgia, noting that mooncakes in Taiwan are perceived as "sweeter/heavier" than those in the mainland.
    - Quotes Qi Jun’s husband: "We are eating the moon, not the cake."

**Article 3: "Mid-Autumn on the Long March" by Xiao Nanxi**
- **Author:** Xiao Nanxi (萧南溪), identified as the daughter of Xiao Feng (萧锋).
- **Content:** A personal memoir connecting the author’s present-day visit to Yan’an with her father’s *Long March Diaries*.
- **Key Details:**
    - Focuses on an entry dated September 12, 1935 (Lunar August 15): "Morning departure...行程八十里" (March 80 li).
    - Notes the absence of mentions of the moon, mooncakes, or homesickness in the diary, attributing this to the extreme fatigue and hunger after crossing the grasslands.
    - Describes the logistical difficulty of rationing captured grain for the 3rd Army Group trailing behind.
    - Recalls a memory from the summer of 1988: Her father, then in his seventies, was found crying while editing wartime diaries, expressing survivor’s guilt over fallen comrades.
    - The author visited the Long March route 91 years later (relative to the diary entry) and ate local old-style mooncakes in Yan’an, interpreting the sweetness as a result of the hardships endured.

**Article 4: "Red Chamber Selected Excerpts"**
- **Content:** A direct excerpt from *Dream of the Red Chamber* describing the Jia family’s Mid-Autumn celebration, specifically the flute music emerging from beneath the osmanthus trees and the reaction of the characters.
