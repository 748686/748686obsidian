*   **Activity:** Traditional brewing cycle initiation coinciding with the Double Ninth Festival (Chongyang, lunar September 9).
*   **Cultural Significance:** The festival serves as a node connecting production timing with cultural heritage and intangible cultural property preservation.

### Brewing Process and Tradition ("Shunshi Niangzao")
*   **"Shasha" (Granary Lowering):** Refers to the first feeding of the production cycle using "sha" (crushed red millet-like sorghum, specifically *Hongyingzi* glutinous sorghum).
*   **Cycle Duration:** A full production cycle requires 2 feedings, 9 steamings, 8 fermentations, and 7 liquor extractions, taking at least **5 years** from feeding to finished product.
*   **Seasonal Logic:**
    *   **Dragon Boat Festival (Duanwu):** Rising temperatures and microbial proliferation create optimal conditions for *qu* (starter) making.
    *   **Double Ninth Festival:** Chishui River water volume is abundant and pure, marking the start of the distilling phase.
*   **Intangible Cultural Heritage:** "Moutai Wine Brewing Technique" was listed in the first batch of **National Intangible Cultural Heritage名录** in **2006**.

### Inheritance Mechanisms
*   **Apprenticeship:** The "Master-Apprentice" model is institutionalized. Masters teach practical operational skills; apprentices practice on the front line.
*   **Scope:** Covers winemaking, *qu* making, storage/assorting, and tasting/evaluating.
*   **Modernization:** Management measures define master/apprentice qualifications, training content, and assessment standards, transforming oral transmission into a stable institutional mechanism.
*   **Scientific Integration:** Modern technologies, including **microbiome science** and **flavor chemistry**, are applied to study brewing mechanisms. Digital tools assist in data analysis of production links.

### Cultural Expression ("Yili Zaiwen")
*   **Rituals:** Annual Chongyang Jijiu (Sacrifice to Wine) ceremony honors nature, labor, and ancestors.
*   **Philosophy:** Represents respecting natural conditions, valuing accumulated experience, and passing down crafts.
