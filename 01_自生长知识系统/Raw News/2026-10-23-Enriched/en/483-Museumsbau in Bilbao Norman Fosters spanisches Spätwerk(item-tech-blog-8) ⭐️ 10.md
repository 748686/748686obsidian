### [Social policing struggles with unusual norm violations](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/m8gZSi9pwmciHjqpk/is-social-policing-of-unusual-norm-violations-hard-to-learn) ⭐️ ?/10

Katja Grace argues that many social norms rely on recursive enforcement, meaning the public learns specific enforcement routines from repeated cases. As a result, society is well-prepared for common violations but under-prepared for unusual ones where standard punishments no longer apply. She uses the example of public lying, noting that the usual punishment of exposure becomes unavailable when the violation is already obvious, leaving observers uncertain about how to respond. This hesitation creates social risk, as deviating from established patterns is dangerous, causing the norm to potentially break down due to a lack of common knowledge.

rss · LessWrong AI风险社区 · Oct 22, 21:56

**「Background」** Social norms are often self-reinforcing through second-order enforcement, where individuals are punished by the group if they fail to punish violators. Because these norms are learned inductively through observation, people generally possess detailed knowledge of how to handle frequent scenarios but lack clear precedents for rare or novel breaches.

**「Impact」** Communities may find it significantly harder to maintain cohesion or enforce standards when faced with highly visible but atypical norm violations. Consequently, unusual offenders might escape consequences simply because the crowd lacks a practiced script for handling the situation.
