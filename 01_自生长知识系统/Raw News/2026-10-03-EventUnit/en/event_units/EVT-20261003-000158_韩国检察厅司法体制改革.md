South Korea Judicial Reform Implementation: Abolition of Prosecutors' Office and Establishment of New Agencies

## Event Overview
On October 3, 2026, President Yoon Suk Yeol formally enacted a major judicial restructuring plan aimed at diminishing the investigative powers of the South Korean prosecution service. The reform involves the dissolution of the existing Prosecutors' Office and the creation of two new agencies: the Serious Crimes Agency (SCA) and the General Crimes Agency. This event represents the culmination of legislative efforts to curb what the administration characterized as the unchecked power and alleged corruption within the current prosecution hierarchy.

## Core Facts
*   **Legislative Action:** The National Assembly passed the "Special Act on the Restructuring of the Prosecutors' Office" and the "Serious Crimes Investigation Act."
*   **Structural Change:** The existing Prosecutors' Office is being abolished. Its functions are being split between two new bodies:
    1.  **Serious Crimes Agency (SCA):** A specialized investigative body focusing on high-profile and complex criminal cases.
    2.  **General Crimes Agency:** A new entity handling routine criminal investigations.
*   **Leadership Appointment:** Cho Jae-hyun was designated and sworn in as the first chief of the Serious Crimes Agency.
*   **Scope of Reform:** The changes affect approximately 7,000 prosecutors and involve the transfer or reassignment of personnel. The reform explicitly removes the prosecution's authority to independently direct police investigations and conduct indictments without judicial oversight in many contexts.
*   **Political Context:** The initiative was championed by President Yoon Suk Yeol, who campaigned on anti-corruption and judicial fairness platforms, arguing that the current system allowed for arbitrary and politically motivated prosecutions.
