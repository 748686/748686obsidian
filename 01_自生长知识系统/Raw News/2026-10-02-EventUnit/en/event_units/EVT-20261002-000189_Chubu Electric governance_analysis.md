### 🔴 Weaknesses (W) – Internal Factors
1. **Legacy Governance Structures:** The need for an "overhaul" implies existing governance flaws, such as opaque decision-making, lack of board independence, or historical mismanagement.
2. **Bureaucratic Inertia:** Large utilities often struggle with slow adaptation to market changes and digital transformation due to entrenched organizational culture.

2. **Regulatory Reform Tailwinds:** Government policies promoting corporate governance codes (e.g., Japan’s Stewardship Code and Corporate Governance Code) can provide a framework and incentive for legitimate reform.

4. **Green Finance Access:** Improved governance can unlock access to ESG-focused investment funds and green bonds, potentially lowering the cost of capital.

### ⚠️ Threats (T) – External Factors
1. **Intense Competition:** Entry of new players (new energy providers, tech companies) into the deregulated energy market threatens market share.
2. **Regulatory Scrutiny & Penalties:** If the governance overhaul is perceived as insufficient or superficial, regulators may impose stricter oversight or penalties.
3. **Energy Price Volatility:** Global fluctuations in fuel prices (LNG, coal) can impact profitability, especially if the transition to renewables is slow.
4. **Stakeholder Activism:** Investors, customers, and NGOs may demand more aggressive climate action and transparency, risking backlash if expectations are not met.
