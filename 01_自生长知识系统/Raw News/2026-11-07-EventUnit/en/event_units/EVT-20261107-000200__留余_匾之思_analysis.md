## Event Analysis: "Leave Some Leftover" Plaque & The Long March Narrative

### 1. Structural Framework Analysis (Based on "Three-Act Structure")

Although the primary event metadata references a cultural essay on the "Leave Some Leftover" (留余) plaque, the available source text (Article #344, "Fragrant Osmanthus") provides a complete narrative arc of the Red Army's passage through Kedu, Yunnan. This narrative fits the **Three-Act Structure** precisely, offering a historical reflection piece with a clear beginning, middle, and end.

#### Act I: Setup (The Call to History)
*   **Establishing the World:** The story opens in **Kedu (Ke Du), Yunnan Province**, setting a historical context in **Spring 1935**. It establishes the atmosphere through a natural omen: "swarms of butterflies appeared in the Kedu basin," creating a sense of anticipation and sacredness before the arrival of the Red Army.
*   **Introducing the Protagonist:** The central figure is **Ma Yingda**, a 102-year-old Hui Muslim man, positioned as the living witness connecting the past to the present.
*   **The Normality Before Change:** The villagers initially live in fear. The "normal state" is one of caution and uncertainty regarding the approaching army.

#### Act II: Confrontation (The Trial and Transformation)
*   **Conflict and Challenge:** The arrival of the Red Army on **April 30, 1935** triggers the initial conflict: fear among villagers versus the strict discipline of the soldiers. The soldiers do not take items and pay for food, dissolving the villagers' fears.
*   **Allies and Enemies:**
    *   **Allies:** **Bi Fadou**, a 23-year-old villager, becomes a key ally figure by recruiting 12 local youths to form the **Hui Squadron**. This represents the integration of the local community with the revolutionary cause.
    *   **The Sacrifice:** The 12 youths all die in battle; Bi Fadou survives with severe injuries. This is the "trials" phase where the cost of the revolution is revealed.
*   **Midpoint Event:** The holding of meetings by **Mao Zedong, Zhou Enlai, and Zhu De** in the **He Family Courtyard** to plan the crossing of the **Jinsha River**. This marks a shift from local interaction to strategic historical significance, elevating the stakes from village survival to national revolutionary fate.
*   **The Crisis:** The near-total loss of the Hui Squadron (11 out of 12 dead) represents the ultimate crisis and sacrifice, deepening the emotional weight of the narrative.

#### Act III: Resolution (The Return and Legacy)
*   **The Climax:** The preservation of the **18 revolutionary slogans** in Kedu, specifically the one protecting the interests of workers and peasants, stands as the spiritual climax. It symbolizes the enduring bond between the army and the people.
*   **Resolution:** The narrative resolves in the present day (circa 2020/2026). The legacy of the past is shown through:
    *   **Ma Yingda’s family:** His son, Ma Yongshun, reports a stable income from agriculture and government subsidies, showing the fruits of peace.
    *   **Dangu Village:** Receives over 150,000 tourists annually, leveraging "red tourism" for economic revitalization.
*   **New Normal:** The Kedu Red Army Long March Memorial Hall, with its dark brown pillars and giant red five-star sculpture surrounded by osmanthus trees, represents the institutionalized memory and the new cultural identity of the region.

### 2. Character Arc Analysis (Based on "Hero's Journey")

The narrative can be viewed through the lens of the **Hero's Journey**, focusing on the collective heroism of the Red Army and the individual witness, Ma Yingda.

#### Phase 1: Departure

1.  **The Ordinary World:** Ma Yingda’s life in Kedu before 1935, amidst the natural omen of butterflies.
2.  **The Call to Adventure:** The approach of the Red Army in Spring 1935. This is the disruptor of the ordinary world.
3.  **Refusal of the Call:** The initial fear and hesitation of the villagers, including Ma Yingda’s grandmother, who worries about the strangers entering their village.
4.  **Meeting the Mentor:** The Red Army soldiers themselves act as mentors through their example. They refuse cigarettes but offer rice to the poor grandmother, teaching the villagers through disciplined action rather than words.
5.  **Crossing the First Threshold:** **Bi Fadou** decides to recruit 12 youths. This is the point of no return, committing the village to the revolutionary path.

#### Phase 2: Initiation

6.  **Tests, Allies, and Enemies:**
    *   **Test:** The villagers test the soldiers’ discipline (observing they pay for food).
    *   **Allies:** The formation of the Hui Squadron unites the local Hui community with the Red Army.
    *   **Enemies:** The broader context of the Long March implies the pursuit by Kuomintang forces and the harsh natural environment.
7.  **Approach to the Innermost Cave:** The strategic meetings led by Mao, Zhou, and Zhu De in the He Family Courtyard. This is the preparation for the ultimate challenge—the crossing of the Jinsha River.
8.  **The Ordeal:** The Battle involving the Hui Squadron. It is a "death and rebirth" moment for the recruits; 11 die, and Bi Fadou is severely wounded. This is the crucible where the true cost of the revolution is paid.

#### Phase 3: Return

9.  **The Reward (The Elixir):** The **revolutionary slogans** left on the walls become the tangible "elixir." Specifically, the slogan protecting workers and peasants is preserved by locals for over 90 years. This is the spiritual treasure gained from the ordeal.
10. **The Road Back:** The transition from the war-torn 1935 to the peaceful present. The narrative follows the lineage from Ma Yingda to his son, Ma Yongshun.
11. **The Resurrection:** The revitalization of Kedu through **red tourism**. The village is "resurrected" from a remote mountain community into a significant historical and economic site, hosting 150,000+ tourists.
12. **Return with the Elixir:** The story concludes with the idea that the "Fragrant Osmanthus" (symbolizing the enduring memory and virtue) now nourishes the community. The values of discipline, ethnic unity, and people-centric governance (the "elixir") are applied to modern rural revitalization, changing the local world for the better.

### 3. Synthesis and Conclusion

The Event Unit **EVT-20261107-000200** presents a complex case of metadata-content divergence. While the editorial header refers to the **"Leave Some Leftover" (留余)** philosophy at Kang Baiwan Manor in Henan—a concept of moderation and preservation—the source text delivers a powerful narrative of **revolutionary sacrifice and continuity** in Yunnan.

From a structural perspective, both themes are linked by the concept of **"preservation"**:
1.  **Philosophical Preservation:** The "Leave Some Leftover" plaque advocates for leaving room for the future, avoiding excess.
2.  **Historical Preservation:** The Kedu narrative shows how the memory of the Red Army (the "osmanthus fragrance") is preserved by descendants like Ma Yingda and the community, ensuring the "elixir" of revolutionary spirit continues to benefit the present generation.

The **Hero's Journey** of the Red Army and the **Three-Act Structure** of the historical account provide a robust framework for understanding how past sacrifices transform into present prosperity. The "reward" of the journey is not just victory in war, but the sustainable development and cultural dignity of the region today.
